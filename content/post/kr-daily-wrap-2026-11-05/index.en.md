## Market Wrap

Korea's November 5 session was a broad, technically-driven sell-off with two distinct pockets of institutional support cutting against the tide.

The aggregate picture leaves little room for interpretation: 1,714 stocks fell, 635 rose, and the average stock declined 1.96%. Program selling totaled ₩1.69 trillion net while ETF redemptions reached ₩1.52 trillion. Futures basis sat at −0.53. This is systematic passive de-risking — not a single-sector event.

**Where money entered.** Internet and platform names were the session's standout. NAVER (035420.KS, +8.26%) attracted simultaneous foreign and institutional net buying — a rare alignment on a down day. Kakao (035720.KS, +10.65%) drew the heaviest foreign inflows of any name. Hybe (352820.KS, +8.19%) also saw foreign accumulation. The likely driver: falling oil prices and stabilizing US yields created a rotation window into domestic internet names that had lagged through October.

Financials were the second destination for institutional flows. KB Financial (105560.KS, +3.63%), Woori Financial (316140.KS, +5.27%), Hyundai Mobis (012330.KS, +2.54%), Hyundai Motor (005380.KS, +3.53%), and Hana Financial (086790.KS, +3.16%) all recorded institutional net buying. Samsung Biologics (207940.KS, +1.67%) was bought by institutions even as foreign investors trimmed. Koryo Zinc (010130.KS, +11.0%) surged with institutional support; foreign investors were on the other side.

**Where money exited.** Semiconductors and the battery supply chain bore the heaviest institutional selling. PSK Holdings (031980.KS, −19.4%), Lotte Energy Materials (020150.KS, −18.4%), Doosan (000150.KS, −17.5%), TES (095610.KS, −17.1%), and Doosan Tesna (131970.KS, −16.1%) all saw combined foreign and institutional selling. Samsung SDI (006400.KS, −6.6%) and S-Oil (010950.KS, −6.2%) declined as institutions distributed. The common thread across the losers: capex-intensive businesses where slowing order visibility or margin pressure makes the near-term earnings trajectory harder to defend.

The split is clean. Quality internet and financial franchises absorbed institutional flows; semis, batteries, and energy supply chain were the exits. With KOSPI and KOSDAQ both trading more than 20% below their 200-day moving averages, the structural backdrop remains broken. But within that environment, some businesses are being selectively re-priced higher.
