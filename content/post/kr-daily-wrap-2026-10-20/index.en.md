## Market Wrap

Monday's session was a broad selloff. Of 2,593 listed names, only 391 closed higher while 1,963 declined. The advance-decline ratio proxy came in at 19.9 — deep in risk-off territory — and the average stock fell 1.57%.

The session had no obvious sector anchor. Declines were wide and simultaneous rather than confined to a single theme. Energy names absorbed some of the heaviest individual hits: **S-Oil (010950.KS)** fell 7.8%, **GS (078930.KS)** dropped 5.1%, and **LS에코에너지 (229640.KS)** shed 14.6%. Battery materials continued their recent reversal — **에코프로 (086520.KQ)** −7.7%, **에코프로비엠 (247540.KQ)** −9.2%. Solar-linked **HD현대에너지솔루션 (322000.KS)** lost 11.7%.

What made the session notable was where foreign and institutional money *did not* fully retreat. Foreign investors net-bought across a surprisingly defensive list: S-Oil and GS (buying into the energy dip), **현대건설 (000720.KS)**, **신한지주 (055550.KS)**, and **삼성에스디에스 (018260.KS)**. This reads as a rotation toward dividend-yielding, domestically-exposed names rather than fresh growth positioning.

Institutions took the other side of the foreign energy trade — selling S-Oil and GS — while absorbing **KB금융 (105560.KS)**, **현대모비스 (012330.KS)**, **우리금융지주 (316140.KS)**, and **삼성바이오로직스 (207940.KS)**. The financials pocket showed modest relative resilience, with KB금융 off only 1.1% and 우리금융지주 actually closing up 0.47% on institutional support.

The derivatives and passive-flow picture adds nuance: ETF net creation came in at +884.1 billion KRW, suggesting passive flows were not aggressively pulling out. Program selling was −228.5 billion KRW on the non-arbitrage side. Futures basis held positive at +2.27. In a Bear regime session this severe, the absence of heavy passive redemptions is a mild stabilizer — but it does not change the directional picture.

The **macro-regime system remains in confirmed Bear for Korea** with the driver flagged as unknown price decline — meaning no single rate, FX, or flow factor fully explains the drop. That ambiguity itself is a risk signal.

### Deep Dive: Top 3 Candidates

**#1 — Celltrion (068270.KS) | Meta Score 61.2 | 6 Independent Families**

Celltrion is Korea's leading biosimilar developer and manufacturer, with a growing global commercial footprint across immunology and oncology biosimilars. It tops today's screen by the widest margin because three distinct evidence streams converge simultaneously.

The *earnings cycle* story is the anchor: operating margin expanded by +14.2 percentage points, and Smart Money Earnings confirms the improvement is fresh and investable. The *flow* story reinforces this — retail investors have been net sellers to the tune of 245.4 billion KRW, and foreign plus qualified-institutional buyers have absorbed that supply (+200.2 billion KRW over five days). This retail/smart-money divergence is the pattern the screener is designed to catch earliest. DART filings add two concrete catalysts: a preliminary earnings disclosure from October 12 and a clinical trial approval for CTP13 SC (Zymfentra) in Korea on October 14. A share buyback completion report was filed today.

*What to check next:* The published earnings figures, the Korea CTP13 SC addressable market, and whether the 5.9% short interest could create friction on the upside.

**#2 — HMM (011200.KS) | Meta Score 46.3 | 6 Independent Families**

HMM is Korea's largest container shipping company, now majority state-owned after the privatization process stalled. It appears here despite the broad market selloff because four independent signals align: Smart Money Quality (foreigners and qualified institutions net-bought +100.5 billion KRW over five days against 219.3 billion KRW of retail selling), a consensus earnings upgrade (composite z-score +0.46), RS80 Not-Late Leadership (passing as a Focus Candidate with pivot gap +3.9% and low box risk), and Kiwoom market surface confirmation. The container freight rate cycle is notoriously volatile, but the screener is measuring whether *this specific stock* is being re-priced by informed capital relative to retail — and the answer here is yes.

DART filings from October 15 include a new capex investment disclosure and shareholder meeting announcement, consistent with the company being in an active strategic period.

*What to check next:* Current spot freight rate trajectory vs. HMM contract mix, and the status of the privatization process.
