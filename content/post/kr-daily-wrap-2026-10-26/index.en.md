## Market Wrap

*Source: KR Market Snapshot DB (2026-10-26). No same-day full close briefing was generated; this section draws from the close-of-day market database.*

October 26 was a broad selloff with little place to hide. Of 2,593 tracked Korean equities, 1,928 declined and only 447 advanced — an ADR proxy of 23.2% and an average stock return of −1.75%. That is bear-market breadth, not selective sector rotation.

The headline KOSPI/KOSDAQ divergence carries the week's clearest signal. KOSPI is down 1.5% over five sessions; KOSDAQ is down 7.0%. The gap points to sustained selling in small- and mid-cap growth: semiconductor equipment, KOSDAQ tech hardware, and select consumer names absorbed the heaviest damage. Large-cap defensives and IT services held comparatively better.

Passive outflows amplified the pressure. ETF net redemptions totaled −₩1.18 trillion, with market program net selling at −₩527bn. The futures basis sat at −1.51 — deeply negative — flagging limited demand for leveraged index exposure.

Foreign flow was fragmented. Foreigners net-bought into weakness on 두산 (000150.KS, −6.5%), S-Oil (010950.KS, −3.6%), NAVER (035420.KS, +0.4%), and 삼성E&A (028050.KS). They were net sellers across financials — BNK금융지주 (138930.KS, −13.8%), KB금융 (105560.KS, −3.8%), 우리금융지주 (316140.KS, −2.8%) — and sold out of 삼성에스디에스 (018260.KS) and LG씨엔에스 (064400.KS) in IT services.

Institutions moved in the opposite direction on those IT services names, net-buying 삼성에스디에스, LG씨엔에스, 삼성생명 (032830.KS), and 삼성물산 (028260.KS) while foreigners sold. Domestic institutions absorbing foreign supply in large-cap IT services was one of the few conviction flows on the session. Elsewhere, institutions sold cyclicals and energy: 한화솔루션 (009830.KS, −6.9%), 피에스케이 (319660.KS, −7.2%), 삼양식품 (003230.KS, −8.9%), and HPSP (403870.KQ, −5.9%).

The overall character: risk-off, broad-based, and KOSDAQ-led. Semiconductor equipment, regional banks, energy, and consumer discretionary absorbed the brunt. The session was not selective — it was a tide going out.
