The three largest ad sellers reported third-quarter results in late October. Meta's ad revenue **grew 26% to $63.1 billion**. In the same quarter Google's ad revenue grew 10.6% to $82.0 billion, and Amazon's grew 10.9% to $19.6 billion. Together the three sold $164.7 billion of ads, 16.0% more than a year earlier, and **Meta took 57% of that increase.** [Meta release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026066700/meta-09302026xexhibit991.htm), [Alphabet release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001652044/000165204426000080/googexhibit991q32026.htm), [Amazon release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001018724/000101872426000038/amzn-20260930xex991.htm)

The market read Google's miss as an AI search story and explained Amazon's miss with the Prime Day calendar. Each explanation holds inside its own company. Put the three companies in one table, and a larger pattern appears.

**Our judgment: Meta is taking Google's performance ad budget, and the shift has run for six quarters. It is structural. The cause lies more in the strength of Meta's ad machine than in Google's weakness. The evidence is that advertisers now pay 14% more per Meta ad than a year ago. The contest with Amazon is still open, and Q4 results are the first real test.**

This article is dated October 30, 2026. Figures come from each company's SEC earnings releases and quarterly reports (10-Q). Share means share of the three companies' combined ad revenue. It is not share of the whole digital ad market.

## The budget shift in numbers

- **Six straight quarters:** since Q2 2025, Meta's ad revenue has grown 11 to 17 percentage points faster than Google's every quarter. A one-quarter calendar quirk or currency swing cannot explain a run this long.
- **Share:** Meta's share of the three companies' ad revenue was 33.2% in 2024, 35.1% in 2025 and 37.6% in 2026. The annual gain widened from 1.9 to 2.5 points.
- **Acceleration:** on a two-year compound basis, which removes last year's base, Meta sped up from 24.4% in Q2 to 25.7% in Q3. Google slowed from 12.4% to 11.6%.
- **Amazon:** on the same basis Amazon's share was 12.1% in both 2025 and 2026. Meta has not yet taken share from Amazon.

## Why Meta is stronger: advertisers pay for results

In Q3, Meta's ad impressions grew 10% and its average price per ad rose 14%. For prices to rise while supply also grows, advertiser demand must grow faster than supply. CFO Susan Li attributed the price increase to ad performance gains and to better macro conditions than a year earlier. In the same quarter, Google Search's cost per click rose 4%. [Meta call transcript](https://s21.q4cdn.com/399680738/files/content_files/META-Q3-2026-Earnings-Call-Transcript.pdf), [Alphabet 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001652044/000165204426000084/goog-20260930.htm)

![Year-over-year change in Meta's ad impressions and average price per ad. From Q3 2025 to Q3 2026, impressions grew 14, 18, 19, 14 and 10%, and price per ad rose 10, 6, 12, 12 and 14%. In Q3 2026 price growth passed impression growth.](/post-assets/meta-ad-edge-google-amazon-share-shift-2026-10-30/meta-price-vs-impressions.en.png)

Meta can raise prices because what advertisers buy from it is closer to a conversion than to an impression. On the Q3 call, Meta put numbers on the effect of its ad ranking models:

- **GEM expansion:** conversions rose 1.7% for off-site conversion campaigns and 9.6% for app ads.
- **Andromeda improvements:** conversions on Facebook rose 1.4%.
- **Partnership ads:** grew more than 97% year over year and passed a $15 billion annual revenue run rate.

Advertisers split budgets by measured conversion results. Meta's 10-Q names online commerce as the largest contributor to its ad revenue growth. Google said retail and finance led its Search revenue growth. The same advertisers compare the two companies on the same performance metrics, and we judge that the result shows up as the growth gap. None of the three companies said budgets are moving, so this link is our inference. [Meta 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001326801/000162828026066773/meta-20260930.htm)

**Meta accelerated in its largest market.** By advertiser location, Meta's US and Canada revenue grew 33.0% in Q3, up from 29.3% in Q2. The region is 41% of Meta's total revenue. Meta said strong impression growth on lower-monetizing surfaces and regions partly offset the price gain. The regional mix pushed the average price down, and the average price still rose 14%. That supports the reading that the same advertisers are paying more.

## Google's searches grew faster than its paid clicks

CEO Sundar Pichai said Q3 search query growth was the highest in the last five years. Yet Search paid-click growth fell from 13% in Q2 to 8% in Q3. Search revenue grew 12.0%. [Alphabet call transcript (Motley Fool)](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/10/29/alphabet-googl-q3-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)

Chief Business Officer Philipp Schindler gave several causes for the slowdown. Currency was the single largest. The EU began charging duties on small parcels on July 1, and some Asia-Pacific retail advertisers pulled back. Media spending that had bunched around the June World Cup normalized in Q3. He also said AI Mode queries grew strongly while AI Mode monetized at a lower rate.

Currency, the World Cup and the Asia-Pacific advertisers are temporary. Currency changes revenue but not the number of clicks. What remains is that each search query now produces fewer ad clicks. The effect is still small. But it comes from a structure in which Google must receive a query before it can sell an ad. The more users get answers straight from AI, the fewer ads there are to click.

Meta's feed does not have this problem. Meta creates its own ad inventory, and it uses AI to rank the ads in that inventory. The same technology is cutting clicks at Google and lifting conversions at Meta. Still, we treat Google's AI Mode gap as a secondary cause. The growth gap dates back to Q2 2025, long before this quarter, when Google first cited AI Mode's lower monetization.

## Meta has not beaten Amazon yet

Amazon's ad growth fell from 26% in Q2 to 12% in Q3, both excluding currency. CEO Andy Jassy said moving Prime Day into Q2 in most countries had an 850-basis-point effect on ad growth. A change to Amazon's search marketing program in Q3 2025 also moved into the base this quarter. [Amazon call transcript (Investing.com)](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-amazon-q3-2026-misses-eps-as-shares-fall-after-hours-93CH-4985489)

Combining Q2 and Q3 removes the calendar effect and gives 18.6% growth excluding currency. Meta grew faster than Amazon, but it has not yet taken Amazon's share. Amazon measures whether people who saw an ad actually bought, using its own store data. Nothing in these results shows that this advantage has weakened.

**Q4 is the first test.** Amazon guided Q4 ad growth to the high teens. Meta's Q4 revenue guidance of $73-76 billion implies 21.9-26.9% growth. If both land on guidance, a 5-7 point gap appears for the first time in a quarter with no calendar distortion. If the gap is 2 points or less, we will conclude that Meta has no edge over Amazon.

## Where Meta does not win

Meta's edge is limited to the performance budgets of online commerce and app advertisers. Meta is not taking Google's search queries.

- **Search intent:** Google is still strongest for ads shown to people who search in order to buy.
- **TV:** in Q3, living-room viewing accounted for more than a third of YouTube ad revenue. Meta has no such surface.
- **Purchase data:** Amazon is strongest for ads measured all the way to a purchase in its own store.

## What could break this judgment

**Meta's growth is now price-led.** Impression growth fell from 19% in Q1 to 14% in Q2 and 10% in Q3. Daily active people grew 3% to 3.64 billion. Prices react first to advertiser returns and the economy, and Meta included better macro conditions among the reasons for its price gain. If the economy turns, price is where growth falls first.

**Asia-Pacific advertisers are wobbling.** By advertiser location, Asia-Pacific is 26.3% of Meta's revenue. Its growth slowed from 25.0% in Q2 to 18.8% in Q3. We estimate these are the same advertisers Google linked to the EU small-parcel duty. Meta did not discuss this on its call.

**Ad cash is flowing into AI capex.** Meta's Q3 capital expenditure was $34.9 billion, and its free cash flow was negative $2.1 billion. Its 2026 capex guidance is $135-145 billion. In late October Meta raised outside capital with $25 billion of common stock (through forward sales) and mandatory convertible preferred stock. The strength of the ad machine and the cash left for shareholders are separate questions. [Meta prospectus (424B5)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000119312526436410/d94886d424b5.htm)

## The numbers that would change our view

| Q4 metric | Judgment holds | Judgment changes |
|---|---|---|
| Meta minus Google ad growth gap | 10 points or more | Below 8 points: we lower the edge over Google |
| Google paid-click growth | Below query growth | Catches up with query growth: AI Mode gap closed |
| Meta price-per-ad growth | At or above impression growth | Falls below impression growth: performance edge weakening |
| Meta minus Amazon ad growth gap | 5-7 points | 2 points or less: no edge over Amazon |
| Meta revenue growth | 21.9-26.9% (guidance) | Below 21.9%: read as weaker total demand, not share shift |
| Meta Asia-Pacific advertiser revenue growth | Recovers | Stays below 18%: the EU duty shock is lasting |

The three companies report Q4 in late January or early February 2027. The dates are not yet set.

## Limits

- **Revenue basis:** Google's ad revenue includes traffic acquisition costs paid to partners ($16.4 billion in Q3). These costs grew 10.0%, close to ad revenue, so they change the growth gap little.
- **Scope:** we did not compare TikTok, Microsoft, AppLovin, Reddit, Walmart or ChatGPT ads.
- **Inference:** the budget shift toward Meta is our inference from public results. We did not see advertisers' actual spending split by platform.
- **Call remarks:** Meta remarks come from the company's official transcript; Google and Amazon remarks come from third-party transcripts. Jassy's 850 basis points is ambiguously worded, so we estimated Amazon's underlying growth from combined Q2 and Q3.
