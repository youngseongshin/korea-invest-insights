On October 28, Alphabet and Microsoft reported their September quarters and Oracle held its investor day. The two hyperscalers spent **about $9 billion less on capex than analysts expected**, and both blamed supply. Alphabet fell **about 10% after hours**, Microsoft rose **about 3%**, and Oracle had already closed **10.7% higher**. [CNBC on Alphabet](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/28/alphabet-googl-earnings-q3-2026.html), [CNBC on Microsoft](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/28/microsoft-msft-q1-earnings-report-2027.html)

The next morning in Seoul, the KOSPI rose to about 6,100, up 1.5%, with all of that US news already known. Then, from 9:50 a.m., Samsung Electronics filed its results, its dividend and its capex plan, and the index reversed. By 11:15 a.m. it was down 3.0%.

**Our judgment: the AI capex cycle has not slowed, but its funding has changed. Spending now runs ahead of operating cash at most builders, so markets reward the companies that can pay and punish the ones that need outside capital.** The same test reached Seoul. Samsung's own capex jump limits how much of its record profit can go to shareholders, and that, at least as much as US capex, is what the market traded as the KOSPI reversed after 9:50.

This article is dated October 29, 2026. Korean market figures are intraday values at 11:15 a.m. KST and are provisional until the close.

## Capex held up; the funding changed

- **The miss came from supply.** Alphabet said it is in a "supply-constrained environment" and started renting outside compute. Microsoft said some third-party capacity deliveries were delayed. Alphabet expects 2027 capex to rise significantly, and Microsoft expects spending to accelerate in January–June 2027.
- **Demand indicators are still rising.** Google Cloud revenue grew 103% and its backlog reached $561 billion. Azure grew 48%.
- **Part of the spending is price.** Microsoft named higher memory and storage costs in its second-half capex. Samsung said its DRAM prices rose about 20% in the third quarter.
- **Investors priced cash.** Alphabet had negative free cash flow, bought back no stock and issued equity. Microsoft had $18.6 billion of free cash flow. Their shares moved in opposite directions.
- **Oracle put the first date on the peak.** It expects peak capex in fiscal 2027–2028, through May 2028, and a step-down in fiscal 2029.
- **Seoul fell on a payout question.** Samsung's shareholder returns are set at 50% of free cash flow, so its 84.4 trillion won capex plan caps the payout that its profit would otherwise support.

Microsoft's figure includes finance leases; Alphabet's is cash paid for property and equipment.

The demand side points the other way. **Google Cloud revenue rose 103% to $30.7 billion.** Its backlog was $561 billion, and Alphabet expects to recognize just over 55% of it within 24 months. Azure grew 48%, and Microsoft's commercial remaining performance obligation rose 73% to $677 billion. We covered Microsoft's quarter in detail [earlier today](/post/microsoft-q1-fy27-azure-capex-cpu-memory-korea-readthrough-2026-10-29/).

Microsoft did not name the late partners. CNBC read the lower capex as a sign that infrastructure partners are taking longer to deliver, and noted that Microsoft relies on neoclouds for capacity. CoreWeave and Nebius fell almost 5% in extended trading, and IREN fell about 3%. [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/28/neocloud-stocks-drop-after-microsoft-revises-capex-down.html) These companies build with debt, and CNBC also cited concern that rising interest rates will make that debt too costly. If the delay sits in that layer, a delivery problem is a funding problem in a different form.

## Part of the spending is price

Microsoft said capex in the second half of its fiscal year, January to June 2027, will accelerate mainly because of CPUs, "inclusive of higher memory and storage costs", and GPUs, as well as third-party deliveries that shifted into that period. In July, according to Data Center Knowledge, Amazon raised its 2026 plan to about $220 billion and said the increase mainly reflected higher memory costs. [Data Center Knowledge](https://datacenterknowledge.com/infrastructure/amazon-lifts-ai-infrastructure-spending-to-220b-as-demand-outpaces-capacity) On its call this morning, Samsung said its DRAM prices rose about 20% and its NAND prices rose in the high teens in the third quarter, led by server products. [Chosun Biz via Naver](https://n.news.naver.com/mnews/article/366/0001201158)

This matters for how to read capex totals. **Capex dollars now grow faster than the compute they buy, and the difference shows up as memory makers' margins.** SK hynix earned an 80% operating margin last quarter. [KII, Oct 29](/post/sk-hynix-80-percent-margin-contract-price-band-2026-10-29/) The buyers see the same number as a cost that their own cash flow has to carry.

## The market priced cash

![Alphabet's cash flows for January to September. Capex rose from $63.6 billion in 2025 to $132.0 billion in 2026, while operating cash flow rose from $112.3 billion to $127.1 billion. Share repurchases fell from $39.8 billion to zero. Net debt issued rose from $11.9 billion to $76.3 billion, and equity issued rose from zero to $61.9 billion, including common and mandatory convertible preferred stock.](/post-assets/big-tech-capex-delivery-delay-who-pays-alphabet-samsung-2026-10-29/alphabet-funding.en.png)

Alphabet's numbers explain its fall better than its capex. In the quarter, **free cash flow was negative $9.1 billion**; CNBC cited an estimate of negative $2.7 billion. Alphabet bought back **no stock**, against $11.5 billion a year earlier. It issued $12.4 billion of common stock through its at-the-market program. From January to September it issued $42.9 billion of common stock and $19.1 billion of mandatory convertible preferred stock, and its net new debt was $76.3 billion. Long-term debt rose to **$125.1 billion from $46.5 billion** at the end of 2025. Alphabet still holds $265.1 billion of cash and marketable securities, so this is a funding choice made from strength. But one of the most profitable builders now funds part of its spending with outside capital. [Alphabet earnings release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001652044/000165204426000080/googexhibit991q32026.htm)

Microsoft is the contrast. Its free cash flow was **$18.6 billion**, down 27% but positive, and its shares rose. Oracle rose 10.7% after it said it would limit additional capital raises through fiscal 2030 to $20 billion, split between equity and debt and excluding refinancing. That is on top of a $20 billion at-the-market equity sale completed in the June–August quarter. [KII on Oracle](/post/oracle-fcf-turn-customers-landlords-pay-first-openai-test-2026-10-29/) The three reactions line up with cash and funding.

The suppliers show the same split from the other side. They rose in the regular session, before the two reports, and gave back only 2–3% after hours while Alphabet fell 10%:

The cost of outside money is rising. The 10-year Treasury yield closed at **5.05%** on October 28. [US Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?type=daily_treasury_yield_curve&field_tdr_date_value_month=202610) The Federal Reserve held its target range at 3.75–4.00% the same day, and two members, Lisa Cook and Beth Hammack, voted for a quarter-point increase. [Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20261028a.htm) When builders need debt and equity to fund capex, a 5% risk-free rate makes the cash test sharper.

## Oracle dated the peak

Oracle said: "Following a period of peak capex in FY27-FY28, capital expenditures are expected to step-down in FY29." Its fiscal 2027 began in June 2026, and fiscal 2028 ends in May 2028. **Oracle gave an explicit window for its capex peak. None of the hyperscalers that reported this month has given one.** It also said vendor financing, customer-provided hardware and customer prepayments will reduce its upfront cash needs. [Oracle investor day release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1341439/000119312526435853/d75538dex991.htm)

The date does not signal weaker 2027 demand. It tells suppliers when one large buyer expects its build to stop rising. The funding sentence moves part of the cash burden to suppliers and customers. For memory makers, payment terms and prepayments will matter as much as price.

## Samsung's payout is bound by its own capex

Samsung's call was positive on demand. It said memory demand next year will "greatly exceed supply" and that it will start HBM4E mass production in the first half of 2027. [Money Today via Naver](https://n.news.naver.com/mnews/article/008/0005433470) Its third-quarter operating profit was **107.4 trillion won** on revenue of 195.3 trillion won. The October 8 preliminary release had already shown 107.4 trillion won, so the profit was not news. [DART](https://dart.fss.or.kr/dsaf001/main.do?rcpNo=20261029800087) It also said lower dollar and euro exchange rates cut operating profit by about 10 trillion won from the previous quarter, mostly in components.

The capex plan is the link to the payout. **Samsung expects 84.4 trillion won of facility investment in 2026, 31.7 trillion won more than in 2025, with 76.8 trillion won in its chip division.** [DART](https://dart.fss.or.kr/dsaf001/main.do?rcpNo=20261029800102), [Asia Economy via Naver](https://n.news.naver.com/mnews/article/277/0005835135) Samsung's policy returns 50% of its 2024–2026 free cash flow. That free cash flow excludes prepayments under long-term memory supply agreements, which Samsung treats as deposits. In August, Samsung put the remaining pool for 2026 at about 90–110 trillion won. [DART, Aug 21](https://dart.fss.or.kr/dsaf001/main.do?rcpNo=20260821800763)

Today Samsung declared the 30 trillion won dividend that it had planned in August, at 4,601 won per share. On the call, it said about 35 trillion won of returns had been executed or confirmed, which leaves **55–75 trillion won**. Samsung said a large part will go to buying back and cancelling shares. The final size will be set at the board meeting in late January 2027. [Financial News via Naver](https://n.news.naver.com/mnews/article/014/0005596321)

The arithmetic is unchanged from August, and the call did not raise the range after a record quarter. An industry source told Herald Economy that the late-January timing of the final decision appeared to disappoint the market. [Herald Economy via Naver](https://n.news.naver.com/mnews/article/016/0002715932) A higher capex plan lowers the free cash flow base. Customer prepayments, which pay for new capacity, are excluded from it. SK hynix had announced about 100 trillion won of returns for this year the day before, including at least 30 trillion won of cash dividends and at least 60 trillion won of buybacks and cancellation. [Newspim](https://www.newspim.com/news/view/20261028000155) Investors compared the two this morning.

**For Korean memory, the capex boom is good for earnings and a constraint on payouts.** The question for Samsung's stock has moved from whether AI demand lasts to how much of the cash reaches shareholders.

## Our judgment, and what would change it

**Cash now ranks ahead of capex as the variable that sets AI-builder valuations.** The quarter's shortfall came from late deliveries. Demand indicators kept rising, and part of the spending growth is higher memory prices. The pressure now falls on funding. Builders with negative free cash flow, such as Alphabet, are issuing new equity and debt while the 10-year yield is at 5%. In Seoul, the same pressure appeared as a payout cap tied to Samsung's own capex.

| Evidence | What it would mean |
|---|---|
| Amazon (after the US close on Oct 29) raises capex while free cash flow stays near zero | Spending continues; the funding test gets sharper |
| Alphabet's fourth-quarter call gives 2027 capex growth well below 2026's | Builders are rationing; the first real slowdown signal |
| Microsoft's December-quarter capex comes in below $50bn | The delay is more than timing |
| Oracle exceeds its $20bn funding cap or its credit spreads widen sharply | Credit becomes the binding constraint |
| Google Cloud backlog or Microsoft's commercial RPO falls | A supply delay is turning into a demand delay |
| Samsung's January board raises total 2026 returns above 110 trillion won | The payout constraint loosens |
| Foreign investors turn net sellers of Korean memory stocks | The Korean move becomes an AI-demand question |

The next checks are Amazon's results tonight, the KOSPI close and foreign flows, and Samsung's January board decision.
