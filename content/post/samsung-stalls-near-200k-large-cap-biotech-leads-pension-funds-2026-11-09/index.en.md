Samsung Electronics closed at ₩188,700 on November 6, <strong>47.9% below</strong> its June 18 peak of ₩362,500. The KOSPI closed at 5,060, <strong>44.5% below</strong> its June 22 closing peak. Only 14.5% of stocks traded above their 50-day average, and our market-regime report reads Bear.

A rebound to ₩200,000–215,000 would be a gain of 6% to 14%. What matters for the rest of the market comes after that. If Samsung stalls there instead of rallying, which group of stocks takes the lead?

We tested four candidates with equal-weighted baskets built from KRX data: large-cap biotech and pharma, data centers (Naver, SK Telecom and other telecom and platform names), shipbuilding, defense and nuclear, and batteries. For each basket we measured how it reacts when Samsung makes a big move and where domestic and foreign money went over the last 20 trading days.

<strong>Our view: if Samsung stalls near ₩200,000, large-cap biotech and pharma is the likeliest leader. Data centers come second, shipbuilding, defense and nuclear third, and batteries last. A fifth outcome stays live: none of the four qualifies, and domestic-demand stocks such as airlines keep the top relative strength. "Leader" here means beating the KOSPI. It does not mean rising in absolute terms, and the typical biotech stock is still 56% below its yearly high.</strong>

- On the 14 days in the last 40 trading days when Samsung fell 2% or more, the biotech basket fell 1.89% on average. Batteries fell 3.82%, defense 3.44%, nuclear 3.22% and shipbuilding 3.01%. On the 9 days Samsung rose 2% or more, biotech gained 1.38%, the most of the six baskets.
- Over the 20 trading days from October 12 to November 6, pension funds bought an estimated ₩871 billion of biotech and sold shipbuilding (₩103 billion), defense (₩168 billion) and nuclear (₩201 billion). Foreign investors sold ₩1.39 trillion of batteries.
- Data-center stocks rose 10.0% in five days, but they barely follow Samsung (correlation 0.06) and make up only 1.56% of market trading value. We read that as a shelter, not a leader, until Naver reports on November 12.
- The lead is limited to KOSPI large caps. Since October 28, Hanmi Pharm rose 26.4%, SK Biopharmaceuticals 18.1%, Celltrion 11.2% and Samsung Biologics 10.5%, while Alteogen, LigaChem Biosciences and ABL Bio fell 10% to 14%.

## Who buys while Samsung stalls decides which group leads

If Samsung stalls, foreign selling of the chip giants can pause. Foreign investors sold ₩16.6 trillion of Samsung Electronics and SK hynix over the last 20 trading days. But the won weakened from 1,325 per dollar on November 2 to 1,356 on November 6, so broad foreign buying of Korean stocks is hard to count on.

That leaves domestic institutions, and pension funds in particular, to set the direction of the rotation. Pension funds put an estimated ₩871 billion into biotech and pharma over the 20 trading days and took money out of all three of shipbuilding, defense and nuclear. Celltrion (₩302 billion) and Samsung Biologics (₩414 billion) account for about 82% of the biotech total.

![Pension funds bought 870.7 billion won of biotech and pharma in 20 trading days to November 6 and sold 103.0 billion won of shipbuilding, 167.6 billion of defense and 201.1 billion of nuclear. They bought 412.3 billion won of batteries and 161.0 billion of data centers. Foreign investors sold 1,387.8 billion won of batteries and 115.9 billion of biotech, and bought small amounts of data centers, shipbuilding, defense and nuclear.](/post-assets/samsung-stalls-near-200k-large-cap-biotech-leads-pension-funds-2026-11-09/flows.en.svg)

We treat this as the heaviest variable. A group that domestic pension money keeps buying can hold up when nobody else is buying. Foreign investors went the other way on batteries, selling ₩1.39 trillion, the largest outflow among the six baskets.

## Biotech keeps pace when Samsung rises and gives back less when it falls

Pension buying shows who is willing to hold biotech. The reaction to Samsung's big days shows how biotech behaves in either direction. In the last 40 trading days, Samsung fell 2% or more on 14 days and rose 2% or more on 9 days.

On the down days the biotech basket lost 1.89% on average, against 3.01% for shipbuilding, 3.22% for nuclear, 3.44% for defense and 3.82% for batteries. On the up days biotech gained 1.38%, the most of the six baskets.

The biotech basket's median hides a split. Large KOSPI names carry the pension buying and the gains. The KOSDAQ platform companies fell over the same period. For the lead to spread to them, US yields would have to keep falling. The 10-year Treasury yield was 4.84% on November 5, down from 5.27% on October 6.

In the first stretch of any Samsung rebound, the most heavily sold names can bounce hardest. A bounce is not leadership, and the scoring rule later in this post keeps the two apart.

## Data centers look like a shelter until Naver reports

The data-center basket (Naver, SK Telecom, KT, LG Uplus, Samsung SDS and Kakao) rose 10.0% in five days and 7.6% since October 28, the only basket that is up. Its correlation with Samsung is 0.06, and it fell only 0.34% on Samsung's big down days. Those are the numbers of a low-beta defensive group. We read the rally as shelter demand during a selloff, and that demand can unwind when Samsung rebounds.

A story could turn the shelter into a leader. After the November 3 midterms, Democrat Gina Hinojosa won the Texas governor's race. She had pledged to halt approvals of new data centers until laws on grid load, water use and electricity bills are in place (ZDNet Korea, November 5). She takes office in January, so for now it is a pledge, not a halt. NH Investment & Securities named SK Telecom, KT and LG Uplus as likely beneficiaries if Big Tech builds data centers outside the US (Newsprime, November 8). On November 6, Hana Securities called the regulation fear overdone for Samsung and SK hynix (Hankyung).

All three items are press reports. We have not read the underlying research, and no one has shown how much of this would reach Naver's or SK Telecom's revenue. The basket also makes up only 1.56% of market trading value, against 2.69% for biotech. That is too small to carry an index.

Naver reports third-quarter results at 09:00 on November 12, according to its disclosure. If AI-infrastructure revenue and the investment plan hold up, data centers move to first.

## Among shipbuilding, defense and nuclear, shipbuilding is strongest and nuclear weakest

All three baskets are 60% to 68% below their yearly highs, and they have different problems. For nuclear, Korea Hydro & Nuclear Power (KHNP) ended construction-contract talks with the Daewoo E&C and Doosan Enerbility consortium for the Czech Dukovany plant on October 29. KHNP said Daewoo E&C's extra cost demand passed ₩1 trillion after KHNP had offered a 10% increase in the construction price (Seoul Shinmun). Doosan Enerbility said it will join the rebidding. Its separate reactor and turbine supply contract continues.

The nuclear basket is down 19.9% since October 28. We did not measure how much of that the Dukovany news explains. Over 20 trading days every investor group sold Doosan Enerbility: foreign investors ₩125 billion, institutions ₩148 billion and pension funds ₩113 billion.

If the Texas pledge turns into policy, it would also cut the AI power-demand story that lifted nuclear and power equipment, and it would weaken the energy-storage demand story for batteries. Shipbuilding sits farther from that path. Institutions bought ₩466 billion of Hanwha Ocean over the 20 days, although pension funds sold ₩56 billion. In our November 6 five-day sector scan, KOSPI shipbuilding ranked fourth on rebound-day alpha (+3.81), but analysts' sector target prices were cut 28.8% over four weeks.

## How we will score it

We fixed the rules before the result. The start day is the first day on which Samsung has closed between ₩200,000 and ₩215,000 for 10 trading days in a row. Over the next 20 trading days, a basket counts as the leader only if it meets all three conditions.

1. Its equal-weighted median return beats the KOSPI by 5 percentage points or more.
2. Foreign and institutional investors together are net buyers of it.
3. Its average share of market trading value over the 20 days is higher than over the previous 60 days.

If two or more baskets qualify, the one with the larger excess return in condition 1 wins. If Samsung falls below ₩180,000 before the start day, the premise is broken and we do not score. If it stays above ₩220,000 for more than ten trading days, we treat that as a new rally and do not score either.
