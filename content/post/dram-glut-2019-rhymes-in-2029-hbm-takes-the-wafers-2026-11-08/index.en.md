Memory downturns used to follow one script. In a boom, makers add supply. When demand growth slows, node shrinks keep adding bits anyway. The gap shows up as falling prices. 2018–2019 was the textbook case. According to Micron remarks (secondary), industry DRAM bits grew <strong>20%+</strong> in 2018 with almost no new fabs. When the 2019 demand outlook slowed to the mid-teens, Micron's average DRAM selling price fell <strong>about 30%</strong>.

The market fears the same script now. Our answer: 2028 is different, and 2029 may not be. Two numbers changed since 2018. Shrinks now add <strong>6–11%</strong> of bits a year, about half the 2018 pace (our estimate). And HBM is set to use <strong>about 30%</strong> of DRAM wafers in 2027 (TrendForce). HBM needs about three times the wafer area of conventional DRAM for the same bits.

So the variable that sets supply this cycle is HBM bit demand, more than shrinks. Conventional DRAM supply comes from the wafers HBM leaves behind. If 2028 HBM bits grow 29% to 43%, conventional DRAM supply grows about 11% to 20%, overlapping or below a demand range of 12% to 20% (our assumption). The risky year is 2029, when new cleanrooms bunch up and HBM growth slows. On that path conventional supply grows 21% to 34%, as much as in 2018 or more.

- On the supply side, shrinks drove the 2019 glut. Per Micron remarks (secondary), bits grew 20%+ with almost no new wafers. The gap to demand was about 5 points on the outlook at the time, yet prices fell about 30%.
- Shrinks now add 6–11% a year. New cleanrooms supply more than half of the growth, and a new cleanroom adds supply only when its owner decides to install tools.
- 2028 HBM bits grow +29% in our central case, and memory makers' wafer allocation to HBM sets that number. Conventional supply then grows 14% to 20%, overlapping the demand range of 12% to 20%.
- If 2028 HBM bit growth comes in below +20%, the glut moves into 2028. That +20% line is the first thing to watch.
- There is a counterpoint. Micron's inventory days are already above 2018, and on an annual basis they equal the 147 days of fiscal 2019.

## The Old Explanation Fit 2019, and It Only Half Fits This Cycle

We first checked the old explanation, "demand slows, shrinks keep adding bits, and the gap becomes a price drop," line by line.

| Claim | 2019 | This cycle |
|---|---|---|
| Makers add supply into a boom | True. They invested in shrinks in 2017–2018 | True. They build cleanrooms for 2027–2029, but tools go in step by step as demand allows |
| Shrinks keep adding bits | True. Bits grew 20%+ with no new wafers | Weaker. 6–11% a year, about half of 2018 |
| The demand-supply gap becomes a price drop | True. Demand mid-teens, average selling price about −30% | True, but how many wafers HBM takes sets the size of conventional supply |
| "Supply is constrained this time" proves this time is different | The same words were said in 2018 | Words prove nothing. The size of the shrink engine and the HBM draw are different numbers |
| NAND is the same | | NAND still adds many bits through stacking (Micron G11 has about 2x the bits of G9), so the old explanation still fits |

What changed is the engine that adds bits and the demand that takes those bits first. The sections below go through each.

## In 2019, Bits Grew 20%+ With Almost No New Fabs

Start with what happened in 2019. Industry bits are the outlook at the time from copies of Micron's calls (secondary). Prices and inventory come from Micron's filings (primary).

![The 2019 cycle. Industry bit growth: 2018 supply 20%+, 2018 demand about 20%, 2019 demand mid-teens (outlook at the time). Micron DRAM average selling price: FY2018 about +35%, FY2019 about −30%. Micron inventory days: FY2017 96, FY2018 105, FY2019 147.](/post-assets/dram-glut-2019-rhymes-in-2029-hbm-takes-the-wafers-2026-11-08/cycle-2019.en.svg)

Supply kept coming from shrinks, 20%+ without new fabs. In 2018 Micron said it would hold wafer capacity and invest in node transitions (call copy, secondary). On the demand side, cloud customers digested inventory, smartphones were weak and CPUs were short. Micron's fiscal 2019 annual report (10-K) attributed the price drop to a supply-demand imbalance, customer inventory adjustments and CPU shortages.[^mu10k]

On the outlook at the time, the gap between demand and supply was about 5 points. Prices still fell about 30%. Producer inventory absorbed the gap. In fiscal 2019 Micron's inventory rose 42% while cost of goods sold rose 2%.[^secfacts] Producers cut wafer starts and capex only after prices had already fallen.

One more sentence is worth noting. In September 2018 Micron said industry supply growth from node transitions was slowing structurally (call copy, secondary). The supply-constraint story existed in 2018 too. So "supply is constrained this time" is not by itself evidence that this time is different. To say that, the numbers have to differ.

## Shrinks Now Add Half the Bits They Did in 2018

A shrink makes circuits smaller so one wafer holds more memory cells. Bits grow without a new fab. That is why, as in 2018, shrink-driven supply is hard to stop once it starts. Split bit growth by source for 2018 and 2028, and it looks like this.

![Where industry DRAM bit growth comes from. 2018: shrinks 15–20% and almost no new wafers, for industry bits of 20%+. 2028: new wafers +15.2% and shrinks 6–11%, about +24% before HBM. About 30% of 2027 wafers go to HBM.](/post-assets/dram-glut-2019-rhymes-in-2029-hbm-takes-the-wafers-2026-11-08/engine-shift.en.svg)

Total bits can still grow in the 20s in 2028. Micron also sees 2028 industry DRAM bit growth in the low 20s. But most of that growth comes from new wafers. New wafers need new cleanrooms and tools, and HBM takes part of them first.

The density gain per node went from 23–25% for 1x to 1y up to above 30% for 1γ (company figure), and falls back to the mid-20s for 1δ, one of the last 6F² generations. The bigger change is the tooling each transition needs.

![Bit density gain per node and EUV layers. Density gain: 1x to 1y (2018) 23–25%, 1γ above 30% (company), 1δ mid-20s%. EUV layers: none in 2018, Micron 1γ 1, SK hynix 1b 4, 1c 5–6, Samsung 1c 6–7. Bits added by shrinks in one year fell from 15–20% in 2017–2019 to 6–11% in 2026–2028.](/post-assets/dram-glut-2019-rhymes-in-2029-hbm-takes-the-wafers-2026-11-08/node-engine.en.svg)

EUV lithography tools are expensive and take a lot of cleanroom space. More layers mean more tools and floor space per transition, and slower transitions. Process steps also differ more than ever between products made on the same node (HBM, mobile LPDDR, server DDR5). Micron's chief technology officer said the process gap between products on one node is the largest in DRAM history.

The makers say the same. Micron said node transitions yield less than they used to.[^mucall] SK hynix said there is a limit to adding bits through shrinks. Samsung said higher capex in 2027 does not translate well into 2028 bit growth. The shared background is that the 6F² cell structure is close to its limit.

## Supply Now Comes From New Cleanrooms, and a Cleanroom Adds Supply Only When Tools Go In

With shrinks weaker, supply has to come from new wafers. Put the main new fab milestones on one page, and they bunch up from the second half of 2028 into 2029.

![Main new DRAM fab milestones. Micron ID1 first wafer mid-2027, Tongluo ships 2H27, ID2, Hiroshima and Singapore 2H28. SK hynix M15X full capacity end-2026, Yongin phase 1 starts 2027, Cheongju M17 cleanroom December 2028, Yongin phase 2 cleanroom June 2029. Samsung P5 late 2027–2028 (reports differ). CXMT from 320k wafers a month to 420k in 2027 and a 500–600k target by end-2028.](/post-assets/dram-glut-2019-rhymes-in-2029-hbm-takes-the-wafers-2026-11-08/cleanrooms.en.svg)

Wafer capacity at the four memory makers, including CXMT, is estimated to grow 15.2% on average in 2028 (based on SemiAnalysis estimates). More than half of supply growth comes from new wafers.

That creates one difference from 2018. In 2018 node transitions in existing fabs pushed bits out, and that supply was hard to stop. Now tool move-in sets supply. In its new fab investment disclosure, SK hynix said construction comes first and tools go in step by step as demand allows. Micron said more than half of its fiscal 2027 capex increase is cleanroom construction for use after late 2028. Producers can leave a building empty. That discretion sets the size of the 2029 supply wave.

## HBM Uses About Three Times the Wafer Area for the Same Bits

HBM, high-bandwidth memory, stacks DRAM dies and sits right next to an AI accelerator. It needs far more wafer area than conventional DRAM for the same capacity.

![Bits from one wafer. One conventional DRAM wafer makes 3 units of bits; one HBM wafer makes 1. Through-silicon vias, wide I/O circuits and more banks make the die bigger. The ratio is about 3x for HBM3E and above 4x for HBM4E (Micron).](/post-assets/dram-glut-2019-rhymes-in-2029-hbm-takes-the-wafers-2026-11-08/trade-ratio.en.svg)

The ratio rises with each generation. Micron said it exceeds 4x for HBM4E, and that vertically stacked HBM again carries a very high ratio. The same HBM bits take more wafers every year.

The result is the pair of numbers below. In 2027 HBM is set to use 30% of wafers and make 13% of bits.

![HBM share of the three makers' DRAM wafer starts: 18% in 2025, 22% in 2026, 30% in 2027. HBM's share of 2027 bits is about 13%. HBM makes 13 units of bits on 30 units of wafers, conventional DRAM makes 87 on 70. For the same bits HBM uses about 2.9x the wafers.](/post-assets/dram-glut-2019-rhymes-in-2029-hbm-takes-the-wafers-2026-11-08/hbm-share.en.svg)

The HBM wafer share is a TrendForce estimate.[^tfshare] The 13% bit share for 2027 is our model value. The ratio between them drives the arithmetic in the next section.

## Conventional DRAM Supply Is What HBM Leaves Behind

The arithmetic is simple. Set end-2027 wafers at 100: 30 for HBM and 70 for conventional DRAM. In 2028 wafers grow 15.2% to 115.2. HBM takes its share first. The wafers left over, times the shrink gain (8% assumed), give conventional DRAM supply.

![End-2027 wafers of 100 (HBM 30, conventional 70) grow to 115.2 in 2028. With HBM bits +43%: HBM 42.9, conventional 72.3, conventional wafers +3.3%, conventional bit supply +11.5%. With +29%: HBM 38.7, conventional 76.5, +9.3%, +18.0%. With +10%: HBM 33.0, conventional 82.2, +17.4%, +26.8%.](/post-assets/dram-glut-2019-rhymes-in-2029-hbm-takes-the-wafers-2026-11-08/wafer-split.en.svg)

If HBM bits grow 43% (the same level as TrendForce's 2028 shipment outlook), conventional wafers grow only 3.3%. With shrinks added, conventional supply grows 11.5%. If HBM bits grow only 10%, conventional wafers grow 17.4% and conventional supply grows 26.8%. Same fabs, same wafers, and HBM demand alone more than doubles the range of conventional supply.

Draw the relationship as a line, and it looks like this. The green band is our assumption that conventional DRAM demand grows 12% to 20%.

![2028 HBM bit growth against 2028 conventional DRAM bit supply growth. On the 8% shrink line: HBM +10% gives conventional +26.8%, +29% gives +18.0%, +43% (TrendForce shipment outlook) gives +11.5%. The 6% and 11% shrink lines sit below and above. The band shows conventional demand of +12–20%, and a dashed line marks +20% HBM growth.](/post-assets/dram-glut-2019-rhymes-in-2029-hbm-takes-the-wafers-2026-11-08/residual-curve.en.svg)

| 2028 HBM bit growth | 2028 HBM wafer share | Conventional supply (shrinks 6%) | Conventional supply (shrinks 8%) | Conventional supply (shrinks 11%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| +43% | 37.2% | +9.5% | +11.5% | +14.6% |
| +29% | 33.6% | +15.8% | +18.0% | +21.3% |
| +20% | 31.3% | +19.9% | +22.2% | +25.6% |
| +10% | 28.6% | +24.5% | +26.8% | +30.3% |
| 0% | 26.0% | +29.0% | +31.5% | +35.1% |

We computed the table values with the same formula. The +43% row puts TrendForce's 2028 shipment outlook on a bit path. The table holds HBM bits per wafer at the 2027 level. As HBM4E lowers bits per wafer, the same HBM bits take more wafers, and conventional supply comes in below the table.

For supply to match conventional demand growth of 12% to 20%, HBM bits must grow 20% to 37% with 6% shrinks. TrendForce sees 2028 HBM shipments up 42.9% (as cited by Forbes Japan, secondary). On that path, 2028 conventional supply sits inside the demand range or below it. For a 2019-style glut to arrive in 2028, HBM bit growth has to fall below +20%.

## 2028 HBM Bits Are Set by Memory Makers' Wafer Allocation

What sets 2028 HBM bits, then? HBM bits are the number of accelerators times the HBM in each one. Each accelerator already carries a lot, and every generation carries more.

![HBM per accelerator. AMD MI455X 432 GB (twelve 12-high HBM4 stacks), NVIDIA Rubin 288 GB (eight 12-high HBM4 stacks), Google TPU v8i 288 GB, Microsoft Maia 300 288 GB, Google TPU v8t 216 GB. NVIDIA Rubin Ultra (2027) planned 12-high HBM4E; 8-high options are under review since the third quarter, and the design is not final.](/post-assets/dram-glut-2019-rhymes-in-2029-hbm-takes-the-wafers-2026-11-08/accelerators.en.svg)

We split 2028 HBM bits into two formulas. The first is a demand ceiling: advanced packaging wafers (TSMC CoWoS and others), which cap how many accelerators can be built, times GB per stack. Analysts estimate TSMC CoWoS capacity at 130,000 wafers a month at end-2026 and 260,000 at end-2028 (not TSMC guidance).[^cowos] The second is supply: the share of new DRAM wafers allocated to HBM times HBM bits per wafer. The bits that reach the market are the lower of the two.

![2028 HBM bit growth. Demand ceiling (advanced packaging times GB per stack): central +53%, range +21% to +92%. Supply (HBM wafer allocation times bits per wafer): central +29%, range +7% to +53%. Realized is the lower of the two, +29%. Below +20%, the conventional glut moves into 2028.](/post-assets/dram-glut-2019-rhymes-in-2029-hbm-takes-the-wafers-2026-11-08/hbm28-limits.en.svg)

In the base case supply binds, because the packaging ceiling (+53%) sits above supply (+29%). So the value that most directly sets 2028 HBM bits is the share of new wafers the three makers give to HBM. Our central assumption is 70%. At 50% HBM bits grow 19%, and at 90% they grow 38% (with bits per wafer at −5%). On the central path HBM wafers grow 35% and conventional supply grows about 14%. Matching Micron's industry bit outlook (low 20s) needs a larger shrink gain for conventional DRAM, and conventional supply then grows about 20%. So we put conventional supply on the central path at 14% to 20%. One Korean broker puts 2028 HBM bit growth at about +50% for SK hynix and +55% for Samsung. At industry averages that would need more wafers for HBM than all the new wafers combined, so it sits outside our range.

We think profit per wafer sets the allocation. Makers give wafers to the product that earns more per wafer. TrendForce said that in the first quarter of 2026 a 64 GB server DDR5 module earned more revenue per wafer than HBM, and that this is the basis for 2027 HBM price increases.[^tfshare] If 2028 HBM contract prices keep per-wafer profit at or above conventional DRAM, the allocation holds.

How should NVIDIA's look at 8-high stacks be read? TrendForce reported that Rubin Ultra is evaluating 8-high stacks instead of 12-high.[^tf1027] 8-high stacks let the same HBM bits serve more GPUs. When HBM is short, accelerator counts rise as per-unit content falls, and total HBM bits hold. The risk is per-unit content falling while accelerator counts do not rise. By our arithmetic, demand starts to set HBM bits only if accelerator orders fall about 16% short of packaging plans. That is an AI-spending slowdown path, and its first signal would be cuts to hyperscaler 2028 capex.

Micron said it can run several products on the same line and shift the mix toward demand.[^mucall] Wafers that HBM absorbs can return to conventional DRAM. Because process steps differ by product, we expect a switch to take a quarter or more (our estimate).

## 2029 Looks Like 2019

In 2029 the fab schedule above overlaps. Micron's ID2, Hiroshima and Singapore (2H28), SK hynix's Cheongju M17 (December 2028) and Yongin phase 2 (June 2029), and CXMT's end-2028 target of 500,000–600,000 wafers a month (secondary report) all overlap. We set 2029 wafer growth at 15% and ran paths where HBM growth slows.

![Conventional DRAM bit supply growth. HBM bits +43% in 2028 and +30% in 2029: conventional +11.5% in 2028 and +20.8% in 2029 (2029 HBM wafer share 39%). +29% then +20%: +18.0% and +26.3% (32%). +29% then +5%: +18.0% and +33.9% (28%). A dashed line marks 2018 industry supply growth of 20%+.](/post-assets/dram-glut-2019-rhymes-in-2029-hbm-takes-the-wafers-2026-11-08/rhyme-2029.en.svg)

| Path (2028 / 2029 HBM bits) | 2028 conventional supply | 2029 conventional supply | 2029 HBM wafer share |
|---|---|---|---|
| +43% / +30% | +11.5% | +20.8% | 39% |
| +29% / +20% | +18.0% | +26.3% | 32% |
| +29% / +5% | +18.0% | +33.9% | 28% |

On all three paths, 2029 conventional supply grows as much as 2018 supply (20%+) or more. 2029 is the year with the highest risk of a 2019-style glut.

There are buffers. Producers can delay tool move-in. SK hynix said long-term agreements with 14 customers cover more than 50% of its 2027–2028 capacity (call summary, secondary). Conventional demand also responds to price. High prices have cut memory content in smartphones and PCs, so lower prices can lift content again and absorb part of a glut.

The order of events is the problem. In 2019 Micron cut wafer starts only after prices fell. If the order repeats, 2029 prices move first. Long-term agreements do not remove the risk either. Take-or-pay contracts move demand risk into buyer inventory. We covered how buyer inventory is part of the DRAM shortage in [an earlier post](/post/dram-shortage-part-inventory-buyers-stop-when-cpus-arrive-2026-10-30/).

## The Counterpoint: Producer Inventory Is Already High

Some argue that producer inventory is low this time, so this time is different. We recomputed Micron's inventory days from SEC filings.

![Micron inventory days (fiscal year-end inventory divided by annual cost of goods sold, times 365). FY16 107, FY17 96, FY18 105, FY19 147, FY20 132, FY21 95, FY22 144, FY23 181, FY24 166, FY25 136, FY26 147. On fourth-quarter cost of goods sold: FY18 99, FY19 134, FY26 142.](/post-assets/dram-glut-2019-rhymes-in-2029-hbm-takes-the-wafers-2026-11-08/inventory-days.en.svg)

At the end of fiscal 2026 inventory days were 147 on an annual basis, equal to the end of fiscal 2019 and above the end of fiscal 2018 (105). This method reads high in a year when cost of goods sold grows fast, so we also used fourth-quarter cost of goods sold. On that basis fiscal 2026 ended at 142 days, still above 99 at the end of fiscal 2018.[^secfacts]

HBM has a long process, so more of it sits in work in progress. Without that, inventory days would be lower. Even so, the claim that low producer inventory makes this time different is weak.

## The Process Engine May Return Around 2030

This analysis rests on a weak process engine. We also need to know when that premise changes.

![DRAM, HBM and NAND technology timing. DRAM 6F² shrinks: 1γ (2026), 1δ/1d (2H27–2028). New structures: 4F²/VCT (Samsung 10a target 2028, uncertain), 3D DRAM (around 2030, estimated). HBM: HBM4 (2026), HBM4E (2027), HBM5 and vertical HBM (2028). NAND: G11 with 400+ layers and about 2x bits (2H27–2028).](/post-assets/dram-glut-2019-rhymes-in-2029-hbm-takes-the-wafers-2026-11-08/roadmap.en.svg)

- 4F² and VCT (vertical channel transistor): Samsung targets mass production of its 10a process in 2028 and claims 30% to 50% higher cell density. The schedule is uncertain. Cells and peripheral circuits are made on separate wafers and bonded, so bits per wafer start may gain less than cell area.
- 3D DRAM: cells are stacked vertically. Samsung reportedly plans to apply it from 10d, and we estimate the timing at around 2030. Once in mass production, it creates an engine that adds bits by adding layers, as in NAND. That is when the old explanation, process transitions push supply, fits DRAM again.
- NAND: the old explanation still fits NAND today. Micron's G11 (400+ layers) has about twice the bits of G9. The spot price of a 512 Gb TLC NAND wafer fell 30%, from $23.00 on March 18 to $16.15 on November 2.

SK hynix's position is to stretch 6F² with EUV as long as possible. So the process engine is likely to stay weak in 2027–2028.

## What Would Flip This Call, and What to Check First

These conditions would flip the call.

- If 2028 HBM bit growth comes in below +20%, a 2019-style glut moves into 2028. That happens if lower HBM per accelerator is confirmed and accelerator counts do not rise to match, or if 2028 HBM contracts are weak.
- If tool move-in at the 2H28 cleanrooms is delayed, the 2029 supply wave gets smaller.
- If 4F² reaches mass production in 2028 as planned, the process engine returns early and 2029 supply exceeds the table above.

![What to check, in order. Mid-December: Micron FQ1 FY27 (date not set) for the 2028 HBM and conventional bit outlook, 2028 contracts and tool move-in. Mid-January: TSMC 4Q26, whether CoWoS is around 200,000 wafers a month at end-2027. Late January: SK hynix and Samsung 4Q26, whether 2028 HBM capacity grows about 30%. First half of 2027: Rubin Ultra HBM configuration. Second half of 2027: 2028 HBM contracts, whether bits under +20% come with price cuts.](/post-assets/dram-glut-2019-rhymes-in-2029-hbm-takes-the-wafers-2026-11-08/signposts.en.svg)

The first check is Micron's earnings, expected in mid-December (date not set). There we look for 2028 HBM bit growth and progress on 2028 contracts. Then, in late January, SK hynix and Samsung results show whether 2028 HBM capacity grows about 30%. Above +40% points to a larger HBM allocation, and +20% or less points to a smaller one.

This call revisits, from the supply side, the conclusion in our [provisional verdict on AI demand](/post/ai-demand-provisional-verdict-2027-supply-capped-2028-bill-2026-11-01/) that the bill lands in 2028. If the demand bill lands in 2028 orders, the bill for conventional DRAM prices lands in 2029 supply.

## Data Notes

- Industry bit supply and demand for 2017–2020 come from copies of Micron call remarks (secondary) and mix the outlook at the time with actual results. We did not find industry wafer capacity growth for those years.
- The 15–20% shrink contribution in 2018 is inferred from "bits up 20%+ with almost no new wafers." If other makers added wafers, the shrink contribution was smaller.
- HBM wafer share, the 2028 HBM shipment outlook, SK hynix's long-term agreements and Samsung's 10a schedule come from secondary reports. TrendForce's 2028 HBM +42.9% is on a shipment basis, not bits. CoWoS capacity figures are analyst and industry estimates, not TSMC guidance.
- The supply model moves about ±6 points with the wafer, shrink and HBM ratio assumptions. 2029 wafer growth of 15% is an assumption. The 2029 math also lets HBM bits per wafer improve 8%, the same as shrinks, while the 2028 math holds them flat. The HBM allocation math uses four-maker wafer growth including CXMT as an approximation; CXMT wafers do not go into Western HBM. Conventional demand growth of 12% to 20% is our assumption.
- Micron's fiscal fourth-quarter 2026 call remarks come from a third-party transcript.[^mucall] We computed inventory days from SEC XBRL data (retrieved November 8).
- If substrate shortages cap advanced packaging output, the HBM demand ceiling falls. In our arithmetic the ceiling still sits above supply, so the call is the same.
