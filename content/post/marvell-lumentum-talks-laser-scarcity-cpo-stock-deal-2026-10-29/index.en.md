On October 28, Bloomberg reported that **Marvell Technology is in advanced talks to acquire Lumentum**, the data-center laser and optics maker. A deal could be announced as soon as next week. As of this writing, neither company has confirmed the talks, and no price, premium or payment mix has been reported. [Investing.com, Oct 28](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/marvell-nears-deal-for-optical-supplier-lumentum-to-bolster-hardware-report-says-4981052)

The market answered in one evening. At 7:20 p.m. ET, Marvell traded at $232.39, **7.7% below its $251.65 close**. Lumentum traded at $1,008, **10.3% above its $913.95 close**. Marvell lost about $17 billion of market value. Lumentum gained about $8 billion on its basic share count, or about $9.5 billion on a diluted count. The combined value of the two companies fell by about $7–8 billion on the news.

**Our judgment: the strategic logic is better than the reaction suggests, and the price will likely be worse than Marvell holders hope.** Marvell wants the one part of an optical link that survives every architecture change: the laser. Co-packaged optics (CPO) can remove Marvell's own signal-processing chips. It cannot remove the light source. But at similar earnings multiples, a stock-funded deal is a transfer of value to Lumentum holders unless the premium stays small. The variable that decides whether the transfer pays back is **whether laser supply stays scarce after 2028**, when today's capacity additions arrive.

This article is dated October 29, 2026. It covers a reported deal, not an announced one. Prices are Nasdaq closes and after-hours trades on October 28, US time. All deal terms below are our scenarios.

After hours on October 28, Lumentum rose 10% and Marvell fell 8%.](/post-assets/marvell-lumentum-talks-laser-scarcity-cpo-stock-deal-2026-10-29/price-rebased.en.png)

## Five things to take away

- **Marvell would be buying a company almost as profitable as itself, at almost the same multiple.** Both reported a 36.6% non-GAAP operating margin in their latest quarter. On guided-quarter earnings, Marvell trades at about 57 times and Lumentum at about 54 times. The common view that Marvell would dilute a high-margin chip business with a low-margin component business is wrong at the operating line.
- **The real asset is laser capacity.** According to a summary of Lumentum's August call, management described its pump lasers as effectively sold out. Its ultra-high-power lasers for CPO reach high volume in the second half of 2027. Nvidia paid $2 billion in March partly for capacity access rights.
- **On our arithmetic, the price would have to be paid mostly in Marvell shares.** Marvell has $3.9 billion of cash and a new $3 billion revolver. Even at its 4.5x acquisition leverage limit, new debt could cover only about a fifth to a quarter of a $110–120 billion price.
- **Marvell's after-hours loss already equals a 20% premium with no synergy credit.** Its $17 billion loss is about what a 20% premium on Lumentum's diluted value would transfer to Lumentum holders.
- **The deal is long-dated.** China's market regulator (SAMR) has cleared Marvell's and Lumentum's past deals, but each review took five to nine months. Lumentum shipped 79% of last year's revenue to customers outside the US.

## What was reported, and what was not

Bloomberg's report says the talks are advanced and private, and that terms and timing could change. Reuters added that neither company immediately responded to requests for comment. Bernstein analysts said a deal of this size probably could not be funded entirely in cash. [Reuters via Boursorama, Oct 29](https://boursorama.com/bourse/actualites/marvell-serait-en-pourparlers-avances-en-vue-d-acquerir-lumentum-selon-bloomberg-news-a1c5f80208f3ffe4c2f665dbf6b9fb19)

One filing makes the report more credible. On October 22, six days before the leak, Marvell replaced its $1.5 billion revolving credit facility with a **$3 billion facility**, expandable by $2 billion. The new agreement lets Marvell's leverage limit rise from 4.0 to 4.5 times for the quarter in which it completes an acquisition of at least $500 million and the following three quarters. [Marvell 8-K, Oct 23](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1835632/000119312526430424/d147430d8k.htm) That is a liquidity tool, not deal funding. But it is the kind of housekeeping a company does before a large transaction.

What we do not know matters more than what we know. We do not know the price, the share of cash, whether Nvidia supports the deal, or what Lumentum's board wants. Every number below is a scenario built from public results.

## Marvell: from connectivity chips to custom silicon

Marvell reported **record revenue of $2.739 billion** for its fiscal second quarter, which ended August 1, 2026. That was 37% more than a year earlier. Data-center revenue was $2.17 billion, 79% of the total, and grew 46%. Non-GAAP gross margin was 58.9%, non-GAAP operating margin 36.6%, and non-GAAP earnings per share $0.94. For the third quarter, Marvell guided revenue of $3.15 billion, plus or minus 5%, and non-GAAP EPS of $1.10. [Marvell Q2 FY27 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1835632/000183563226000022/q227_8kx812026ex-991.htm)

Growth comes from connectivity and custom silicon.

- **Connectivity.** This is the Inphi heritage: optical DSPs for 800G and 1.6T modules, active electrical cable chips, retimers and switches. On the August call, management said 1.6T DSPs are ramping and that scale-out switching should more than double this fiscal year. [MarketBeat call summary, Aug 27](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/transcript-marvell-technology-q2-earnings-call-highlights-2026-08-27/)
- **Custom silicon.** These are AI accelerators and attached chips designed with hyperscalers. Management said custom revenue ramps sharply in the second half of this fiscal year and should more than double next year.

Agreements signed in 2026 changed the shape of the company.

The Google warrant is worth reading closely. Its 240 performance tranches vest at one per $500 million of custom revenue through fiscal 2033. Full vesting would therefore require **$120 billion** of Google custom revenue. That is a ceiling, not a forecast. It still shows how large both companies think the opportunity could become.

At its October 6 Investor Day, Marvell raised its fiscal 2028 revenue target to about **$20 billion** and set a fiscal 2031 target of **$70–90 billion**, with EPS above $30. Press reports put interconnect at about $37.5 billion of the 2031 midpoint. [Investing.com, Oct 6](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/marvell-technology-stock-rallies-following-ambitious-investor-day-targets-4934767) The stock closed at $287.01 that day and has fallen since.

The balance sheet is modest for a company worth $220 billion. On August 1, Marvell held **$3.93 billion of cash** and **$4.96 billion of debt**. Operating cash flow was $606 million in the quarter. Diluted shares were 921 million before any Google warrant shares vest.

## Lumentum: the laser maker that AI made scarce

Lumentum reported revenue of **$1.006 billion** for its fiscal fourth quarter, which ended June 27, 2026. That was 109% more than a year earlier and 24.5% more than the previous quarter. Components, mostly lasers, were $649 million. Systems, mostly transceivers and optical circuit switches, were $357 million. Non-GAAP gross margin was 50.4%, non-GAAP operating margin 36.6%, and non-GAAP EPS $3.23. Revenue for the full fiscal year was $3.01 billion, up 83%. [Lumentum Q4 FY26 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1633978/000162828026055726/lite_ex991xq4fy26.htm)

For the September quarter, Lumentum guided revenue of **$1.225–1.275 billion**, a non-GAAP operating margin of 39.5–40.5%, and non-GAAP EPS of $4.05–4.35. It reports on November 5. [Lumentum, reporting date](https://investor.lumentum.com/financial-news-releases/news-details/2026/Lumentum-Announces-Reporting-Date-for-Fiscal-First-Quarter-2027-Financial-Results/default.aspx)

The headline GAAP number looks alarming and is not. Lumentum reported a **GAAP net loss of $7.2 billion** in the quarter. A non-cash $7.8 billion loss on debt extinguishment more than explains it, because holders converted notes into shares at prices far below the current stock price. The cost is real, but it is paid in shares, not cash. Common shares rose from 71.4 million in January to 89.7 million in August. Non-GAAP weighted diluted shares were 101.1 million in the June quarter.

These Lumentum products matter most for AI data centers.

- **Electro-absorption modulated lasers (EMLs).** These are the light sources inside most 800G and 1.6T pluggable modules. Lumentum reported record EML revenue. 200G-per-lane EMLs were more than a quarter of EML revenue.
- **Continuous-wave and ultra-high-power (UHP) lasers.** These supply light to silicon-photonics engines and to CPO. Lumentum expects high-volume UHP shipments in the second half of 2027. It has a first order for an external light source module, also for delivery in the second half of 2027.
- **Optical circuit switches (OCS).** These switch light without converting it to electricity. Shipments doubled from the previous quarter, and Lumentum guided its first quarter of more than $100 million of OCS revenue.
- **Transceivers.** Through Cloud Light, acquired in 2023, Lumentum builds modules itself. It began production shipments of 1.6T modules.

Source for the product details: [MarketBeat call summary, Aug 11](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/lumentum-q4-earnings-call-highlights-2026-08-11/)

On the same call, according to MarketBeat's summary, management said pump lasers were effectively sold out. That phrase explains the deal better than any synergy slide.

Nvidia had already reached the same conclusion. On March 2, Nvidia announced a **$2 billion investment in Lumentum**, a multi-billion-dollar purchase commitment and **future capacity access rights for advanced laser components**. The money supports a new US fab. [Nvidia–Lumentum release, Mar 2](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1633978/000119312526085412/d41019dex991.htm) On the same day, Nvidia announced a similar $2 billion agreement with Coherent, Lumentum's closest merchant rival. [DatacenterDynamics, Mar 2](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/nvidia-invests-2bn-in-optics-solutions-supplier-coherent/) Nvidia, whose switches and CPO platforms need these lasers, secured two laser suppliers in one day. It did not bet on one.

Lumentum's customer list is concentrated. Its two largest customers were 26.6% and 15.0% of fiscal 2026 revenue; the company does not name them. Shipments to customers outside the United States were 79.2% of revenue. [Lumentum 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1633978/000162828026057358/lite-20260627.htm)

The operating-margin and capex lines carry the argument. The operating margins are the same. The capital intensity is not. Marvell designs chips and pays foundries to make them. A combined company would earn about the same operating margin, but it would need about 1.7 times Marvell's current capex per dollar of revenue: 7.2% instead of 4.3%.

## Why Marvell wants lasers: CPO removes the DSP, not the light

![The laser is the one part every optical link keeps. In pluggable modules, the DSP, laser, silicon photonics engine and module assembly are separate parts. Linear-drive optics reduce or remove the DSP. Co-packaged optics remove the DSP and move the photonics and assembly into the switch package, but still need an external laser source.](/post-assets/marvell-lumentum-talks-laser-scarcity-cpo-stock-deal-2026-10-29/optical-link-layers.en.png)

An optical link consists of a DSP that cleans up the signal, a laser that makes the light, a photonics engine that puts data on the light, and a module that packages everything. Marvell is strong in the first layer and has been building the third through Celestial AI. It has no laser.

Each step toward tighter integration attacks the layer where Marvell is strongest.

- **Linear-drive optics** remove or simplify the DSP to save power.
- **Co-packaged optics** move the optics next to the switch chip. The DSP disappears from that link, and the module maker loses the assembly work.
- In both cases, the **laser stays**. In CPO it moves outside the package as an external light source, because lasers run hot and fail more often than silicon.

This is a hedge Marvell's shareholders should understand. Marvell's fiscal 2031 plan relies heavily on interconnect. Some of that interconnect revenue is exposed to the same CPO transition that Nvidia and Broadcom are promoting. Owning Lumentum would give Marvell a stake in every version of the link, including the one that removes its DSP. We wrote in June that EML and CW lasers had become the bottleneck of AI optics. [KII, Jun 3](/post/marvell-follow-up-eml-cw-dfb-laser-source-korea-cpo-2026-06-03/)

There is also a competitive reason. TrendForce lists Broadcom among the leading suppliers of CW and EML lasers. [TrendForce, Jun 3](https://www.trendforce.com/presscenter/news/20260603-13077.html) Broadcom also sells CPO switches. [Broadcom, Oct 8](https://investors.broadcom.com/news-releases/news-release-details/broadcom-redefines-ai-infrastructure-industry-leading-networking) Broadcom can offer a hyperscaler a full optical path. Marvell cannot today. With Lumentum, Marvell could offer custom accelerators, scale-up optics from Celestial AI, lasers and OCS from one supplier.

## Synergy: what is real and what is a slide

We separate the kinds of synergy, because the deal will likely be marketed with all of them.

**Real: supply assurance in a shortage.** Today a hyperscaler that buys a Marvell DSP still has to find lasers. If lasers are the constraint, a supplier that can commit DSP, laser and photonics capacity together has a selling advantage. This synergy is largest exactly while lasers are scarce.

**Plausible: co-design for scale-up optics.** Celestial AI's Photonic Fabric needs external light. Lumentum's UHP lasers and external light source modules are designed for that job. Designing them together could shorten qualification time. The volumes start in the second half of 2027 at the earliest.

**Weak: cost synergies.** The two companies use different fabs, different processes and largely different engineers. Overlap is limited to corporate costs, sales and some photonics research. We do not expect cost synergies to be large relative to a $110 billion price.

Then come the dis-synergies, which the market priced on Wednesday evening.

- **Channel conflict.** Marvell sells DSPs to module makers. Lumentum's Cloud Light competes with those module makers. After a deal, Marvell would compete with some of its own DSP customers.
- **Customer dual-sourcing.** Large buyers dislike depending on a supplier that also sells against them. Nvidia already holds capacity rights at both Lumentum and Coherent. In a shortage, customers cannot easily leave. When capacity loosens, they can.
- **Capital intensity.** Fabless Marvell would own fabs. Lumentum's fiscal fourth-quarter capex alone was $167 million.
- **Gross margin optics.** On the latest quarters, the combined non-GAAP gross margin would be about 56.6% instead of 58.9%. Operating margin would barely change. Some investors screen on gross margin, so the stock may be valued lower even though the business is not.

## Financing: on the arithmetic, this is a stock deal

Lumentum's diluted equity value at its October 28 close was about **$92 billion**, on the June quarter's weighted diluted share count. A 20–30% premium implies a purchase price of roughly **$111–120 billion**, before Lumentum's remaining convertible notes.

Marvell's capacity to borrow is limited by its own covenant. The 8-K does not define the leverage ratio, so the arithmetic below is illustrative. On guided quarters, Marvell earns about $1.17 billion of non-GAAP operating income a quarter. Lumentum's guidance implies about $0.5 billion. Together that is roughly **$6.7 billion a year** of operating income; earnings before depreciation are somewhat higher. If leverage is measured on earnings of that size, the 4.5x acquisition-quarter limit allows total debt of about $30–35 billion. The two companies already carry about $6.6 billion. That leaves roughly **$23–28 billion of new debt at the covenant ceiling**, and less if Marvell wants to keep its credit rating comfortable.

Our assumption, consistent with Bernstein's view: **at least 75–80% of the price would be paid in Marvell shares**, unless a partner such as Nvidia contributes capital. That makes the exchange ratio, not the headline price, the number to watch next week.

![Change in Marvell run-rate EPS before synergies at premiums of 0% to 40%, in an all-stock deal. At no premium, EPS rises 1.5%. At 20%, it falls 4.0%. At 30%, it falls 6.5%. At 40%, it falls 8.9%. Existing Marvell holders would own 64% to 71% of the combined company.](/post-assets/marvell-lumentum-talks-laser-scarcity-cpo-stock-deal-2026-10-29/deal-dilution.en.png)

Because the two companies trade at similar multiples, a stock deal at no premium would be roughly neutral to Marvell's earnings per share. The premium is what dilutes. At a 20% premium, Marvell's run-rate non-GAAP EPS would fall about 4%; at 30%, about 6.5%.

Growth can close the gap. Lumentum guided 24% sequential revenue growth for the September quarter; Marvell guided 15%. If Lumentum keeps growing faster, the dilution shrinks each quarter. This is the honest bull case on the arithmetic. It only works if laser prices and volumes stay strong for several years.

## Regulators: SAMR is the clock

Marvell and Lumentum both have experience with China's State Administration for Market Regulation. SAMR cleared Marvell's purchase of Cavium in June 2018, about seven months after it was announced. [Marvell, Jun 28, 2018](https://investor.marvell.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/838/marvell-technology-group-ltd-announces-receipt-of-chinese-regulatory-approval-for-cavium-acquisition) It cleared Marvell's purchase of Inphi in March 2021, about five months after announcement. [MLex, Mar 24, 2021](https://www.mlex.com/mlex/articles/2128899) It cleared Lumentum's purchase of Oclaro in December 2018, about nine months after announcement. [Lumentum 8-K, Dec 6, 2018](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001633978/000163397818000150/lumentumform8-ksamrpressre.htm)

This deal is larger and more strategic. Much of the world's optical-module assembly takes place in China, so Chinese module makers buy large volumes of optical DSPs and EMLs. A regulator that wants to protect them can ask for supply commitments or simply take time. We assume a review of at least nine to twelve months. During that period, Lumentum will trade at a discount to the offer price, and Marvell will carry the uncertainty.

## Stock impact: Marvell's evening loss already matches a 20% premium

Lumentum's diluted value at the close was about $92 billion. A 20% premium on that value is about **$18.5 billion**. Marvell lost about **$17 billion** of market value after hours. Marvell's holders, in other words, treated a premium of roughly 18–20% as a straight transfer and gave the strategic logic no credit yet. Lumentum's own 10% rise points to a smaller premium, or to a real chance that the talks fail.

That makes the announcement terms the first test. The table below assumes an all-stock deal and no synergy credit. It shows the mechanical effect on Marvell's market value relative to the after-hours price.

| Announced premium | Value transferred to Lumentum holders | Already priced after hours | Further move for Marvell, no synergy credit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10% | about $9bn | about $17bn | about +4%, a partial recovery |
| 20% | about $18bn | about $17bn | about −1% |
| 30% | about $28bn | about $17bn | about −5% |
| 40% | about $37bn | about $17bn | about −10% |

These are mechanical estimates, not forecasts. Marvell would trade above them on credible supply commitments from Nvidia or a hyperscaler to the combined company, a visible synergy plan for CPO lasers, or a share-heavy structure without large new debt. It would trade below them on a large cash component funded by debt, or on SAMR conditions that limit how the combined company sells.

If the talks fail, the arithmetic reverses. Marvell would likely recover most of the after-hours loss, and Lumentum would give up its gain.

For Lumentum holders, the stock will start trading on the deal spread once terms are public. With a long review, we would expect a spread wider than for a routine semiconductor deal.

## Our judgment, and what would change it

The deal makes industrial sense. It is the most direct way for Marvell to stay relevant as optics move into the package. **Laser supply is the variable that dominates**, and it ranks ahead of the premium and the regulator. If lasers remain the bottleneck through 2028 and beyond, Marvell will have bought the scarcest asset in AI networking, and the premium will look small. If Coherent, Broadcom, Lumentum's new fab and Asian suppliers add enough indium phosphide capacity by 2028, Marvell will have paid about 55 times earnings for a cyclical component maker. It will also own its fabs.

| Evidence | What it would mean |
|---|---|
| Announced premium of 20% or less, mostly stock | The evening move already priced the cost; Marvell can recover part of it |
| Nvidia or a hyperscaler signs long-term supply agreements with the combined company | Customers accept a vertically integrated Marvell; dis-synergy risk falls |
| Lumentum's November 5 report shows laser lead times and prices still rising | Scarcity is lasting; the strategic value is growing |
| Premium of 30% or more, or debt above $15bn | Dilution exceeds what growth can close within a year |
| Coherent or Broadcom announce large new InP laser capacity for 2027–28 | Scarcity premium fades; Marvell would be buying near a cyclical peak |
| SAMR review extends beyond twelve months or adds supply conditions | Value is delayed, and the combined company loses pricing freedom in China |

The next check is November 5, when Lumentum reports and may comment on the talks. Marvell reports its fiscal third quarter in late November or early December. If a deal is announced first, the exchange ratio will settle most of the near-term debate.
