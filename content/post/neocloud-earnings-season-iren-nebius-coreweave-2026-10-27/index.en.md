A wave of earnings calls is coming for the companies that rent AI computing, often called neoclouds. Their stocks fell hard in October. Which call can show that the fall went too far?

Price alone does not answer this. From the highest close in October, IREN fell 39.1%, Nebius 38.7% and CoreWeave 39.8%. So we rank the calls by what each one can prove, not by how far the stock fell. This is an order of examination. It is not an expected-return ranking, a price target or a call to buy.

Prices are US regular-session closes on October 26, 2026. All call times are Korea time. Where a number is our calculation or an outside estimate, the text says so.

## Five things to take away

- **Price does not pick a favorite.** The three main names are within 1.2 points of each other from their October highs. If each recovered half of its drop, the gain would be 32% to 33% for all three, a gap of about 1.6 points.
- **IREN has the most concrete test.** Its call is confirmed for November 6 at 07:00. It can show whether delivered equipment became accepted, billed and cash-generating. Its new $2.4bn GPU loan repays in 30 months, so the funding table matters as much as the revenue.[^iren10k]
- **Nebius is close behind.** Its revenue conversion is better documented. But the market already expects revenue to jump about 55% in one quarter, and the company has not confirmed a call date.[^nbisq2]
- **CoreWeave is a financing story.** Its own outlook shows interest expense far above operating profit. The stock can move most if funding worries ease, and the same logic works in reverse.[^crwvoutlook]
- **The biggest drop is not the best setup.** Cipher Digital (CIFR) fell 50.9%, but it carries the first $359.3m of overruns at one of its projects.[^cifrbarber]

## 1. Order of examination

| Order | Name | Why it comes here | What would change the order |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | IREN | A confirmed near date. The call can link delivery, customer acceptance and billing to revenue and cash. | Lower if only the ARR figure improves, if acceptance slips, or if a funding gap appears. |
| Close second | Nebius (NBIS) | Revenue conversion and margins are better documented. It must beat a high bar. | Could move first if it beats Q3 expectations and shows Q4 billing and long-term funding. Lower if conversion slips while spending rises. |
| Conditional | CoreWeave (CRWV) | The largest swing if financing worries ease. | Needs better funding terms and better cash flow together. We found no fresh credit-market data. |
| Later | Cipher Digital (CIFR) | The biggest drop, but real cost and schedule problems. | Higher once costs and delivery dates settle and rent is collected. |

IREN and Nebius are close. The IREN rank reflects a confirmed date and a concrete chain to check. It does not mean IREN is cheaper, safer or more likely to rise.

## 4. IREN: can delivered equipment become revenue and cash?

IREN's AI cloud revenue was $70.5m in the quarter to June, up from $33.6m in the quarter before. Total revenue was $137.2m. The company reported $1bn of operating annualized run-rate revenue (ARR) today and $4bn of contracted ARR for 2026 capacity. At the time, the 50MW Horizon 1 site was delivered, Horizon 2 was being commissioned, and Horizons 3 and 4 were targeted for the fourth quarter of 2026.[^irenfy26]

**The first trap is the ARR definition.** IREN's operating ARR is not the last month's revenue times 12. The company takes the number of contracted GPUs that are ready to run, applies the hourly contract price and 8,760 hours a year, and adds minor items. It says actual recognized revenue can be materially lower. So the $1bn operating figure and the $4bn contracted figure both measure contract value for GPUs, not recognized revenue. The gap between them is capacity that still has to be delivered and billed. Neither is comparable with Nebius, which defines ARR from the last month's actual AI revenue.[^irenfy26][^nbisq2]

**The second trap is the loan schedule.** A $2.4bn GPU financing signed on August 25 carries a fixed 9% rate, matures 30 months after each drawdown and repays in installments. The parent company gives an unconditional guarantee of payment.[^iren10k] The maturity table at June 30 shows only $177.4m of repayments in fiscal 2027, but it does not include this new loan. Reading that table alone would understate near-term repayments. Reading the loan as isolated project risk would be wrong too, given the guarantee. A separate $3.6bn financing tied to Microsoft work carries a weighted 6% rate. With customer prepayments it covers 96% of the GPU spending for that contract. It does not cover the whole data center build or later expansions.[^irenfy26]

**Expectations are not low.** Public aggregates expect total revenue of $221m (S&P estimates via Moomoo) to $230m (Investing.com) for the quarter to September. Against $137.2m last quarter, that is a rise of 61% to 68%. These are aggregates, not company guidance.[^consensus] Total revenue also includes mining and the Mirantis acquisition that closed in August, which added about 580 people and costs.[^iren10k] A revenue beat alone does not show that AI deployment drove it.

Four things would strengthen the case: higher recognized AI revenue, Horizon 2 customer acceptance and billing, Horizon 3 and 4 dates kept, and a funding table that nets cash, prepayments and committed loans against remaining capex and repayments. A new contract announcement helps but does not replace them. The case weakens if ARR rises while acceptance, revenue and cash do not, or if a funding gap shows up for equipment already contracted.

## 5. Nebius: stronger evidence, higher bar

Nebius group revenue: 582.3 last quarter, expected 905.7, a rise of 55.5 percent in one quarter." loading="lazy" style="display:block;width:100%;min-width:720px;max-width:none;height:auto;margin:0 auto"></div>

Nebius reported second-quarter group revenue of $582.3m, of which AI cloud was $574.9m. The adjusted EBITDA margin of the AI business was about 50%, and ARR was $3.0bn, defined as the last month's actual AI revenue times 12. Adjusted EBITDA leaves out depreciation, financing costs and capital spending, so it is not cash left for shareholders.[^nbisq2]

The bar is high. Public aggregates expect third-quarter revenue of about $906m, or 55.5% more than the second quarter.[^consensus] So a year-on-year multiple is not enough. The call needs to show whether capacity is actually running and billing, and what the path through the fourth quarter looks like.

Nebius's funding access is open, but the terms differ by period. It closed a $5.75bn convertible note offering in August.[^nbiscb] A low coupon does not mean a low total cost, because conversion terms and dilution matter. On October 22 it announced a $3.5bn senior secured facility from Citi with a 364-day term, an initial margin of SOFR plus 275 basis points and a stated top of plus 350.[^nbiscity] Securing a limit, drawing cash and refinancing for the long term are three different steps.

Nebius could overtake IREN if it meets the high Q3 bar and shows Q4 billing, prepayments received, remaining spending and long-term funding in one picture. It should fall if conversion slips while spending rises and short-term funding keeps being extended.

## 6. CoreWeave: financing, not revenue, is the swing

| CoreWeave Q3 outlook | Range |
|---|---:|
| Revenue | $3.45bn to $3.60bn |
| Adjusted operating income | $200m to $260m |
| Interest expense | $860m to $940m |
| Capital spending | $11.5bn to $13.5bn |

At the midpoints, adjusted operating income of $230m is $670m below interest expense of $900m. This compares two different measures to show scale. It is not a net income forecast.[^crwvoutlook] At June 30, about 89.6% of its debt was recourse to the company by carrying amount. In the first half, operating cash flow of $3.66bn minus purchases of equipment and software of $14.12bn left a gap of $10.45bn.[^crwv10q] These are past or guided figures, not forecasts for later quarters.

With this much debt, less worry about funding can change what is left for shareholders a lot, which is why CoreWeave has the largest conditional swing. The same effect works the other way if funding gets harder. A revenue beat or a bigger backlog does not settle the financing question. We would look at the rate, term, collateral and recourse of any new borrowing, the credit quality of customer contracts, the amount drawn, installed capacity and the remaining funding gap. We did not obtain fresh credit-default swap or bond trade data, so we do not call the credit worry resolved.

## 7. Cipher and the other landlords

Cipher Digital fell 50.9% from its October high and 56.5% since September 25. A September 25 amendment to its Barber Lake lease extended the term to 20 years. It also set delivery windows from the fourth quarter of 2026 to the first quarter of 2027. Cipher bears the first $359.3m of cost above the original budget. Above that, the tenant reimburses 50% through rent over 20 years.[^cifrbarber] A larger contract does not mean cost and timing risk got better. On October 14 Cipher priced $850m of 0% convertible notes.[^cifrcb] That announcement shows market access but does not settle closing, net cash or dilution. We would revisit Cipher when costs and dates are stable and rent from Black Pearl is received.

| Company | Evidence so far | Decisive question for the call |
|---|---|---|
| Core Scientific (CORZ) | Q2 colocation revenue $136.7m; billable capacity 395MW, 437MW by mid-July | Does more billable capacity become recurring revenue and cash? |
| TeraWulf (WULF) | Q2 HPC lease revenue $31.9m; 102MW running in July; adjusted EBITDA −$18.3m; interest expense $56.4m | Do new deliveries narrow the gap between future contracts and today's cash? |
| Hut 8 (HUT) | 949MW contracted as of its Aug 4 release; $26.6bn contract value; $7.5bn project financing | Do loan drawdowns and construction match billing dates? |

These businesses are at different stages, so we do not compare them on one revenue multiple.[^peers]

## 8. Short interest: it can amplify a move, but it does not rank the stocks

The latest figures are for the settlement date of October 15, not October 26.

| Company | Shares short, Sep 30 | Shares short, Oct 15 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| IREN | 81,653,639 | 94,617,935 | +15.9% |
| Nebius | 44,695,449 | 47,638,283 | +6.6% |
| CoreWeave | 59,952,639 | 69,779,354 | +16.4% |

This is the change in the number of shares sold short from a secondary source. It is not the change in the share of shares outstanding.[^short] All three companies have convertible notes, and some short positions may hedge them. We do not know how many, so we cannot treat the balance as shares waiting to be bought back. The same rule applies to all three. IREN should not be judged by a harsher standard than the others.

## Conclusion: the first test is delivery turning into cash

If we had to examine one call first, it would be IREN's on November 6. We would look for four things at once: recognized AI revenue, Horizon 2 acceptance and billing, kept delivery dates and a funding table that includes the new loan. If they improve together, the rebound case gets stronger.

Nebius is a nearly equal alternative. If it beats a high bar and also shows fourth-quarter billing and long-term funding, it could come first. CoreWeave has the largest conditional swing, but we would not call the credit worry resolved on revenue growth alone. Nothing in this post says the credit market as a whole has improved.
