Samsung Electronics reported preliminary third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 107.4 trillion. Its consolidated operating margin was 55.1%. The scale of earnings is clear. It does not prove that earnings at this level can last.

CXMT's threat is also easy to miss if viewed only as a technology ranking. Before the Chinese supplier matches all of Samsung's leading products, it may win some orders from Chinese customers. Once customers have an alternative, Samsung can face pressure on both the price of remaining volumes and the terms of its next contracts.

For a Samsung investment, the more useful question is not “Who announced 4F² first?” but “How much demand remains for products that customers must buy from Samsung?” The durability of the boom should be assessed through CXMT's good-die shipments, Samsung's 2027 contracts, its share of premium products, and AI customers' cash flow after investment.

## Current earnings are strong. Their durability needs to be tested again.

- CXMT's new-process announcement alone is not enough to discard the Samsung investment case. Applying a low multiple to high current earnings does not automatically create a margin of safety.
- The nearer-term risks are higher supply of existing DDR5 and mobile DRAM, and import substitution within China. If 4F² economics are proven, lower costs would add to those risks.
- Whether Chinese capacity expansion drives prices down depends on demand growth and the existing shortage. The same capacity increase means different things when AI installations are growing versus when orders and inventories are shrinking.
- Samsung's defenses are good-die cost, customer qualification, supply reliability, and product mix. The question is whether these can protect earnings as the boom weakens.

The as-of date is October 13, 2026. This article compares Samsung Electronics common and preferred shares (005930 and 005935) with Chinese DRAM maker CXMT, whose listed entity is ChangXin Technology (688825.SH). It distinguishes official announcements and conference materials from reprinted company filings, industry estimates, and reported sourcing. The supply and cost calculations below are illustrative assumptions, not forecasts or investment probabilities for a specific year.

Second-quarter DS operating profit of KRW 89.2 trillion was nearly the same as consolidated operating profit of about KRW 89.5 trillion. MX and Networks reported an operating loss of KRW 0.7 trillion.

The claim that “Samsung has finished-product businesses, so it is safe if memory prices fall” does not match its current earnings structure. At the same time, DS includes foundry and System LSI as well as memory. DS earnings should not be labeled as memory-only earnings.

## G5 mass production and 4F² economics are different questions

On September 20, CXMT announced mass production of its G5 platform and 24Gb LPDDR5X products. A reprint of company announcement 2026-005 says that scale sales had not yet formed, and that yield stabilization and customer validation and qualification would take time. It also warns that G5 may have no material effect on near-term results. [Reprinted G5 filing](https://stock.stockstar.com/notice/SN2026092200000007.shtml)

These statements do not conflict. Putting a product on a process line and repeatedly selling enough good die to earn a profit are different stages. G5 mass production should not be conflated with successful 4F² mass production.

Financing is evidence of the intention to expand supply, but an initial plan should not be reported as the final result. The RMB 57.9 billion in the July IPO report was the planned amount before the greenshoe option. An August 29 announcement reports gross proceeds of about RMB 66.607 billion after the full greenshoe exercise and net proceeds of about RMB 66.310 billion after expenses. [Offering and greenshoe implementation results](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-08-29/1225529556.PDF)

Disclosed funding and designed capacity are not actual shipments. Equipment must be delivered and installed; processes must stabilize; products must be converted; customers must approve them; and the facility must ramp for part of the year. Quarterly good-die bit shipments are the figures that connect supply directly to Samsung's earnings.

## 4F² may raise cell density without lowering good-die costs

A DRAM cell contains a capacitor that stores charge and a transistor that controls access to it. 6F² and 4F² describe the cell's planar area. F is the minimum dimension used for comparison. A generation name such as “10a” or a half-pitch in a particular area should not automatically be equated with F.

At the same F, cell area falls by 1 − 4 ÷ 6 = 33.3%, while the number of cells that fit in the same area rises by 6 ÷ 4 − 1 = 50.0%. A calculation showing density at 4/3 times is wrong. The density increase is 1.5 times.

Equal cell area requires 4 × F₄² = 6 × F₆². Since F₄ ÷ F₆ = √1.5 = 1.2247, a 4F² cell with F = 15 nm has the same area as a 6F² cell with F ≈ 12.25 nm. China benefits because a structure change could offset the use of a wider line. It does not automatically deliver the same speed, power, or cost.

A finished chip also has peripheral circuits for reading, writing, and input/output. Under the hypothetical assumptions that cells occupy 65% of the chip and peripheral area stays unchanged, the result is:

| Illustrative calculation | Value |
|---|---:|
| New die area ratio | 0.35 + 0.65 × 4 ÷ 6 = 0.7833 |
| Theoretical output increase | 1 ÷ 0.7833 − 1 = about 27.7% |

The 65% cell share is not an estimate for Samsung or CXMT. Wafer-edge losses are omitted. This example shows why a 50% increase in cell density cannot be transferred directly into a 50% increase in finished-chip output.

Cost analysis requires another step. A simplified ratio for cost per good bit is <strong>total process-cost multiple ÷ (pre-yield bit-output multiple × relative yield)</strong>. Assume process cost rises 20% and pre-yield bit output rises 30%:

| Relative yield versus baseline | Calculation | Change in cost per good bit |
|---|---|---:|
| 95% | 1.20 ÷ (1.30 × 0.95) | Down about 2.8% |
| 85% | 1.20 ÷ (1.30 × 0.85) | Up about 8.6% |

Relative yield of 95% does not mean an absolute yield of 95%. Higher process, bonding, or inspection cost and lower yield can reduce the benefit of smaller cells. Competitiveness depends on supplying good die that meet performance and reliability requirements at low cost, not on an image of a small cell.

## CXMT's research is real. Server-scale production needs separate proof.

A paper by CXMT researchers at IEDM 2023 describes a junctionless vertical-channel transistor and a gate structure surrounding the channel. It also reports that word-line-to-word-line leakage in a specific test structure fell from 200 µA to 2.5 pA. The ratio is 200 × 10⁻⁶ ÷ (2.5 × 10⁻¹²) = 80 million. [IEDM research paper](https://mys.mapyourshow.com/mys_shared/iedm23/handouts/6-2_Mon_16771.pdf)

The result concerns that specific leakage path. It does not establish data-retention performance, error rate, or production yield for an entire DDR5 module. It is also inaccurate to say that no technical data exist.

Samsung also presented research in the official ISSCC 2026 program combining vertical cell transistors, peripheral circuits beneath the cell array, and wafer-to-wafer copper hybrid bonding for 4F². It is not accurate to say CXMT has started the transition while Samsung has not. A technology gap of two years cannot be established by comparing a product announcement with press reports about a mass-production target. [Official ISSCC program, session 15.10](https://submissions.mirasmart.com/ISSCC2026/PDF/ISSCC2026AdvanceProgram.pdf)

Technologies that are easy to confuse differ by where they apply.

| Structure | What it changes |
|---|---|
| 4F² vertical transistor | Planar area occupied by each cell |
| Cell-to-peripheral wafer bonding | Manufacturing and placement of storage and control circuits |
| 3D DRAM with multiple cell layers | Vertical stacking of the cell array |
| HBM | Stacking completed DRAM die and connecting them to an accelerator |

Progress in 4F² DDR5 does not mean immediate replacement of the HBM4 supply chain. The fact that it does not replace HBM4 does not mean there is no effect on Samsung's conventional DRAM earnings. Public evidence is still lacking on CXMT 4F² product yield, temperature-dependent data retention, speed and power, customer qualification, and repeat shipments.

Samsung's defenses also need to be separated into those that are difficult to copy and those that must be proven repeatedly.

| Defense | Hard to replicate | Conditions that could weaken it |
|---|---|---|
| Manufacturing cost and yield | Process combinations that jointly optimize performance, cost, and reliability | Learning by a follower through repeated production and investment |
| Customer qualification | Server compatibility, power and thermal performance, long-term quality, and supply history | Chinese customers provide volume and qualification opportunities |
| Product and system integration | Joint development of HBM, server DDR5, low-power modules, SSDs, and customer platforms | Standard product specifications and expansion of alternative suppliers |
| Supply reliability | Delivery, change control, quality response, and regulatory compliance | Market separation gives CXMT procurement advantages among Chinese customers |

Memory and foundry integration create opportunities in base die and bonding technology. Internal sourcing does not automatically guarantee lower costs. A competitor's external sourcing is not inherently inferior either. Finished-product performance, cost, and delivery decide.

Digital twins could accelerate development, but without published quantitative comparisons, they do not support a calculation of how many years of development CXMT has saved. Both the claim that manufacturing know-how is a permanent shield and the claim that simulation can immediately replace it are overstated.

## Sales in China can still displace Samsung orders

Suppose a Chinese customer buys 100 units from Samsung and then switches 20 units to CXMT. If China's total demand has not grown, Samsung has lost 20 units of its existing sales channel. It must find buyers elsewhere or change its production plan.

It is wrong to say Chinese supply has no global effect simply because it is consumed in China. The key is whether it meets new consumption or replaces imports. The volume of qualified alternative supply is a more direct competitive indicator than the date China makes the world's best DRAM.

The model should also avoid the opposite error. Counting the same transaction as a 20-unit decline in world demand and a 20-unit increase in Chinese supply double-counts it. The global balance should reflect actual changes in supply and end consumption; company models should separately reflect customer and regional share shifts.

TrendForce's second-quarter DRAM revenue shares were 39.4% for Samsung, 24.9% for SK hynix, and 23.3% for Micron. TrendForce News reported CXMT at 9.5% on the same second-quarter revenue basis. Revenue share is not bit shipments or wafer capacity share. These figures are not converted into assumptions in the calculations below. [DRAM revenue report](https://www.trendforce.com/presscenter/news/20260907-13219.html), [CXMT market and technology roundup](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/10/12/news-cxmt-accelerates-4f%C2%B2-dram-push-eyes-year-end-server-ddr5-rdimm-launch/)

Supply available to a specific market can be divided into <strong>capacity × utilization × pre-yield bits per wafer × good-die yield × share of product that can be sold into that market</strong>. Designed capacity doubling does not mean actual shipments double. Conversely, bit supply can rise through node migration and better yield even when wafer starts remain unchanged.

## The impact of capacity growth differs when demand rises 15% versus 25%

Current data do not show a price collapse. TrendForce's September 30 forecast put fourth-quarter conventional DRAM contract prices up 10–15% quarter over quarter and NAND contract prices up 15–20%. It expected enterprise SSD prices to accelerate. These are industry forecasts, not confirmed realized prices. [TrendForce fourth-quarter pricing forecast](https://www.trendforce.com.tw/presscenter/news/20260930-13256.html)

Supply shortage, easing shortage, slower price growth, price declines, and a return to losses are different states. Even if prices keep rising, a lower expected rate or duration of growth can pull a stock down. There is also no basis to attribute the whole October 13 share-price decline to the 4F² news alone.

Use assumptions to test how much a supply increase could reduce a shortage. Set initial non-HBM DRAM supply and demand at 100 each. Assume CXMT has a hypothetical 10% share of bits and other suppliers increase supply by 15%. This assumes an initially balanced market with no shortage.

| Assumed CXMT bit-supply growth | Total supply growth | At demand growth of +15% | At +20% demand | At +25% demand |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| +30% | +16.5% | +1.3% | −2.9% | −6.8% |
| +50% | +18.5% | +3.0% | −1.3% | −5.2% |
| +70% | +20.5% | +4.8% | +0.4% | −3.6% |

The supply-demand gap is supply ÷ demand − 1. A positive result means supply exceeds demand; a negative result means a shortage. In the middle row, supply is 90 × 1.15 + 10 × 1.50 = 118.5. If demand rises 20%, 118.5 ÷ 120 − 1 = −1.25%; if it rises 15%, 118.5 ÷ 115 − 1 = +3.04%.

If CXMT grows 50% rather than the 15% assumed for other suppliers, its incremental effect on total supply growth is 10% × (50% − 15%) = 3.5 percentage points. Under these conditions, whether the threat merely eases the shortage or creates an oversupply depends on demand growth.

If the market was short of supply to begin with, this balanced-market assumption may be too pessimistic. Aggregate balance can also coexist with shortages of qualified high-capacity server DRAM and excess supply in some consumer products. Do not use the table as a forecast or price-change estimate.

The process transition also has to be scoped. Suppose a supplier with a hypothetical 10% share of global bits applies a new process to 20% of its production and raises good-die output there by 20%. The incremental effect on global supply is 10% × 20% × 20% = 0.4%. Treating an initial product reveal as a full conversion overstates the near-term impact. If process migration is already included in total bit growth, do not add it again.

## The most adverse combination is more Chinese supply and slower AI orders

The adverse case for Samsung is not Chinese supply growth alone. It is a combination of more good-die supply from CXMT and incumbent suppliers, delayed AI installations and procurement, and customer inventory drawdowns.

Purchases equal actual consumption plus the change in inventory. Even when usage rises, orders can slow faster if customers stop building precautionary inventory. That is why AI usage growth should not be converted directly into memory-order growth.

If expected HBM demand falls, incumbent suppliers may have an incentive to reallocate future wafer capacity to conventional DRAM. This is neither immediate nor cost-free. After a lag, slower AI orders and higher conventional DRAM supply could overlap.

AI funding risks differ by customer. In the quarter ended June 2026, cash-flow figures were as follows, in USD billions.

| Company | Operating cash flow | Cash-based capital-related spending | Free cash flow |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Microsoft | 55.4 | Cash purchases of property and equipment: 35.8 | 19.6 |
| Meta | 31.86 | 31.08, including finance lease principal | About 0.784 |

Microsoft's total capital investment of 41.0 and cash spending of 35.8 use different definitions. Meta's figure includes finance lease principal. Do not compare investment efficiency from these numbers alone. [Microsoft FY2026 Q4 earnings call](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/events/fy-2026/earnings-fy-2026-q4), [Meta Q2 results](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/)

There is no basis to say Microsoft is already running out of funds. Meta's capital-related spending was about 97.6% of operating cash flow for the quarter. That leaves limited room, but financial assets and access to financing also matter. One quarter does not predict an investment halt.

For Samsung demand, actual equipment delivery, available power, paid usage, cash collection, and cancellation or renegotiation rights matter more than headline investment totals. If memory prices rise and increase investment spending, physical demand has not necessarily grown at the same rate. Lower prices can also let customers buy more servers with the same budget and partly cushion a decline.

Consumer demand cannot be assumed to be unconditionally strong either. Counterpoint's preliminary estimate put third-quarter smartphone shipments 20% below a year earlier. Supply constraints and price pressure both contributed, so this should not all be read as an end-demand collapse. It still shows that mobile purchases face constraints. [Counterpoint preliminary estimate](https://counterpointresearch.com/en/insights/global-smartphone-shipments-hit-a-new-low-with-20-percent-yoy-decline-lowest-in-14-years)

## Price floors and purchase obligations matter more than the size of long-term contracts

TrendForce says some U.S. cloud providers have signed multi-year contracts that limit price increases. Additional volumes outside those contracts and customers without long-term agreements face different pricing. The arrival of an alternative supplier does not mean every customer will avoid long-term contracts. Customers facing a severe shortage may secure existing supply and test Chinese products at the same time. [Server DRAM contract analysis](https://www.trendforce.com/presscenter/news/20260709-13140.html)

The following terms in Samsung's 2027 contracts need to be checked.

| Contract term | What it can protect | Risk if unverified |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum purchase obligation | Shipment floor | Customer orders can fall if demand weakens |
| Price floor and adjustment formula | Selling price and margin | Prices can be renegotiated despite a multi-year term |
| Cancellation or renegotiation rights and compensation | Effective commitment to volume | Actual delivery may fall short of headline contract value |
| Product-generation transition terms | Ongoing supply relationship | Terms may change when customers move to a new product |

These terms of individual contracts have not been confirmed from public sources. Do not assume that the existence of a long-term contract guarantees both price and volume. It is also too strong to assume that an unverified product announcement alone will force major price concessions.

Policy works differently across markets. A proposed FAR rule published on February 17 explains the December 23, 2027 effective date in NDAA 5949. It restricts federal procurement of certain products and services related to designated semiconductors. It is not a blanket ban on every U.S. private-sector purchase; exceptions, waivers, and existing-equipment provisions also apply. Distinguish the proposed rule from the statutory effective date. [Federal Register proposed rule](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/02/17/2026-03065/federal-acquisition-regulation-prohibition-on-certain-semiconductor-products-and-services)

The advantage of trusted suppliers is not exclusive to Samsung. Micron is also a competitor. Import substitution in China can proceed separately from U.S. procurement restrictions. Greater market separation can also produce different pricing and qualification terms in China and elsewhere.

China-related controls are not limited to EUV. The Bureau of Industry and Security's December 2024 action covered manufacturing equipment, software, and HBM. Progress on a specific lithography workaround does not prove that all controls have been neutralized. Assess equipment access separately from customer-market access. [BIS announcement](https://media.bis.gov/press-release/commerce-strengthens-export-controls-restrict-chinas-capability-produce-advanced-semiconductors-military)

## When prices normalize, earnings can move more than revenue

Use a hypothetical unit-economics example, not an estimate of Samsung's product margins. Set price at 100 and cost per bit at 40. Then assume price falls while unit cost improves.

| Scenario | Selling price | Cost per bit | Simple unit profit | Change in unit profit |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Baseline | 100 | 40 | 60 | Baseline |
| Price −20%, cost −10% | 80 | 36 | 44 | −26.7% |
| Price −30%, cost −10% | 70 | 36 | 34 | −43.3% |

This is not an estimate of Samsung's actual product margins. It shows why higher shipments and lower costs may not offset price declines. Actual operating profit also depends on fixed costs, depreciation, foreign exchange, product mix, and inventory valuation.

Lower memory costs in finished products provide some cushion. But changes in internal Samsung memory-transfer prices are eliminated on consolidation. The real cushion comes from lower external purchasing costs and a recovery in finished-product demand. It does not automatically offset lost external memory profit.

Valuation should separate normalized conventional DRAM earnings from current earnings, HBM share and product mix, NAND and enterprise SSD profitability, non-memory results, post-investment cash flow, and shareholder returns.

The cited Monte Carlo claims of a 48% probability of a 30% price decline in 2028, a 20% CXMT bit share by 2030, or 70% of earnings declines already reflected in the share price are not adopted as verified conclusions. The code, input distributions, price model, and validation materials were unavailable, so these results could not be reproduced.

Running 3,000 iterations makes the output of given assumptions more precise; it does not make those assumptions more realistic. A setup in which only the three incumbents cut production and CXMT never does structurally drives a rising CXMT share. The model also needs to account for correlations among prices and capacity growth, AI demand and financing, and export controls and yield.

Reverse-engineering future earnings from a share price requires assumptions about discount rates, terminal value, normalized margins, net cash, non-memory earnings, and capital returns. The result from one combination is not the market's only expectation. A conditional stress test and the claim that “the worst case is already priced in” are different things.

## A longer boom needs evidence of Samsung gaining share itself

The paths below are not forecasts that fix a peak year or assign probabilities. They are criteria for assessing different combinations of demand and supply.

| Path | Required demand and supply conditions | Interpretation for Samsung |
|---|---|---|
| Base | AI installation growth moderates; CXMT's existing products and G5 penetrate gradually | Check whether premium-product growth offsets slower price increases |
| Upside | AI expansion backed by cash flow and Samsung customer approvals outpace supply growth | A longer boom and a Samsung share recovery can occur together |
| Downside | Delayed AI installations and inventory cuts coincide with more supply from China and incumbents | Pressure on price and volume, and higher fixed-cost burden. Assess HBM and NAND separately |

2027–2028 is not a confirmed peak or downturn period. The key is whether contracts, real utilization, inventory, and good-die supply confirm one of these paths.

Events that could change the assessment need to be judged alongside their prerequisites.

| Event | Prerequisites | First signal to check | Earnings path and failure risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Samsung expands HBM4E and custom memory | Customer approval, packaging yield, and delivery | Approval and repeat shipments after sampling; contract terms | Better mix and share. Risk of approval delays or price concessions |
| DDR5, SOCAMM2, and enterprise SSD grow | Platform adoption and real server demand | Server deliveries and product-level shipments | Demand across several memory layers. Risk of lower capacity per system |
| CXMT ships G5 at scale | Stable yield and customer qualification | Scale sales and repeat orders | Pressure on Samsung's channels and bargaining power. Cost or quality may miss targets |
| CXMT proves 4F² economics | Cell area, yield, power, and reliability met together | Independent analysis, customer validation, and multi-quarter shipments | Some manufacturing barriers weaken. Risk from bonding cost, defects, or throughput |
| AI investment improves post-investment cash flow | Paid usage and utilization | Cash collection and higher free cash flow | Supports longer demand. Risk of cancelled investment or higher financing costs |

Technical risks include yield, power, or delivery shortfalls in Samsung's next-generation products and faster-than-expected improvement in CXMT's good-die cost. Business risks include Chinese import substitution, stronger customer bargaining power, and inventory reduction. Financing costs, power availability, foreign exchange, and procurement restrictions may amplify these risks or split them across markets.

Five items need to be checked in the next quarters and half-years.

| Review timing | Indicator | Condition that changes the assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Samsung detailed results and contract renewals | Conventional DRAM and NAND prices and bit growth, inventory, and 2027 contracts | Heightened caution if prices rise while bits, inventory, or contract terms deteriorate |
| Late 2026 to 1H 2027 | CXMT G5 scale sales and repeat server orders | Raise near-term competition risk if it moves from mass production to commercial supply |
| After CXMT's 4F² announcement | Independent die analysis, yield, power, and temperature-dependent retention | Assess structure separately from economics and repeat shipments |
| AI customer quarterly results | Cash investment, operating cash flow, utilization, and contracts | Prioritize cash collection and installation delays over total investment |
| Each quarter | Samsung customer approvals and shipments of premium products | Raise sustainable-earnings view if share improves independently of price |

Samsung's response is not a binary choice between moving only to HBM and defending every bit of conventional DRAM share. It must maintain customer ties while allocating capital to products with the best risk-adjusted return. Expanding conventional capacity without adequate returns is not a solution.

The public evidence does not justify a full exit based on one CXMT announcement. Current-year earnings alone do not justify additional buying either. To retain the investment case, add evidence for this claim: <strong>“Memory is in short supply” needs to be joined by “Samsung's good-die cost, customer position, and product mix can protect earnings even as the shortage eases.”</strong>

The shareholder's remaining question is how much of today's cash flow can be returned, how much can be invested in the next competitive advantage, and how much will remain after prices normalize.

## Evidence scope and calculation assumptions

This article prioritizes Samsung's annual and quarterly disclosures and official conference materials. Third-quarter results are preliminary, and profits by HBM, conventional DRAM, and NAND are not disclosed. CXMT's corporate announcement is distinguished from a reprinted company filing. The full CXMT half-year financial report, 4F² economics, server deliveries, and Samsung's individual contract terms remain insufficiently verified.
