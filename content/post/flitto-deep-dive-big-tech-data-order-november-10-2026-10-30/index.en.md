In October 2025, a US big tech company sent Flitto an order for AI training data worth **4.23 billion won**. Over the next year the customer extended that order once and raised it five times. It now stands at **23.78 billion won ($16.9 million)**, 5.6 times the original. Flitto's stock went the other way. It closed at a high of 19,750 won on December 2, 2025, and traded at 8,740 won on the morning of October 30. That is **56%** below the high.

The filings show what the market is worried about. The contract's end date is November 10. But the filing says delivery was due by **September 26**, and November 10 is the deadline for payment, 45 days after delivery. The 12.63 billion won of data added on June 29 also had to be delivered by September 26. Third-quarter revenue rose 9.68 billion won from the second quarter, and operating profit of 8.97 billion won was a record. That jump overlaps the delivery window for the June 29 increase. The company does not report revenue by contract, so contract A's exact contribution cannot be confirmed. As of October 30, no new order big enough to repeat it in the fourth quarter had been filed.

**Our view:** the third-quarter profit will not repeat in the fourth quarter. But the filing record does not support calling the contract a one-off windfall either. This customer has ordered about every six months since September 2024. In December 2025, instead of closing this order, it extended the order by nine months and then kept adding volume. The AI training-data bottleneck supports demand for human-made multilingual data. So we think a next order is likely. We think it is unlikely to be as large as 2026. The same customer's other contract was cut 55% in October, and no autumn order has appeared yet. **The size of the next order will move the stock more than whether there is one.**

The first answer will likely come from the amendment filing around November 10. This customer's earlier orders each drew an amendment filing on their end date. The second comes from the third-quarter report, due by November 16, which will show the backlog at the end of September.

Written October 30, 2026. This is filing analysis, not a recommendation. The customer's name is withheld at its request to protect trade secrets. We assume that the "global IT company" in the contract filings and "US-based global big tech company A" in the half-year report are the same customer. ## Key points: the third quarter was a delivery quarter; the order book decides the next one

- **All of Flitto's disclosed 2026 data orders were increases to 2025 orders.** The customer added volume to its October 3, 2025 order five times, for a total of 19.55 billion won ($13.9 million).
- **November 10 is a payment deadline.** Delivery was due September 26. So the filing around November 10 is likely to fix the final contract amount.
- **Final amounts have changed before.** The customer's March 2025 order was cut 16% at its end date. In December 2025, contract A was extended instead, and it grew in 2026.
- **Profit follows data deliveries.** In 1Q26, revenue of about 5.2 billion won left operating profit near zero, and 78% of the revenue added from the second to the third quarter became operating profit.
- **Fourth-quarter revenue is about 10.8 billion won from filed contracts alone, and up to about 16.5 billion won by backlog arithmetic (our estimates).** The won has strengthened since September, which also shrinks the won value of the remaining dollar contracts.
- **The demand case is strong.** Public text is projected to run out between 2026 and 2032, and key web sources have started to block AI crawlers. Korean is 0.88% of Common Crawl pages.
- **Books are a stock that you scan once. Flitto sells a flow that it makes to order.** A US court held that scanning purchased books for training was fair use, and a pirated library led to a settlement of about $1.5 billion.

## 1. One purchase order grew 5.6x in a year

Contract A is an order received on October 3, 2025. The filing describes it as "data supply for the research and development of AI-based language models." The counterparty is a "global IT company." Payment is due within 45 days of delivery.

No new data order from this customer was filed in 2026. The ceiling on an existing order kept rising instead. That suits the customer: it can add languages and volume as it needs them without a new contract. It does not suit investors: nobody can tell in advance whether the next tranche will appear as a new contract filing or as an amendment.

## 2. November 10 is a payment deadline

The September 2 amendment explains how the contract period was set. The end date reflects "the contract term that payment is made within 45 days of delivery completion (September 26, 2026)." The June 29 filing gave the same delivery date. So the volume added on June 29 had to be delivered within three months. That timing fits the 9.68 billion won jump in third-quarter revenue over the second quarter.

What will the November 10 filing show? The customer's earlier orders show how its contracts end. Each of them drew an amendment filing on its end date, under continued confidential treatment that discloses only summary terms at the customer's request.

| Order | First filed value | Last value | What happened at the end |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| September 2024 order | 3.91bn won | 4.26bn won | Raised before the end, held at the end date |
| March 2025 order | 9.04bn won | 6.68bn won | Cut 16% at the end-date amendment, "final supply amount changed" (-26% vs first filing) |
| July 2025 order | 6.37bn won | 6.37bn won | Extended, value held |
| October 2025 contract A | 4.23bn won | 23.78bn won | End date pushed out nine months, raised five times, November 10 pending |
| October 2025 contract B | 3.88bn won | 4.03bn won | Raised to 8.89bn won in June, cut 55% on October 6, end date moved to 2027-02-09 |

Sources: [DART Aug 12, 2025](https://dart.fss.or.kr/dsaf001/main.do?rcpNo=20250812900088), [Nov 11, 2025](https://dart.fss.or.kr/dsaf001/main.do?rcpNo=20251111900394), [Nov 28, 2025](https://dart.fss.or.kr/dsaf001/main.do?rcpNo=20251128900653), [Oct 6, 2026](https://dart.fss.or.kr/dsaf001/main.do?rcpNo=20261006900642).

Based on those precedents, the filing around November 10 can be read like this:

- **Extension and increase:** the December 2025 precedent. The customer reopened the order instead of closing it. That would be the first signal of 2027 volume.
- **Settlement cut:** the March 2025 precedent. The final supply amount is fixed below the ceiling, so less volume is left in the contract. The filing alone cannot show why, or whether revenue already booked is revised.
- **Value held:** the July 2025 precedent. The contract ends at 23.78 billion won, and the next volume has to come from a new order.

Contract B has already sent a signal. On October 6 its value fell from 8.89 billion won to 4.03 billion won ($2.65 million), and its end date moved from November 10 to February 9, 2027. The new delivery date is December 26. The filing gives the reason only as "change in contract amount and period." We cannot tell whether the customer cut volume or moved it to a later contract.

## 4. The fourth quarter: between filed contracts and backlog arithmetic

The half-year report puts the data backlog at **28.31 billion won** on June 30. The report defines it as the amount of contracts not yet ended at the report date, converted at the report-date exchange rate. At the end of June, contracts A and B together came to $21.97 million, or about 34 billion won at the June-end rate of about 1,548 won per dollar. The backlog is smaller than that, so we read it as the amount not yet delivered.

The exchange rate matters too. The dollar fell from about 1,548 won on June 30 to about 1,358 won on September 30 and about 1,337 won on October 30, as the won strengthened. The won value of the remaining dollar contracts fell with it. So we did the fourth-quarter arithmetic in dollars. All of it is our estimate.

| Case | 4Q revenue | Calculation |
|---|---:|---|
| Filed contracts only | about 10.8bn won | Base business about 3.4bn won (first-half non-data revenue averaged 3.36bn won a quarter) + contract B up to $2.65M (about 3.5bn won) + contract C 3.87bn won. Assumes the contract B remainder and contract C are all booked in the fourth quarter |
| Backlog arithmetic | up to about 16.5bn won | June-end backlog about $18.3M + September contract A increase $0.8M - estimated 3Q data deliveries of $9.4M to $10.1M - October contract B cut $3.2M = about $5.8M to $6.5M (about 7.8 to 8.7bn won). Add 3.5 to 4.0bn won of base business and contract C |

The third-quarter data deliveries are third-quarter revenue minus 3.5 to 4.5 billion won of non-data revenue, converted at the third-quarter average rate of about 1,420 won per dollar. If part of the data revenue comes from won contracts, the numbers change.

The gap between the two is about 4.2 to 5.2 billion won ($3.2 million to $3.9 million) of data backlog that contract B cannot explain. It may be contract A volume that missed the September 26 delivery date. It may also be another customer's data contract. This figure comes from our arithmetic; it is not a confirmed backlog. The September-end backlog in the third-quarter report is the first thing to check it against.

Contract C is a solution build, not a data sale. On October 8, Flitto signed a 3.87 billion won contract with a "global semiconductor company" to build an AI real-time interpretation system. It runs to December 30, and the value combines a solution license with system-integration labor. The filing says Flitto had no similar contract with this counterparty in the past three years. Because it is a build project with labor costs, its margin may be lower than data sales. Source: [DART Oct 12, 2026](https://dart.fss.or.kr/dsaf001/main.do?rcpNo=20261012900229).

If operating profit is (revenue - 5.2 billion won) x 0.7, fourth-quarter operating profit is about 3.9 to 8.0 billion won. The 5.2 billion won is 1Q26 breakeven revenue, and the 0.7 is the third-quarter incremental margin of 78%, cut for caution. Neither is a tested model. On these assumptions, 2026 operating profit is about 14.2 to 18.3 billion won, and the market value of about 144.5 billion won (October 30, intraday) is 8 to 10 times that. At the end of June, cash and financial assets were about 18.8 billion won against about 0.1 billion won of debt (half-year report, our calculation). No filed contract yet carries volume for delivery in 2027. Even contract B finishes delivery on December 26. The stock sits between 2026 profit and a 2027 that is still empty.

## 5. Demand: the human writing that AI learns from is running short

Why the customer buys this data is the case for a repeat order.

**Public text is finite.** Epoch AI estimates the stock of quality- and repetition-adjusted public human text at about 300 trillion tokens (90% interval: 100 to 1,000 trillion). At current trends it will be fully used between 2026 and 2032 (80% interval), or earlier with heavy overtraining. [Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/blog/will-we-run-out-of-data-limits-of-llm-scaling-based-on-human-generated-data)

**Usable text is shrinking faster.** Researchers at the Data Provenance Initiative reported that in one year, from 2023 to 2024, more than 5% of all tokens in the C4 dataset, and more than 28% of its most actively maintained critical sources, became fully restricted for AI crawling. By terms of service, 45% of C4 was restricted. [Consent in Crisis, arXiv 2407.14933](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14933)

**AI-made text is a poor substitute.** Shumailov and co-authors showed that when each model generation trains on the previous generation's output, the tails of the original distribution disappear. In runs that kept 10% of the original data, performance degraded only slightly. Human-made data slows the collapse. [Nature, 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07566-y)

**Korean was scarce from the start.** In Common Crawl's latest crawl (CC-MAIN-2026-43), Korean is 0.88% of pages and English is 42.6%. [Common Crawl statistics](https://commoncrawl.github.io/cc-crawl-statistics/plots/languages) In Meta's Llama 2 pretraining data, Korean was 0.06%. [Llama 2 paper, Table 10](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288) The rare language pairs Flitto highlights, such as English-Arabic, specialist fields such as medicine and law, and generational slang are even harder to find on the web.

## 6. Books are a stock; Flitto sells a flow

The same bottleneck explains why AI companies buy and scan print books. On June 23, 2025, Judge William Alsup of the US District Court for the Northern District of California split his ruling in Bartz v. Anthropic. Training a model on lawfully purchased books was fair use. Destroying purchased print originals and scanning them into digital copies was also fair use, because the number of copies did not grow. Keeping millions of books downloaded from pirate sites in a central library was not fair use. Anthropic agreed to a settlement of about $1.5 billion (about $3,000 per work), and the court granted preliminary approval on September 25, 2025. [Goodwin analysis of the ruling](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/06/alerts-practices-aiml-district-court-issues-ai-fair-use-decision), [Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/print/20250929/98706-judge-gives-preliminary-approval-to-anthropic-settlement.html) It is a district court ruling and does not bind other courts.

The ruling shows the economics of data supply. Books are a stock of text written in the past. You scan them once, and no new ones appear. Flitto sells something else. On its own platform, Arcade, it pays participants in points to complete language missions, and so produces new data to order in the languages and formats a customer wants. The half-year report lists the data types as text and voice. The company names multilingual data checked by both machine translation and professional translators as its edge.

That difference is the logic of repeat orders. You buy a stock once. You buy a flow again each time a model needs a new language, a new feature or a new evaluation standard. The customer's behavior over the past year, raising the ceiling instead of closing the order, fits that logic.

## 7. The other side: a bottleneck is not the same as Flitto's volume

- **There is one customer.** The half-year report says US big tech company A is more than 10% of revenue. Both 2026 data contracts, A and B, name a "global IT company" as the counterparty, and we take it to be company A. If its data roadmap (languages, formats, timing) changes, volume falls whatever the bottleneck does.
- **The size signals have weakened.** Contract B was cut 55% from its peak on October 6. A new order arrived each autumn in 2024 and 2025, but none for autumn 2026 had been filed by October 30.
- **Small orders show up late.** The disclosure threshold is 10% of the prior year's revenue, now about 3.6 billion won. Contract B was first filed six months after the order, when an increase pushed it over the threshold. No filing does not mean no order. But when a filing comes, it can lag the order by months.
- **$16.9 million is small money for big tech.** We think the next order depends on the customer's data needs more than on its budget. The customer can also split volume with other data suppliers.
