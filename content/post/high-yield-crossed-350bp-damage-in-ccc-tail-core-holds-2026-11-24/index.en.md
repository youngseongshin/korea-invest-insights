On November 14 we wrote that AI corporate bonds were barely charging for risk. We named the first signals that would change that view: a high-yield spread of 350bp and an AA spread of 75bp.

On November 19 the US high-yield spread closed at 352bp, past that line. On November 20 it reached 376bp. The same day the S&P 500 stood 11.6% below its October 6 peak and the VIX closed at 35.89. Credit has started to charge.

Break the index down by rating, though, and the charge lands in a narrow place. CCC and lower bonds trade at a 1,402bp spread, 265bp above the April 2025 tariff shock. BB and B, the body of the high-yield index, are 77 to 78bp below that peak.

<strong>Our view: over the four weeks to Micron's results on December 18, this widening is the weakest borrowers being repriced. We do not read it as the start of a broad credit cycle. The next lines that would make us look again are an AA spread of 75bp (72bp on November 20) and a high-yield spread that holds above 400bp for five trading days.</strong>

- A 376bp high-yield spread is in the top 4% of the last three years. Those three years were a period of unusually tight spreads, and 376bp is still 85bp below the 461bp tariff-shock peak.
- The gap between CCC and BB spreads is 1,173bp, the widest in three years. CCC spreads have widened 564bp since late January, ahead of the stock sell-off.
- Of the 254 days since 2010 with a VIX of 30 or more, November 20 had the lowest Baa corporate spread (1.61 points). Credit was barely startled by equity volatility.
- On November 23, when the S&P 500 rose 4.7%, the high-yield spread narrowed by only about 5bp on our estimate. Credit moved far less than stocks on the way down and barely at all on the way up.
- Next checks: the official spreads for November 23 to 25, the credit reaction to the December 10 FOMC, and the AA spread against 75bp by December 18.

## High yield crossed 350bp but is still 85bp below last April's peak

The high-yield spread widened 110bp in two months, from 266bp on September 21. It widened 53bp in the five trading days from November 13 to 20, the eighth-fastest move of the last three years. Only the August 2024 yen carry unwind and the April 2025 tariff shock were faster.

![Daily US high-yield OAS from November 24, 2023 to November 20, 2026. Three-year median 3.02%, low of 2.59% on January 22, 2025, tariff-shock peak of 4.61% on April 7, 2025, 3.76% on November 20, 2026, and an estimated 3.71% on November 23.](/post-assets/high-yield-crossed-350bp-damage-in-ccc-tail-core-holds-2026-11-24/hy-oas-3y.en.png)

"Top 4% of three years" sounds heavy, but the window matters. FRED, the St. Louis Fed's database, publishes the ICE high-yield indexes for the last three years only. Over a long history, the average spread of those three years sits on the low side (see the third section).

## The damage sits in CCC bonds

In April 2025, BB, B and CCC spreads all peaked on the same day. This time only CCC has crossed that line.

![Three panels of option-adjusted spreads by rating. BB at 2.29% on November 20, 77bp below 3.06% on April 7, 2025. B at 4.08%, 78bp below 4.86%. CCC and lower at 14.02%, 265bp above 11.37%.](/post-assets/high-yield-crossed-350bp-damage-in-ccc-tail-core-holds-2026-11-24/hy-ratings-small-multiples.en.png)

| November 20 | Level | Rank in 3 years | vs April 7, 2025 | vs September 21 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| High yield, all | 376bp | top 4% | -85bp | +110bp |
| BB | 229bp | top 5% | -77bp | +76bp |
| B | 408bp | top 2% | -78bp | +138bp |
| CCC and lower | 1,402bp | highest | +265bp | +325bp |
| Investment grade, all | 98bp | top 15% | -22bp | +21bp |
| AA | 72bp | last year's peak 73bp | -1bp | - |
| High-yield total yield | 8.46% | top 1% | -5bp | - |

The table is about the distance between the tail and the body. CCC spreads went from 838bp on January 28 to 1,402bp, up 564bp in ten months, and the CCC-BB gap of 1,173bp is the widest in three years. Risk is piling up in the weakest layer, not across the index.

The tail itself is concentrated. Vanguard said on November 10 that four of more than 150 CCC issuers account for more than 30% of CCC spreads, many of them in telecom and media. CCC bonds are less than 10% of the high-yield market. On the AI side, CoreWeave's five-year credit default swap was reported at 1,100 to 1,200bp, with its 2031 bond yielding close to 16% (CNBC, November 18).

## On days with VIX above 30, credit is the calmest since 2010

The long history of the high-yield index is not public, so we used the gap between Moody's Baa corporate yield and the 10-year Treasury, published without a break since 1990. Baa is the bottom rung of investment grade, so its level differs from high yield, but daily changes in the two series correlated 0.64 over the last three years.

![Scatter of daily VIX against the Baa minus 10-year spread, 1990 to 2026. In December 2008, March 2020, October 2011 and April 8, 2025, high VIX came with high spreads. November 20, 2026 sits alone near the bottom, with a VIX of 35.9 and a spread of 1.61 points.](/post-assets/high-yield-crossed-350bp-damage-in-ccc-tail-core-holds-2026-11-24/vix-vs-baa-1990.en.png)

On the 254 days since 2010 when the VIX closed at 30 or higher, the median Baa spread was 2.99 points. November 20 was the lowest of all 254, though by a narrow margin: the next lowest was 1.63 points during the February 2018 volatility spike. Going back to 1990, August 1990 had lower readings. The stock market showed near-panic volatility while the credit market held close to normal prices.

## Stocks recovered 4.7% in a day, and credit barely moved

On November 20 the S&P 500 fell 3.7% and the Nasdaq 5.2%. Reports described a breakdown in the AI trade and a 14% fall in Nvidia, alongside news of its CEO's departure (Investopedia). On November 23, Nvidia rose 31% as reports said its board had brought Jensen Huang back (Yahoo Finance). The S&P 500 closed 4.7% higher.

Credit did move on the way down. The high-yield spread widened 24bp on November 20 alone. That was still small next to the fall in stocks, and on the rebound day credit barely came back.

![Drawdown from the most recent peak since late August, shown from September 1 to November 23, 2026. The S&P 500 ETF (SPY) went from -11.5% on November 20 to -7.3% on November 23; the BDC ETF was at -7.1%, the high-yield bond ETF at -2.8% and the leveraged loan ETF at -0.5%.](/post-assets/high-yield-crossed-350bp-damage-in-ccc-tail-core-holds-2026-11-24/drawdown-equity-vs-credit.en.png)

The high-yield bond ETF (HYG) rose only 0.25% on the rebound day. The official November 23 spread is not yet published, so we estimated it from three months of HYG returns and 10-year yield changes. The estimate is about 371bp (range 362 to 380bp), about 5bp tighter on the day. The leveraged loan ETF slipped slightly. Stocks reacted to news about Nvidia's leadership; credit appears to have read that news as unrelated to anyone's ability to pay interest.

## Tightening moved from rates to the risk premium

| Measure | November 13 | November 20 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1-year Treasury minus SOFR | 75bp | 59bp | -16bp |
| 10-year Treasury | 5.15% | 4.92% | -23bp |
| High-yield spread | 323bp | 376bp | +53bp |
| High-yield total yield | 8.20% | 8.46% | +26bp |

In the second week of November, tightening came through higher real rates, and high-yield spreads actually narrowed. In the third week the direction flipped. Treasury yields fell, and so did the gap between the 1-year Treasury yield and SOFR, a rough gauge of expected rate hikes. The risk premium took their place. The price of money fell while the price of risk rose.

## Why the body holds and the tail breaks

More than 90% of the high-yield index is BB and B. A total yield in the 8% range is an attractive price for insurers, pension funds and income funds that buy on yield. On November 20, the 10-year Treasury yield fell 12bp while the spread widened 24bp, so the total yield rose only 11bp. When the total yield barely moves, that buying tends to stay. This reading is an inference; we could not confirm it with fund-flow data.

CCC borrowers are in a different position. They must refinance at market rates. A 10-year Treasury near 5% plus a 14% CCC spread puts new borrowing costs near 19%. Companies whose cash flow cannot carry that interest are the first to be marked down.

The same frame explains why stocks and credit parted ways. The November 20 shock came from Nvidia's leadership and AI valuations. That news does not change most companies' ability to pay interest. So it is consistent that credit moved much less than stocks on the way down, and that it did not come back with them.

## Where this reaches AI capex and memory

Hyperscalers fund capital spending from operating cash flow and investment-grade bonds. The AA spread, where those bonds trade, is 72bp, below the 75bp line we set. If credit starts to cut AI spending, the path opens first at marginal buyers that borrow to buy GPUs, such as neoclouds, not at the hyperscalers themselves.

When marginal buyers pay more to borrow, the edge of incremental GPU and high-bandwidth memory (HBM) orders for 2027 and 2028 gets trimmed. Hyperscaler volumes move separately from that path. So to read memory demand early, neocloud bond prices and the AA spread are more useful than the high-yield index as a whole.

For stocks, today's credit picture looks like the 2022 kind of drawdown, where prices fell hard while earnings and capex held. Moving to a 2000-style collapse would take high yield holding above 400bp for five trading days, together with capex guidance cuts from the large cloud companies.

## The case against us: in 2007 the tail went first too

The strongest counterexample to our view is 2007. Then the weak tail of high yield widened first, and the body and stocks followed months later. The steady widening of CCC spreads since January looks like that leading pattern.

If that case is right, the November 23 rally is a bounce inside a bear market, and today's quiet BB and AA spreads will widen within weeks. That is why we watch the space between the tail and the body more than the tail itself. AA at 75bp is the first checkpoint.

## Data notes

- High-yield and rating spreads are option-adjusted spreads (OAS) of the ICE BofA indexes, taken from FRED. FRED publishes these series for the last three years only, so we could not compare the 2008 and 2020 high-yield peaks on the same measure. For the long view we used the gap between Moody's Baa corporate yield and the 10-year Treasury.
- The November 23 high-yield spread is our estimate. We regressed daily spread changes on HYG returns and 10-year yield changes over 62 observations in the last three months (R² 0.65, residual standard deviation 4.4bp). In stress, ETF prices can drift from the underlying bonds.
- CCC levels differ by index provider. The roughly 1,000bp Vanguard cited on November 10 and the ICE reading of about 1,300bp at the same time appear to reflect different index definitions.
- The causes of the November 20 and 23 moves come from press reports. We did not obtain primary data for high-yield fund flows, CoreWeave bond prices or large-tech CDS.
- "Rank in 3 years" is the share of days from November 24, 2023 to November 20, 2026 with a lower reading than November 20.
