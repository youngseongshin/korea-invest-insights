Altman, Zuckerberg, Bezos, Gates, Thiel, and Khosla have invested in 94 companies across biotech, longevity, and medical technology in the list reviewed for this analysis. Only 10 of them currently trade under their own tickers. The rest are private, have been absorbed by other companies, or have disappeared.

For investors looking at listed stocks, one question remains: How does private-company funding reach the results of listed companies, and how is the stock market valuing that today? Aging and brain interfaces attracted the most money, yet have virtually no listed proxies. Where the money has reached listed companies, acquisition prices and revenue multiples have moved before operating results. Here is the conclusion first.

<strong>Money invested in private biotech reached listed stocks first through acquisitions and narratives. It has not yet been confirmed in operating results. Third-quarter results and FDA decisions are the first test of whether those narratives turn into revenue and approval language. When interest rates are rising, cash burn determines the downside for companies that fail this test.</strong>

- The areas that attracted the most money have the fewest listed proxies. Of the 94 companies, exposure through listed stocks can be confirmed for only 10. We found no listed company generating revenue from brain interfaces. Clinical trials at listed companies focused on aging do not use aging itself as an indication.
- The clearest route for money to reach listed markets is through acquisitions. IQVIA counted 42 biopharma acquisitions in the first half of 2026, worth $130 billion. Ten of the 13 companies Lilly has acquired this year were private.
- In U.S.-listed stocks, narratives have shown up in multiples. Revenue at three diagnostics companies grew 26% to 44%, and their multiples range from 19x to 33x. Large-cap tools companies grew revenue 5% to 10% and trade at 6x to 9.5x. Revenue at AI drug-discovery platforms fell 38% to 76%, but clinical events drove divergent share-price performance.
- Interest rates have changed the market baseline. XBI is up 26.1% this year but down 6.4% over the past three months. The 10-year Treasury yield rose from 4.18% at the start of the year to 5.24%. Late-stage assets trade at 1x to 3x cash, while narrative-driven stocks trade at 4x to 13x.
- In Korea, Samsung Biologics and ST Pharm are the only companies with overseas contracts confirmed through disclosures and positive first-half results. Cell and lab-automation companies with overseas evidence reported first-half revenue in the KRW 500 million to KRW 4.6 billion range, and all posted losses.

The analysis date for private investment events is Oct 9, 2026. U.S. share prices are closing prices from Oct 9. Results for U.S.-listed companies are based on their second-quarter 2026 reports. Korean disclosures and news were checked on Oct 10. Korean markets were closed on Hangul Day, Oct 9. Price returns exclude dividends. Secondary sources and our own calculations are identified as such in the main text.

## 94 Companies Focus on Drug Discovery and Cell Therapy, but Each Leader Backs Different Themes

The list of 94 includes biotech, longevity, and medical technology companies funded or founded by tech leaders, along with historical ventures, with no duplicates. Of these, 90 have an investment or founding connection to an individual, an affiliated fund, or a foundation. That does not mean all 90 were direct personal investments, or that the list contains only private companies that are still active.

The other four are not counted as personal investments. Spring Discovery is linked to Altman only in a supplementary investment database, so its attribution needs further verification. Calico, founded by Google, was a corporate venture and is not counted as a personal investment by Larry Page. Ambience Healthcare and Thrive AI Health, which the OpenAI Startup Fund invested in or co-launched, are included only as comparisons for affiliated capital.[^a-calico][^a-ambience]

Personal investments, affiliated investment firms and funds, foundation equity investments, corporate investments, and donations are different kinds of money. The long-term preferences of wealthy individuals, the return targets of venture funds with fixed terms, and the public-benefit conditions of foundations may differ. For this reason, SIF, the Gates Foundation’s equity investment vehicle, is counted separately from Bill Gates personally. Investments by the OpenAI Startup Fund are also not treated as Altman’s personal investments.[^a-oaifund]

I did not aggregate or calculate total investment amounts, company valuations, or investment success rates. Individual contributions are mostly undisclosed, and publicly reported cases are subject to selection bias. Unless stated otherwise, the amounts in this article refer to the total size of a company’s funding round.

I verified 81 connections using event-specific primary sources from companies, investors, or disclosures, or official portfolio lists. Another 12 rely in whole or in part on media reporting, and one was verified only in a supplementary database. These evidence grades show how firmly an investment connection was verified. They are not scores for technology, clinical, or financial quality. An appearance on an official list does not prove current ownership, the investment date, or the amount invested.

## Aging Attracts the Most Capital, but No One Has Reached the Human Functional Outcomes Stage

A reduction in epigenetic age, changes in cell function, effects in animals, human safety, improved human function, and lower disease incidence and mortality are separate validation stages. Counting success at an earlier stage as success at a later one inflates valuations. The ladder figure answers how far aging and reprogramming companies have progressed based on public announcements.

Altman’s initial investment of $180 million was reported, and a pre-money valuation of $1.8 billion was disclosed at the first close of a follow-on round in May 2026. RTR242 entering Phase 1 does not prove reprogramming or healthspan extension.[^a-retro-altman][^a-retro]

NewLimit, co-founded by Brian Armstrong, raised $435 million in a 2026 Series C, but its first human trial is planned for 2027. Altos Labs announced a $3 billion commitment at its launch in 2022, but this research did not confirm any publicly disclosed clinical trials. Unity reached human trials but dissolved in September 2025. This research found no one at the stages of improved human function or reduced disease incidence and mortality. Loyal, which works with dogs, is not placed on the human ladder.

The judgment that aging research is wrong differs from the judgment that current prices already assume success. Investors may want to look first at companies that can explain which function they aim to improve in which patients, rather than companies that first promise people a longer life.

The specific disease programs on the lower row of the ladder can define clear comparables and metrics. Aspen Neuroscience aims to replace Parkinson’s disease neurons using stem cells made from a patient’s own cells (autologous iPSCs), while Lifemine’s LIFE-001 targets organ transplant rejection. Neuralink and Synchron’s efforts to restore function in people with severe paralysis, and NewLimit’s liver program, belong in the same group.[^a-lifemine]

## Widely reported figures often describe neither capital raised nor personal investment nor clinical results

Retro’s $1.8 billion figure is its pre-money valuation, not the amount raised. Taking the largest number in a report at face value can lead investors to misread cash runway and the stage of validation. The table below ranks the figures corrected in this research by how easily investors could misread them.

| Figure shown | Company and date | What it actually means | Misreading it could lead to |
|---:|---|---|---|
| $1.8 billion | Retro, 2026-05-22 | Pre-money valuation. Amount raised undisclosed; first close of a follow-on round | Treating the valuation as capital raised and overstating cash runway |
| $3 billion | Altos Labs, launched in 2022 | Total committed capital. Not Bezos’s personal investment | Confusing commitments with deployment, or an individual with the whole fund, and misattributing the amount |
| $263 million | LifeMine, announced in 2026-08 | The combined total of a $75 million Series D and a $188 million Series E | Treating them as one round and missing the differences in participation timing and price |
| 75% | Neko Health, 2026-07 | The share of customers who booked and prepaid for another test after their first test | Mistaking it for the actual return rate the following year or long-term retention |
| Positive advisory committee view | GRAIL, 2026-09-23 | An FDA advisory committee assessment, not final approval | Reading only the favorable development and omitting the missed primary endpoint earlier that year |
| 37 | Khosla Ventures portfolio list | Investment links confirmed in the official portfolio | Mistaking these for Khosla’s personal investments, current holdings, or clinical validation |
| $650 million | Neuralink, 2025-06-02 | A company-wide funding round. Its goal is to restore function for people with severe paralysis | Stretching it into evidence for a rejuvenation or longevity drug portfolio |

LifeMine’s Series D closed in Q4 2025, and its Series E closed in July 2026. Bezos- and Gates-related investment firms joined in the E round. Even when investors back the same company, different participation dates and prices make them different bets.[^a-lifemine]

Neko Health is a screening company that combines its own sensors, blood tests, and physician consultations. It is not a whole-body MRI company. Its 75% figure may signal strong demand, but whether the service is medically useful requires separate validation. For GRAIL, the analysis should also follow the burden of tests and procedures after a finding, why insurers would pay, and whether that translates into company profits.[^a-neko]

What changes for investors is the order in which they read the figures. First determine whether an amount refers to capital raised, valuation, or commitments. For a percentage, check whether it means a booking or an actual return visit. For a regulatory event, check whether it is an advisory opinion or an approval.

Juno Therapeutics was acquired by Celgene in March 2018, but the actual exit returns for Bezos Expeditions have not been confirmed.[^a-juno]

Kymab, in which the Gates Foundation Strategic Investment Fund (SIF) held an equity stake, was acquired by Sanofi in 2021. Affinivax was acquired by GSK in 2022. The foundation’s public-benefit conditions may differ from commercial investors’ objectives.[^a-kymab][^a-affinivax]

Stemcentrx, backed by Peter Thiel’s Founders Fund, was sold to AbbVie. Development of its lead asset, Rova-T, was discontinued in 2019. VC exit and the acquirer’s clinical and business performance are different questions.[^a-stemcentrx][^a-rovat]

All cases involved successful financing, while only the acquisition cases involved exits. Clinical or product success and patient benefit must mostly be assessed separately. This sample was not used to calculate the success rate of biotech investing or the investment ability of famous people. An acquisition announcement means only that an exit path emerged. It is not evidence that the acquired asset will succeed clinically.

## Venture investors can investigate experimental automation and proprietary data first

Somite, Cellino, and Vivodyne are companies that connect AI models to cell production and experiments. The reason to look at the left side of the map first is that as hypothesis generation becomes easier, the value of judgment data and production capacity rises. The order below is not a ranking of investment attractiveness. It is the order in which research time is likely to improve judgment the most.

We did not obtain valuations, shareholder registers, cash balances, or original contracts. Therefore, this order is not an investment recommendation for any individual company.

| Order | Type | Representative companies | Evidence to check first |
|---|---|---|---|
| Priority | Experiment and cell automation | Somite, Cellino, Vivodyne, Opentrons | Reproducibility verified by external customers, batch yield and cost, recurring revenue |
| Priority | Proprietary experimental data | Gordian, Relation, insitro | Commercial usage rights, causal links between interventions and disease responses, predictive power on external data |
| Priority | Molecular design | Profluent, Atomic AI | Experimental success rate versus the model alone, delivery, manufacturing, and safety, contract cash |
| Priority | Clinical workflows and diagnostics | Abridge, Rad AI, Ambience, Karius | Simultaneous improvement in time, errors, medical costs, and paid renewals |
| Conditional | Medical devices | HistoSonics, Synchron | Function and safety by indication, usage after installation, procedure and reimbursement revenue |
| Conditional | Preventive care | Neko, Grail, Viome | Actual long-term retention, follow-up costs after false positives, benefit versus control groups |
| Long-term watch | Aging and organ preservation | Retro, NewLimit, Altos, Until, eGenesis | Improvement in human function, long-term safety, funding through the next validation milestone |

Compare cell and experiment automation on the same basis: target-cell production rate, batch failure rate, manufacturing time, quality-testing cost, freezing and thawing losses, and cost per dose. Do not evaluate automation built for one customer as a repeatable sales platform.

Break down “platform” revenue into software subscriptions, contract research, co-development, upfront payments, conditional milestones, and royalties. Cash already received, cash that may be received, and the maximum amount payable only upon ultimate success carry different risks.

Companies in the long-term watch category also require an accurate reading of their business. NewLimit is developing therapies that alter the epigenetic state of cells. Until Labs is developing cryopreservation and rewarming technology for transplant organs. Reading Until as a business for freezing and reviving entire people would miss its actual development task.[^a-until]

The evidence that would change the assessment is not additional participation by famous investors. The assessment should change when independent effects in humans, repeatable production, actual contract cash, and funding durability are demonstrated. Applying the same criteria to public stocks reveals how money concentrated in private companies reaches listed companies.

## Private Money Reached Listed Stocks First Through Acquisitions, and the Path Narrows in Hotter Themes

Of the 94 companies on the list, 10 currently trade under their own tickers. The rest are private, have been absorbed by other companies, or have disappeared. For listed-equity investors, the remaining question is how this money reaches listed-company results.

### The Clearest Path Is Through Acquisitions and Licensing by Large Companies

The events completed or announced between October 2025 and October 2026 trace the paths shown in the figure. Private themes reach listed-company results through acquisitions and licensing, supply chains, competition, listings, and regulation and coverage.

<div class="fig-scroll" style="overflow-x:auto;-webkit-overflow-scrolling:touch;margin:1.4em 0"><img src="/post-assets/tech-leaders-biotech-private-money-to-listed-stocks-us-korea-2026-10-10/paths.en.svg" alt="Paths from private themes to listed-company results" loading="lazy" style="display:block;width:100%;min-width:720px;max-width:none;height:auto;margin:0 auto"></div>

Even within the same theme, the numbers investors need to check depend on the path. For acquisitions, they should look at the asset's share of the large company's revenue. For listings, they should look at the new ticker's cash burn.

Acquisitions and licensing provide the clearest evidence that private-company value has transferred to a listed company. Abbott completed its acquisition of Exact Sciences in March 2026.[^g-abbott-exact] Lilly completed its acquisition of AtaiBeckley in September.[^g-lilly-atai] Along this path, the value created at the private-company stage is absorbed into the large company's results, and any single asset accounts for only a small share.

Along the supply-chain path, equipment and reagents used by private companies are recorded as revenue by listed suppliers. Spending by lab automation and cell-data companies flows to equipment and reagent companies such as Thermo Fisher, Danaher, and Tecan. In consumer testing, Quest Diagnostics runs blood tests for the WHOOP app.[^g-quest-whoop] However, this revenue is diluted within the larger company, and Quest does not disclose the share of revenue from consumer channels.

Along the competition path, established players offer the same functions directly. Private capital has flowed to companies such as Abridge, which uses AI to transcribe clinical conversations. But electronic health record company Epic launched its own AI documentation feature in February 2026.[^g-epic] Microsoft said that more than 100,000 clinicians use Dragon Copilot daily.[^g-msft]

The listing path first opened with comparable companies. Hinge Health listed in the United States in May 2025.[^g-hinge-ipo] Protein-design AI company Generate Biomedicines listed on Nasdaq in February 2026.[^g-generate-ipo] This created the closest listed comparable for private protein-design AI companies.

Freenome is the only company on the list of 94 to have listed recently. It listed on Nasdaq on July 20, 2026, through a SPAC merger.[^g-freenome-spac] One week after listing, its blood test for colorectal cancer received FDA approval, but Abbott has exclusive rights to sell it in the United States.[^g-freenome-fda]

The regulation and coverage path is still pending, so it is shown with a dashed line in the figure. The Medicare MCED law, signed in February 2026, opens the way to coverage starting in 2028 for tests that receive FDA approval.[^g-mced-law] GRAIL's Galleri received more favorable than unfavorable votes at an FDA advisory committee meeting on September 23, but the date of a final decision has not been made public.[^g-grail-adcom]

There is also a case where coverage has taken effect. Guardant Health's Shield blood test for colorectal cancer was added to the American Cancer Society's screening guidelines in May 2026. In July, UnitedHealth announced that it would cover the test.[^g-shield-uhc]

Whichever path a company takes, converting a private-market valuation into revenue for a listed company takes time. Takeda acquired Nimbus's TYK2 inhibitor in 2023, and the drug entered priority review for an FDA new drug application only in September 2026.[^g-takeda-nda] The PDUFA target action date is in the first quarter of 2027. This is why news of a private funding round is difficult to read directly as a signal of listed-company results.

### The Bigger the Money Flowing Into a Theme, the Fewer Listed Proxies There Are

We found no listed company that generates revenue solely from brain-computer interfaces (BCIs). That means there is no listed proxy: no listed company earning revenue from the same area as the private theme. Among listed companies, ONWARD Medical, traded on the Belgian exchange, is the only one conducting human BCI trials, and its BCI revenue is zero.[^g-onward]

Funding on the private side is substantial. Synchron raised $200 million in a Series D round in November 2025.[^g-synchron] We found no BCI company among the 94 on the list, including Neuralink and Synchron, that had announced a listing or SPAC merger.

The listed-company side is also empty for the aging theme. The drug BioAge Labs is testing is an NLRP3 inhibitor targeting cardiovascular inflammation, despite the company's focus on aging biology.[^g-bioage] Longeveron, which started with a focus on treating frailty, failed to meet its primary endpoint in a pediatric heart disease trial on September 16, 2026, and began reviewing strategic alternatives.[^g-longeveron]

Xenotransplantation, the transfer of organs from other species into humans, has listed and private companies at the same starting line. United Therapeutics' xenotransplantation kidney, UKidney, directly competes with the kidney developed by private company eGenesis. Both companies have zero xenotransplantation revenue, and most of United Therapeutics' revenue comes from pulmonary arterial hypertension drugs.[^g-uthr]

Consumer preventive screening shows the same pattern. Neko Health raised $700 million in July 2026, but we found no listed company offering the same kind of full-body scan.[^g-neko] Smart-ring company Oura delayed its schedule shortly before its listing after making its registration statement public in September. The delay was reported by a single outlet, and we could not confirm it against a company source.[^g-oura]

For the private value in these themes to flow into listed markets, an acquisition or listing would need to follow initial human results. This is an inference from this analysis. The judgment about which themes have attracted large amounts of money also relies on disclosed funding-round amounts; it is not based on a sum of total investment.

## U.S.-Listed Markets Priced Private-Market Money First Through Acquisition Prices and Narrative Multiples

We gathered 2026 second-quarter results and Oct 9 closing prices for about 60 U.S.-listed biotech and medical technology companies. We reviewed quarterly reports filed with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) and company earnings materials. All stock returns in this section are price returns excluding dividends.

In 2026, traces of private-market money in U.S.-listed biotech stocks appeared first in acquisition prices and high revenue multiples, before they appeared in earnings. The first test of whether those traces reach revenue and regulatory language will come with third-quarter results and FDA decisions in late October.

### Biotech Outperformed the Market This Year, but Lagged Over the Past Three Months as Rates Rose

Over the past three months, XBI fell 6.4% while SPY gained 3.6%. Large-cap-focused IBB also gained 4.6%, so the weakness was concentrated in XBI, which weights stocks equally, and therefore in small- and mid-cap biotech.

The figure below plots second-quarter revenue growth on the horizontal axis and this multiple on the vertical axis to show how the market values growth rates. Recursion (RXRX) was excluded because its revenue fell 60% and its 32.9x multiple put it outside the axis range.

At the same growth rate, multiples vary by several times across segments, so choosing comparables based only on growth rates produces a mismatch.

Second-quarter revenue grew 5~10% at Thermo Fisher (TMO), Danaher (DHR), and Illumina (ILMN), with multiples of 6~9.5x[^u-tmo]. Large tools companies cluster near the center of the figure, where growth rates and multiples are not far apart.

Second-quarter revenue grew 26~44% at GRAIL (GRAL), Natera (NTRA), and Guardant Health (GH), with multiples of 19~33x. Diagnostics is a segment with both high growth and high multiples. GRAIL has the highest multiple of the three, despite an operating loss about four times its revenue[^u-grail].

On Sep 23, an FDA advisory committee voted 7 to 2 that the benefits of GRAIL's Galleri multi-cancer early detection blood test outweigh its risks[^u-grail-adcomm]. The remaining step is the FDA's premarket approval (PMA) decision, for which no date has been announced. The FDA directly asked the advisory committee whether performance supports the "early detection" label, and the test's sensitivity for detecting Stage 1 cancers is 13.6~21.4%[^u-fda-grail].

If approved, Medicare, the U.S. public health insurance program for older adults, could provide coverage starting in 2028. However, the reimbursement benchmark is about $509, based on a figure in Freenome's (FRNM) listing documents[^u-freenome], while GRAIL's current revenue per test is about $699 (our calculation). This analysis judges that the "early" wording in the approval label and the launch of competing products will determine the diagnostic multiple more than the approval decision itself.

Hinge Health (HNGE) grew second-quarter revenue 53%, but its multiple is 10.9x[^u-hinge]. Omada Health (OMDA) has a 3.5x multiple[^u-omada]. Both companies have growth rates similar to or higher than those of the three diagnostics companies, but their multiples are much lower.

Hinge's second-quarter operating margin is 19% under U.S. generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP), while Omada's is 4%. Omada's membership grew sharply, but revenue per member rose only 2% over the past year. Both companies went public in 2025, and Hinge, with its higher margin, has gained much more than Omada relative to its IPO price.

Insurer Oscar Health (OSCR) grew its membership 46% year over year[^u-oscar]. However, second-quarter earnings included a one-time gain of $164 million from releasing reserves set aside in prior periods. The company's earnings power after enhanced Affordable Care Act (ACA) subsidies end in late 2025 needs to be reassessed during the 2027 enrollment period, which typically begins on Nov 1.

10x Genomics (TXG) saw second-quarter revenue fall 12.6%, but its multiple is 16.1x[^u-txg]. Excluding the patent settlement received in the same quarter last year, growth was still 3%. Instrument revenue fell sharply, and the company said customers delayed purchases while waiting for Atera, its new spatial biology instrument.

Twist Bioscience (TWST) has an operating margin of −31% and a multiple of 23.4x[^u-twist]. Revenue grew 23%, with a large increase in revenue from therapeutics customers. Both companies sell consumables and DNA used to produce biological data at scale for training AI models.

There is little documentary evidence of the AI connection at either company. In an Aug 18 filing, Twist said that after Anthropic published a post describing results from protein design across 15 targets, Anthropic selected Twist as an independent evaluator for the production and binding tests of designed proteins.[^u-twist-8k] For 10x Genomics, the evidence consists only of management comments on earnings calls and analyst notes, both secondary sources. We found no quantitative study that measured the causes of the gains, and the sample is small, so this analysis does not conclude that AI expectations caused the multiples.

Thermo Fisher's second-quarter organic growth rate, excluding acquisition and currency effects, was 5%, while Danaher's core revenue growth rate was 3%[^u-tmo][^u-dhr]. Organic growth at large tools companies remains in the single digits.

Danaher reported that second-quarter orders for biopharmaceutical production equipment and consumables (bioprocessing) rose by the mid-teens[^u-dhr]. China accounted for 4.8% of Illumina’s revenue, 9.9% of 10x Genomics’, and 12.5% of Bruker’s (BRKR) (calculated from each company’s second-quarter filings). Second-quarter revenue at AbCellera (ABCL), Recursion, Absci (ABSI), and Generate Biomedicines (GENB) fell 38% to 76% from a year earlier. Partner revenue recognized from research collaborations with pharmaceutical companies declined. Recursion said revenue fell because some stages of its Roche projects ended[^u-recursion].

Second-quarter revenue at Caris Life Sciences (CAI) rose 45%. Tempus AI (TEM) rose 22%, and Schrödinger (SDGR) rose 8%. These companies sell recurring products such as molecular profiling tests and data, clinical data, and molecular design software.

Schrödinger’s second-quarter net income of $6 million came from a one-time event. Eli Lilly (LLY) acquired Ajax, which Schrödinger co-founded, and Schrödinger received $57.2 million in equity proceeds and milestone payments[^u-sdgr]. Its core business still had an operating loss of $41.5 million.

In large pharmaceutical contracts confirmed against original documents, upfront payments at signing were 3% to 6% of the announced total (this analysis’s calculation). The rest is payable only when development, regulatory, or sales conditions are met, and public companies rarely include these contingent amounts in accounting revenue forecasts[^u-recursion]. Platform companies have received more cash from one-time sources such as equity realizations, settlements, and capital raises than from recurring revenue.

AbCellera’s share price rose 267% year to date after an August Phase 2 announcement, an intermediate clinical trial stage that first checks for efficacy. Absci rose 191%. Recursion, which depends on partner revenue, rose 6.6%, below XBI’s gain[^u-nasdaq]. This year, clinical events, rather than platform revenue, have driven share prices of AI drug discovery companies.

On Sep 23, the FDA approved lirafugratinib, a bile duct cancer treatment designed using molecular dynamics simulation by Relay Therapeutics (RLAY)[^u-relay-fda]. The approval holder is Elevar, a U.S. subsidiary of South Korea’s HLB, according to reports[^u-elevar]. Relay is the licensor that granted technology rights and receives milestones and royalties. Relay has received $18.7 million to date, and contractual milestones total up to $485 million[^u-relay-10q].

This approval is based on physical simulation, not generative AI, and an approved drug already exists for the FGFR2 target. The evidence points to a more selective molecule against the same target, not to the discovery of a new target. Takeda’s zasocitinib, designed by Nimbus using the Schrödinger platform, was also accepted for priority review on Sep 14. The target date for an approval decision is the first quarter of 2027[^u-takeda].

No U.S.-listed AI drug discovery company has yet filed for approval of its own asset. The most advanced internally owned asset is in Phase 3, the final clinical stage before approval, and results are not expected until after 2028.

Alphabet-affiliated Isomorphic Labs raised $2.1 billion in a single round in May, according to reports[^u-isomorphic]. The EVs of listed AI drug discovery companies are $1.4 billion to $3.5 billion (calculated). Generate, which went public in February, was trading 10% below its IPO price as of Oct 9.

BioAge Labs (BIOA), which emphasizes aging, trades at 0.76x, with a market capitalization below its cash. Its EV is about −$90 million[^u-bioa]. The primary endpoint in its ongoing Phase 2 trial is hsCRP, an inflammation marker, not an aging measure.

Moderna (MRNA) is highest in the figure at 13.0x. United Therapeutics (UTHR) is also at 6.2x, but its value has a different basis. Its pulmonary arterial hypertension drug business, which generated $333 million in second-quarter net income, supports its valuation. Xenotransplantation revenue is still zero[^u-uthr]. This analysis views the value of xenotransplantation as layered on top of a profitable business rather than priced separately.

Based on first-half cash burn, Recursion has about 1.5 years of cash, Generate about 1.7 years, and Absci about 1.9 years. These estimates divide June-end cash by first-half operating cash outflow annualized.

The companies have given longer runways. Recursion said it expects to fund operations into early 2028, and Absci into the second half of 2028. Those figures assume cost reductions and contract payments[^u-recursion]. This analysis sees cash burn as determining the downside for companies whose clinical results fall short of expectations when interest rates are rising.

A binary event is one with two possible outcomes: success or failure. The largest dated binary event is Intellia’s approval decision for lonvo-z, a treatment for hereditary angioedema. It is the first approval decision for a CRISPR gene-editing therapy that alters genes inside the body. The PDUFA, or FDA target date for an approval decision, is Mar 10, 2027[^u-ntla]. The FDA told the company it does not plan to hold an advisory committee meeting.

The route varies by theme. Even within biotech, investors need to identify public companies and confirmation methods separately for each theme.

Four of the five routes are strong for diagnostics and screening. Evidence includes Abbott’s acquisition of Exact Sciences; competition between Abbott and Guardant in blood tests for colorectal cancer; Freenome’s SPAC merger and listing; and a law that allows Medicare to cover multi-cancer early detection starting in 2028.

For cell, gene, and organ therapies, the acquisition and regulatory routes are strong. Evidence includes Lilly’s acquisition of a private in vivo CAR-T company, which changes immune cells directly inside the patient’s body, and Intellia’s approval decision deadline. For tools and measurement, the acquisition and supply-chain routes are strong, while the competition, listing, and regulatory routes are weak.

AI drug discovery is generally medium, while its supply-chain and regulatory routes are weak. For digital health, only the listing route is strong, thanks to Hinge and Omada going public. For medical AI, only the competition route is strong.

No U.S.-listed company focuses only on AI for medical documentation. Epic, a private electronic health record company, launched the same function directly in February[^u-epic]. Doximity (DOCS), a physician network, has fallen 58% over the past year amid discussion of competition from free medical AI. For public investors, the theme has reached them first through competition rather than as a benefit.

Both routes are weak for brain-computer interfaces (BCI) and aging. This research found no IPO filing or announced SPAC merger by a U.S. private brain-computer interface company. No listed company focused on aging has a clinical trial where aging itself is the indication.

IQVIA’s tally, which counts only transactions above $250 million, recorded 42 biopharma acquisitions worth $130 billion in the first half of 2026[^u-iqvia]. That is close to the total for all of 2025. Acquisitions are the route by which private-company value has most often been confirmed entering public markets through actual transactions.

Lilly acquired $26 billion worth of companies in the first half alone, according to IQVIA, and 10 of its 13 acquisition targets this year are private companies.[^u-lly-10q][^u-biobucks-ma] In vivo CAR-T companies Kelonia and Orna are among them. This is a route for private-company value to flow directly to large pharmaceutical companies without passing through publicly traded biotech companies.

Lilly's market capitalization was $1.11 trillion on Oct 9. Individual acquired assets are small by comparison, so the theme gives Lilly shareholders limited exposure. For public-market investors, the direct share of this route is the premium paid to shareholders of acquired public biotech companies.

Based on BioBucks' deal table, the median premium (the amount by which the acquisition price exceeded the prior share price) for 15 acquisitions by strategic buyers of public biotech companies was about 41%.[^u-biobucks-ma] The range was 2% to 140%. The figures mix prior closing prices and average share prices over specified periods, so they are reference points. Across all 24 disclosed public-company targets in 2026, the median disclosed premium was about 39%. When a late-stage small or mid-cap company trading near its cash value is acquired, its shareholders receive this premium. Whether an acquisition will happen is unknown. This analysis establishes that such deals have occurred.

The near-term confirmation points are late-October tools and diagnostics earnings and Moderna's detailed results. The largest scheduled binary event is in March, after the turn of the year. Until then, valuations on the therapeutics side depend more on cash and interest rates.

### The first-priority diligence types are companies in Korea with revenue still of only a few billion won

The cell and experimental automation types marked for first-priority diligence on the global map are also present in Korea. Curiosis, IBEAM Technology, Protina, Excel Therapeutics, CuriOx Biosystems, and Microdigital have evidence of selling measurement equipment and services, or cell-manufacturing materials, to overseas customers. But their first-half 2026 revenue ranged from KRW 520 million to KRW 4.62 billion, and all reported operating losses.

The strongest evidence comes from companies whose filings disclose the contract counterparty and amount. In January 2026, Curiosis disclosed an ODM contract with Revvity’s Chinese subsidiary for colony pickers. ODM means the manufacturer develops and produces a product that the customer sells under its own brand. The contract was worth $1.48 million, or about KRW 2.18 billion. The company said the contract uses a minimum-purchase arrangement, so actual revenue depends on orders.[^k-curiosis]

In September 2026, IBEAM Technology disclosed two supply contracts for intravital microscopes. The contracts go through a Canadian distributor, but the end customers are the US National Institutes of Health’s NIEHS and Yale University.[^k-ibeam] According to a news report, all KRW 2.79 billion in intravital microscope revenue in the first half came from exports. The company stated in its semiannual report that purchase decisions typically take about two years.[^k-ibeam-export]

In September 2025, Protina disclosed a $850,000 contract to analyze clinical specimens for a US multinational pharmaceutical company.[^k-protina] Yet its first-half 2026 revenue was KRW 520 million, about 18% of its 2025 full-year revenue. This shows that a single contract does not ensure steady recurring revenue.

News reports confirm Excel Therapeutics’ culture-media supply to China’s Shanghai Cell Therapy Group and its white-label contract with Germany’s PL BioScience, under which products are sold under the counterparty’s brand.[^k-excell] Its first-half 2026 revenue of KRW 2.96 billion already exceeded its full-year 2025 revenue of KRW 1.95 billion. However, its total equity at the end of June was only KRW 4.33 billion, so its growth rate and funding capacity should be assessed together.[^k-opendart]

Microdigital corrected its disclosure to state that cumulative payments under its supply contract with Japan’s Shinkou Seiki totaled KRW 690 million. Its bioreactor supply to India’s Serum Institute is supported by news reports.[^k-mdigital] Cash and cash equivalents at the end of June were KRW 5.11 billion. Even including short-term financial instruments, this was below its first-half operating loss of KRW 9.23 billion.[^k-opendart]

CuriOx Biosystems’ claim that it supplied 18 of the world’s top 20 pharmaceutical companies came from a company announcement at its 2023 listing. News reports say its annual revenue fell from KRW 6.8 billion in 2023 to KRW 5.2 billion in 2025. The number of customers still purchasing from the company needs to be checked separately.[^k-curiox]

The key question for this group is whether an initial contract leads to repeat orders. For equipment companies, examine the share of revenue from consumables. For minimum-purchase contracts, check the order fulfillment rate against the committed volume. Curiosis’ KRW 2.08 billion public procurement contract bundled third-party equipment with its own, so the two revenue sources should be separated.[^k-curiosis-procure]

## What Changes the Judgment Is Numerical Evidence, Not Participation by a Famous Investor

Across every cell in the map, the evidence that would change the judgment is the same: effects in independent human studies, productivity that can be reproduced, contract cash actually received, and enough funding to last until the next validation. A rising share price is not one of these four.

The near-term checkpoints are clear. From Oct 21 to 29, check whether bioprocessing orders increased in the third-quarter results from Thermo Fisher, Danaher, and Illumina. Hinge Health’s results on Nov 2 will show whether earnings quality is holding up. In mid-November, check the Korean listed companies’ third-quarter reports to see whether overseas contracts have turned into recurring revenue. Grail’s FDA decision date has not been announced, but whether the authorization language retains “early” will determine the diagnostic valuation multiple. Intellia’s Mar 10, 2027, decision deadline is the largest dated binary event.

## What This Analysis Could Not Confirm

This analysis is based on public sources. It did not obtain valuations, shareholder registers, cash balances, or original contracts. It therefore does not assess any individual company’s investment value or the timing of a trade.

- The investment links rely on media reports and official portfolio lists. Individual investment amounts and current ownership stakes remain unconfirmed in most cases. An investment not found in the search was not judged to be nonexistent.
- The placement of the thematic map and the strength of each route are judgments in this analysis. They are not measurements, and placements could differ under other criteria.
- Acquisition counts, IPO counts, and premiums rely on secondary compilations. Their scopes vary, so they were not added together.
- Some dates, including Illumina and Lilly earnings dates and the first deadline for the Biosecure Act list, are based on media reports or law firm analyses. They should be checked against company disclosures.
- Regional revenue shares for Korean companies in 2026 were mostly not verified against the original semiannual reports. Stock prices and market capitalizations were not checked for Korean companies.
- All financial figures should be checked again when third-quarter reports are released in mid-November.
