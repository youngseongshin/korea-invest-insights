On November 5 the Nasdaq Composite fell 5.5% in one day. AppLovin rose 31.0% and Unity rose 14.9% the same day. Over two days AppLovin gained <strong>44.0%</strong> and Unity <strong>21.3%</strong>, while the Nasdaq lost 4.5%.

AppLovin reported third-quarter revenue of <strong>$2.185 billion (+55%)</strong>, $100 million above the top of its own guidance. Unity's strategic revenue, the part tied to advertising, was <strong>$597 million (+59%)</strong>, 8.5% above the top of its range. Unity also reported its first positive operating income under GAAP (accounting rules) since its IPO.

One number stands out more. AppLovin's installs grew 2% from a year earlier, but the money it earns per install grew 55%. Both beats came from how accurately each company can match an ad to a person. Their ad prediction models took a step up during the quarter, and advertisers raised budgets to match.

<strong>Our call: this beat is not one-off demand. It is a step change in a prediction model showing up in the numbers. The same structure means next quarter can swing hard in either direction. Whether it lasts depends on when the next model improvement arrives. AppLovin's consumer ads and Unity's Spark matter for 2028 and later, more than for 2027.</strong>

## How to read the unfamiliar terms

| Term | In plain words |
|---|---|
| Guidance | The range a company gives for next quarter's numbers. A result above the top of the range is "better than guided". |
| User acquisition (UA) ads | Ads a game maker buys to find new players. The maker pays when a player installs the game. |
| ROAS | Revenue earned per dollar of ad spend. Advertisers keep raising budgets as long as they hit their ROAS target. |
| Adjusted EBITDA | Profit before interest, taxes and depreciation, with some further costs (such as stock pay) removed by the company. Useful for comparing one company's quarters, not different companies. |
| Quarter-on-quarter, year-on-year | Growth versus the previous quarter, and versus the same quarter a year earlier. |
| CPM | The price of 1,000 ad impressions. |

## One ad is decided by one auction, and a prediction model sets the price

A mobile game maker needs new players. So it buys ads inside other apps and games. Each time an ad slot opens, an auction decides which ad to show and how much to bid. AppLovin and Unity build and sell the machinery that runs that auction.

The model that sets the bid predicts one thing: how likely this person is to install the game and spend money. AppLovin's model is called Axon. Unity's is called Vector.

Think of an appraiser at an auction house. The sharper the appraisal, the higher the price the house can bid, and the advertiser still comes out ahead, so it is glad to spend. A bad appraisal works the other way.

![How one ad gets matched to one game slot. The game maker (advertiser) hands a budget to the ad engine. The engine's prediction model sets the bid and sends the ad to an app or game that owns the slot. Installs and in-app purchases flow back and the model keeps retraining. AppLovin learns from auction records on its ad switchboard MAX plus install and purchase events. Unity learns from game engine runtime information and install and billing records on its ad network.](/post-assets/applovin-unity-3q26-prediction-model-step-up-price-per-install-2026-11-08/ad-matching.en.svg)

The two companies are built a little differently.

AppLovin also runs MAX, software that connects advertisers with the apps and games that own ad slots. It books as revenue only what is left after paying app owners their share. That is why its revenue growth splits into "money left per install × number of installs".

Unity sells game-making tools (an engine) and also learns from information produced by games built on that engine. It has two businesses: Grow for advertising and Create for the tools. Vector is the ad model inside Grow, and it switched to a self-learning design in 2025.

## Almost all of AppLovin's growth came from the value of each install

AppLovin's quarterly filing (10-Q) explains revenue growth as "net revenue per install × number of installs". Put three quarters side by side and the nature of this quarter shows.

| Quarter | Net revenue per install (YoY) | Installs (YoY) | Revenue (YoY) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1Q26 | +93% | −18% | +59% |
| 2Q26 | +58% | −2% | +53% |
| 3Q26 | +55% | +2% | +55% |
| 9 months | +68% | −7% | +56% |

![Almost all of AppLovin's growth came from the value of each install. Net revenue per install was +93%, +58% and +55% in 1Q26, 2Q26 and 3Q26, while installs were −18%, −2% and +2%.](/post-assets/applovin-unity-3q26-prediction-model-step-up-price-per-install-2026-11-08/price-vs-volume.en.svg)

In the first half, installs fell and growth came from price alone. In the third quarter installs turned positive for the first time while net revenue per install held at 55%. Multiply the two and you get about +58%, which is 3 points off the +55% revenue growth. We could not confirm the reason in the filing, so we use the two numbers only to read direction.

The CEO said the improvement from the next-generation model was largest where "data is scarce", meaning new and small advertisers and new categories. We read this as a sign that the model did not only lift bids for large existing advertisers. It widened the range of advertisers and installs the company can serve. That fits installs turning positive.

By region, the United States brought $1.098 billion (+59.6%) and the rest of the world $1.087 billion (+51.5%). The direction matches the second-quarter call remark that consumer (web e-commerce) advertising is concentrated in the West.

The CEO said gaming ad revenue grew about 50% year on year and re-accelerated. The company does not report segment revenue, so we cannot confirm that number. The next-generation model is only partly deployed, and the company made no promise on when it will be fully switched. The CEO described the change as comparable in size to the earlier Axon 2.0.

## A better model turns into revenue along two paths

Advertisers on both platforms keep raising budgets as long as they hit their ROAS target. So a better model opens two paths to revenue.

The first path is a higher value for each install. If the model predicts higher value on the same impression, the bid goes up, and the advertiser earns the same or more for each ad dollar. The AppLovin CEO's remark that "better targeting lifts CPM" points to this path. AppLovin's +55% net revenue per install belongs here.

The second path is more advertisers the company can serve. Advertisers, categories and regions that did not pay off before start to. AppLovin's installs moving from −18% and −2% to +2% belongs here, and so does Unity's advertiser accounts rising 27% quarter on quarter. Unity's CFO said 90% of that growth came from existing advertisers.

![Two ways a better prediction model turns into revenue. A sharper model raises bids, advertisers still hit their target and raise budgets. Revenue grows along two paths: each install is worth more, and more advertisers become viable.](/post-assets/applovin-unity-3q26-prediction-model-step-up-price-per-install-2026-11-08/model-to-revenue.en.svg)

Unity's CEO said Vector's growth came from more installs and more player value, and that the take rate is not a variable the company manages. The two CEOs describe the same mechanism in different words.

## AppLovin kept 87% of its added revenue as profit

When AppLovin's revenue rose $261 million in the quarter, adjusted EBITDA rose $228 million. The share of added revenue that stayed as profit (the incremental margin) was 87.5%. Computing cost barely followed the revenue increase.

R&D expense rose 132% year on year. The 10-Q gives $55 million of added stock-pay-related labor cost as the reason, and the added cost of computing for training does not show up much in this line. The cost to watch next is the renewal of its minimum cloud purchase commitment: $1.3 billion over three years as of August 2024, of which $1.1 billion had been paid by the end of September.

## Unity's profit jumped because the same engine met a clean-up

Unity's strategic Grow rose 31.2% from the prior quarter and 90% from a year earlier. The company does not report Vector revenue, but it gave growth rates. Vector grew 23% quarter on quarter in the second quarter (guided 12 to 13%) and 40% in the third (guided 20%). That is nearly double the guide two quarters in a row.

So we estimated Vector's revenue. We assume Grow revenue outside Vector (LevelPlay mediation and similar) is roughly constant from quarter to quarter. Backing the $103 million rise in strategic Grow out of Vector's +40% gives third-quarter Vector revenue of about $359 million. The same assumption gives $256 million for the second quarter, which matches the "annualized above $1 billion" remark on the second-quarter call ($256 million × 4 = $1.02 billion).

![Unity revenue mix. Estimated Vector revenue grew from $159 million in 3Q25 to $359 million in 3Q26, from 34% to 58% of total revenue, while the closed or sold businesses fell from $96 million to $26 million.](/post-assets/applovin-unity-3q26-prediction-model-step-up-price-per-install-2026-11-08/unity-revenue-stack.en.svg)

If the estimate holds, two things follow. Unity is already a Vector company. And the fourth-quarter guide (Vector up 21 to 23% on the quarter) assumes almost no growth outside Vector. The estimate rests on the assumption that non-Vector Grow is constant, and we put the error at about ±$15 million.

The profit side is in the next figure. Adjusted EBITDA rose $96 million from the prior quarter. About 76% of that rise came from higher gross profit and about 23% from lower marketing cost. The lower marketing cost reflects user-acquisition spend that disappeared with Supersonic, sold in August.

![Where Unity's profit came from. Adjusted EBITDA rose from $160.2 million in the second quarter through gross profit +72.9, lower marketing +22.1, lower R and D spending +1.6 and higher G and A spending −0.2 to $256.6 million in the third quarter.](/post-assets/applovin-unity-3q26-prediction-model-step-up-price-per-install-2026-11-08/unity-profit-waterfall.en.svg)

GAAP operating income turned positive later than adjusted EBITDA because amortization of intangible assets and stock pay were large. Both fell, and the first profit arrived in the third quarter.

| Unity item | 3Q25 | 2Q26 | 3Q26 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Adjusted EBITDA | $110 million | $160 million | $257 million |
| Adjusted EBITDA margin | 23% | 29% | 41% |
| GAAP operating income | −$126 million | −$32 million | +$101 million |
| Amortization of intangibles | $125 million | $78 million | $77 million |
| Stock pay | $92 million | $76 million | $63 million |

The company guided amortization to $50 million in the fourth quarter and $15 million a quarter in 2027. It guided stock pay to $75 million a quarter in 2027 and the effective tax rate to 5 to 10%. If that holds, GAAP profit in 2027 sits closer to adjusted EBITDA.

Unity's tools business (Create) has settled into a business growing about 9% a year. The fourth-quarter guide is +4 to 6%. The drivers are price and mix more than seat count.

On the balance sheet, Unity has $2.65 billion of cash. Its convertible notes are $558 million due November 15, 2026, $1 billion due 2027 and $690 million due 2030. Repaying the $558 million in cash on November 15 leaves cash of about $2.1 billion and $1.69 billion of convertible notes. Shares outstanding are 443.8 million, up 2.5% from the start of the year.

China is a new risk factor. China revenue was $157 million (+74%), 25% of the total, up from 19% a year ago and 21% in the second quarter. The company did not explain the mix. We guess it is Vector's demand from Chinese publishers buying overseas users, plus strong Create sales in China.

## Whether it lasts depends on when the next model improvement arrives

Both companies put only model improvements that are already running into their guidance. They leave out models not yet deployed. AppLovin's CFO said in the second and third quarters that guidance counts "improvements already running, further consumer expansion, normal seasonality and rising training cost". Unity's CFO also builds guidance from run rate, seasonality and planned improvements, and said "unpredictable step changes like the third quarter" can happen.

So whether next quarter beats the top of guidance comes down to whether a new improvement lands inside the quarter. AppLovin's second and third quarters are the two sides of one variable.

![AppLovin's quarterly growth moves in steps. Quarter-on-quarter revenue growth was 11.6% in 3Q25, 18.0% in 4Q25, 11.1% in 1Q26, 4.4% in 2Q26 and 13.6% in 3Q26, and the 4Q26 guide is 8.2 to 9.6%.](/post-assets/applovin-unity-3q26-prediction-model-step-up-price-per-install-2026-11-08/applovin-growth-steps.en.svg)

In the second quarter, as the CEO put it, the pace of improvement was lighter than usual and the next jump came just after quarter-end. Revenue came in slightly below the guidance midpoint and the stock fell 16%. In the third quarter that jump landed early, revenue beat the top of the range by $100 million and the stock rose 44% in two days. Averaged over the two quarters, growth returns to normal.

AppLovin stresses that it beat the top of its range in 14 of 16 quarters. Still, one late quarter like the second moves the stock a lot. The fourth-quarter guide (+8 to 10% on the quarter) is below past fourth quarters (+20%, +18%). That looks conservative given the consumer-ad peak season, but the CEO warned of "a harder first quarter after a big fourth". The bigger the consumer-ad share, the bigger the seasonality.

Unity's CEO drew a line: "not 40% every quarter". The fourth-quarter Vector guide is +21 to 23%.

Put the five variables side by side and the ranking shows.

| Variable | AppLovin | Unity | Our read |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Model improvement cycle | Next-generation model partly deployed, no date for full switch | 6 to 10 product changes a quarter; CEO: "not 40% every quarter" | Drives 2027 numbers most. Both are mid-switch, so room for another jump remains through the first half. |
| 2. Demand pool (game ad budgets) | Most revenue is gaming. Installs turning positive shows a wider reach | Mobile game ad market above $40 billion, double-digit growth (CEO claim) | Both accelerate at once, so there is no sign yet of one taking from the other. |
| 3. Supply of ad slots (impressions) | MAX publisher revenue up double digits; market-wide impressions up single to low double digits | Creator daily users up 25% from early August | Impressions are not growing fast, so growth leans on value per impression. It lasts only while advertiser ROAS holds. |
| 4. New demand pools | Consumer (web e-commerce), then non-game apps, open web and CTV | Spark's own ad slots, asset store | Small in 2027 numbers. A 2028-and-later condition. |
| 5. Cost and cash | Cloud commitment renewal, stock pay | Convertible repayment, stock pay 10% of revenue, China share 25% | Cash generation covers cost risk at both. Unity's China share is a new risk. |

The dominant variable is the first. The second and third set where growth converges if the first stops. The AppLovin CEO's long-run 30% compounding, Unity's Create at 9% and Vector's "healthy growth" are the companies' own versions of that landing point.

For two companies in the same market to grow near 50% and 90%, the market has to grow while Meta, Google and older networks lose share. We covered ad budgets moving between platforms, with Google and Meta, in our [October 30 post](/post/meta-ad-edge-google-amazon-share-shift-2026-10-30/).

## The new growth engines matter for 2028 and later, not 2027

### AppLovin: consumer (web e-commerce) ads

AppLovin is widening beyond games, and online shopping ads come first. Visibility is still low. The company does not disclose advertiser counts or revenue share, and the CEO said it does not give KPIs because the business is "not at a stable stage".

The public clues are these. In the second quarter, consumer advertiser spend was up 28% from the fourth quarter of 2025. A new customer spends on average $70,000 in its first year. The company goes after mid-sized advertisers first and has put the long tail on hold. The biggest bottleneck is ad creative. It is also widening integrations with analytics platforms such as Triple Whale.

The CEO said that supply expansion beyond games (non-game apps, then the open web, then CTV) is "at pilot stage and will not contribute to numbers for now". Sell-side estimates vary widely, so we did not use them.

### Unity: Spark and the Google partnership

Spark is a browser-based, no-code, collaborative creation tool announced on October 7. It deploys to Google Playground, aims for a private beta by the end of 2026 and a full launch in 2027, and is non-exclusive. Guidance contains no revenue from it. We read it as a door for new users more than as revenue.

The CFO stressed two sets of economics. Unity gets its own (O&O) ad inventory outside the mediation ecosystem. And Unity's own assets sold in its asset store carry a 100% margin.

Creator daily users rose 25% from early August after Unity opened its coding agent and CLI. That opening includes a Claude Code plugin. We disclose our interest below.

## How to read the signs that it is breaking

- AppLovin: two quarters in a row that the company explains by "no new model landed", with quarter-on-quarter growth at or below the seasonal average, would say growth has not escaped its dependence on step jumps. The first test is the first quarter of 2027. If net revenue per install and installs both slow to single digits, the pricing engine has run dry.
- Unity: if Vector's quarterly growth falls below 15% while advertiser account growth stalls, the share shift is over. If strategic Grow outside Vector (about $70 million) starts to shrink, LevelPlay's mediation position is weakening.
- Common 1: if one company accelerates in a quarter when the other slows, that is a zero-sum phase. Today both accelerate. What Meta and Google say about gaming ads, and publishers' in-app purchase trends (secondary sources such as Sensor Tower), are side indicators.
- Common 2: if advertisers' ROAS does not hold, the higher CPM reverses. For AppLovin's consumer ads there is a measurement debate between platform-reported ROAS and truly incremental revenue, and it is not yet confirmed that more advertiser spend leads to more purchases by end consumers.

Both stocks rose a lot in two days. We think the prices have taken in some of the next jump. These numbers are our own calculation from the November 6 close.

| On fourth-quarter annualized adjusted EBITDA | | about 19.4x |

Method: market value is shares outstanding times the closing price, and enterprise value is market value plus debt minus cash. AppLovin's 2026 adjusted EBITDA is nine months of actual results ($5.01 billion) plus the midpoint of the fourth-quarter guide ($2.01 billion), and the FCF yield assumes about $5 billion of 2026 FCF (a 72% conversion). Unity's is nine months of actuals plus the guidance midpoint, with convertible notes counted only as debt.

The annual numbers for net revenue per install and installs arrive in the 10-K. ## What this means for Korean investors

For mobile game publishers (Krafton, Netmarble and others), this change means better user-acquisition efficiency, but the same model also lifts CPM. Translating it into simple marketing savings gets it wrong. For a publisher, spend rises when ROAS stays the same.

The link to memory and AI infrastructure is small. AppLovin's computing cost is under 10% of its added revenue. Ad prediction models are often cited as proof that AI investment pays back, but their contribution to hyperscaler revenue is small. AppLovin's $1.3 billion three-year cloud commitment works out to about $430 million a year.

The two stocks share exposure to mobile-game ad budgets, the multiple paid for AI growth stocks, and step changes in models. Holding both may give less diversification than it appears. AppLovin stands out for pricing power that is disclosed in numbers in its 10-Q and for cash returns. Unity stands out for more room left in growth acceleration and operating leverage.

## Limits of the data

- Vector revenue and AppLovin's split of gaming versus consumer revenue are not disclosed. Our quarterly Vector estimate rests on non-Vector strategic Grow staying constant, and the roughly 50% gaming growth at AppLovin is a CEO remark.
- Consensus figures are pre-release values from aggregator sites (MarketBeat, Zacks, Benzinga) and differ by provider. We could not obtain raw LSEG or FactSet values.

- Market share figures (an iOS ad revenue share of 39 to 44% and similar) and Triple Whale's ROAS compilations come from blogs and vendors, so we did not use them as a basis.
- AppLovin's shareholder letter holds only charts and financial tables, so we relied on the earnings call for narrative. Unity has issued no shareholder letter since the third quarter of 2024.
- We did not recheck, in this 10-Q, the gross-versus-net recognition basis for Unity's advertising revenue.
