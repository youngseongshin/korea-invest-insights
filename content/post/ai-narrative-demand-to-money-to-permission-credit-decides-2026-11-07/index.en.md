Since September 23, the KOSPI is down <strong>28.5%</strong>. From their June highs, Samsung Electronics is down <strong>47.9%</strong> and SK hynix <strong>61.2%</strong>. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOX) fell <strong>38.4%</strong> from June 22 to its November 5 low.

Over the same weeks, the profit side got stronger. Samsung booked KRW 107.4 trillion of operating profit in the third quarter. TrendForce expects fourth-quarter conventional DRAM contract prices to rise another 10-15% ([TrendForce, September 30](https://www.trendforce.com/presscenter/news/20260930-13258.html)). All four big US hyperscalers raised their 2027 capex on third-quarter earnings calls. Korea's October chip exports reached $56.44 billion, up 259.4% from a year earlier.

Both facts are true because the market's question kept changing. In late September it asked whether AI infrastructure could keep its pricing power at high rates. In mid-October it asked how long record profits last. By late October it asked who pays. This week it asked whether politics will keep allowing the build-out. Each question sits further from the one thing that improved, which is profit.

<strong>Our call: the price verdict is bear. The 2027 profit verdict is bull. The variable that connects them is credit. The high-yield spread closed at 348bp on November 5, 2bp below the 350bp line we watch.</strong>

Both verdicts can be tested against lines you can watch: 330bp and 350bp on the high-yield spread. At the end we ask which side you take.

## Prices fell like a bear market while profit estimates rose

Korean memory's big drop started in June, not October. Samsung peaked on June 18 and SK hynix on June 22. October added a second leg: from October 6 to 15, Samsung fell 22.8%, SK hynix 28.8% and the KOSPI 20.0%.

The US looks different. The S&P 500 is 5.7% below its October 6 record close, and the Nasdaq-100 is 13.6% below its high. The gap comes from how sensitive each company's revenue is to the business cycle. The side that buys AI demand fell least. The side that sells components fell most.

## The question moved four times, each time away from profit

![The market's question moved four times. Sep 18 to Oct 6: can AI infrastructure keep its pricing power at high rates. Oct 7 to 16: how long do record profits last and what does capital cost. Oct 17 to 31: demand is real, so who pays for it. Nov 1 to 7: will politics keep allowing it.](/post-assets/ai-narrative-demand-to-money-to-permission-credit-decides-2026-11-07/question-ladder.en.svg)

The October turn shows it best. Samsung's record KRW 107.4 trillion landed 1.2% above one consensus (LSEG, KRW 106.1 trillion) and 4.7% below another (Visible Alpha, KRW 112.73 trillion), and the two use different bases. The KOSPI still fell 2.62% that day. The argument had moved from the size of profit to its duration.

Then it moved to money. On October 14, CoreWeave fell 15.5% and Oracle 8.8% in US trading. Both fell more than Micron (-4.1%). Oracle's five-year credit default swap (CDS) stood at 288bp, up 26bp. CoreWeave's 2032 bond yielded 14.8%. The market was selling the financing chain, and memory sat inside it.

By the third quarter calls the demand evidence was hard to argue with. Amazon booked most of 2027 and a large part of 2028. Google's API token volume rose 40% quarter on quarter. North American data center vacancy stood at 1.4%. But the five companies' combined third-quarter free cash flow was -$31.8 billion, and capex in the latest quarter ran at 116% of operating cash flow. External funding needs for 2027 come to about $200 billion, with guarantee paths of $48 billion at Alphabet and $41.5 billion at Meta.

The policy row needs one more line. The market used to treat Washington as a backstop for the AI build-out, an "AI put". Our reading is that it is weaker at the federal level and now has state-level brakes. The same election also pulled Brent and the 10-year yield down, which lowered macro risk. Counting "AI put weaker" and "rates and oil lower" as independent factors double counts one event.

## Why credit decides

![Four variables, one that decides. V1 2027 profit delivery leans bull. V2 funding cost and credit leans bear and is marked as the deciding variable. V3 political permission leans bear and moves slowly. V4 positioning is bearish over 1-2 weeks and bullish over 1-3 months.](/post-assets/ai-narrative-demand-to-money-to-permission-credit-decides-2026-11-07/four-variables.en.svg)

Political limits and AI-lab profitability both reach 2028 orders through one channel: financing. When the policy cushion thins, return on invested capital and the durability of free cash flow become the only backstops. The market then asks harder about credit. This is why strong 2027 profit cannot lift the price discount yet. Price will not release its duration discount until credit settles.

![High-yield spread in basis points. 266 on Sep 21, 323 on Oct 15, 344 on Oct 30, 340 on Nov 3 and 348 on Nov 5. Two lines are drawn: 350bp, above which for five sessions confirms a credit squeeze, and 330bp, at or below which for five sessions reverses it. The latest value is 2bp below the 350 line. CCC spreads rose from 1,075bp to 1,356bp and investment grade from 77bp to 93bp.](/post-assets/ai-narrative-demand-to-money-to-permission-credit-decides-2026-11-07/credit-gate.en.svg)

The chart carries the best bull argument and the best bear argument at once. In the two sessions when SOX fell 15%, spreads widened only 8bp, so credit has not followed equities down. But the spread has widened 82bp since September 21, and it sits 2bp from the line where the funding story stops being a worry and becomes a fact ([FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/BAMLH0A0HYM2)).

The two bull conditions must arrive together. One is not enough. The bear conditions work alone.

## Where we could be wrong

<strong>Objection one: "A 15% two-day drop in chips is forced selling, not a financing story."</strong> Partly right, and it is right for the next one to two weeks. Trend-following funds and retail leverage dominate that window. Our credit claim is about one to three months, when crowded shorts turn into rebound fuel instead of pressure.

<strong>Objection two: "Fear at 28 and a P/E under 3.2x say buy."</strong> Fear and low multiples describe the price. They say nothing about who finances 2028. We do not treat them as evidence for a bottom by themselves.

<strong>Objection three: "2027 profit is sealed, so 2028 is far away."</strong> Stocks price duration, not one year. Buyer inventories sit above normal and the big four plan 15-17% more wafers for 2028. If orders for 2028 depend on credit, the 2027 profit does not release the price.

## What this means

Korean memory prices already paid for a cyclical bear market, while 2027 profit estimates went up. The gap between them closes when credit decides, and not before.

Over one to two weeks, selling by trend-following funds and by retail leverage dominates. Over one to three months, crowded shorts increase the size of any rebound. The S&P 500 has already broken 7,544, a mid-term line that trend-following funds watch. Their long-term line sits at 7,040.

This is not a call to buy. Fear gauges and low multiples alone are not a reason.
