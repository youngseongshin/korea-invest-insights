At the moment the United States declared it would build nuclear power plants again on a large scale, Korea is negotiating what it will receive in return for providing the capital and manufacturing capacity to build those plants. Winning a few construction contracts is a different transaction from participating in the long-term profits of the nuclear industry. That difference is precisely why the Westinghouse stake negotiations matter.

Korea–US cooperation can expand the market for Korea's nuclear industry. However, the economic value depends less on the ownership percentage and more on procurement rights, the scope of joint exports, and the allocation of construction losses. Among Korean-listed companies, we see Doosan Enerbility as the industrial center of gravity for this benefit — but that does not mean it is the stock most likely to deliver the greatest return at its current share price.

America's 400GW is not a confirmed order backlog. Westinghouse does not hold a monopoly on nuclear technology outside China. Acquiring a stake does not automatically increase Korean companies' contracts. Distinguishing these three points is what allows a fair assessment of the negotiations' significant opportunity — without exaggeration.[^wh400][^ieanuclear][^motir]

## 1. How Large a Plan Is 400GW

In a May 2025 executive order, the United States set a target to expand nuclear capacity from approximately 100GW in 2024 to 400GW by 2050. This does not mean building 400GW from scratch; it means increasing the total operating capacity to 400GW in the end.[^wh400]

GW is a measure of a plant's instantaneous output capacity. 1GW equals 1 million kW. How much electricity is actually generated in a year is expressed in GWh or TWh — output multiplied by operating hours. Plant size and electricity consumption should not be compared as if they were the same unit.

Converting the net addition of 300GW into AP1000-sized units of 1.1GW yields approximately 273 reactors. The actual plan may blend uprates of existing plants, life extensions, large reactors, and small modular reactors, so 273 units does not mean 273 AP1000 orders.

The near-term 2030 target is 5GW of uprates at existing plants and the groundbreaking of 10 new large reactors. Reading this as a target to complete all 10 reactors by 2030 significantly overstates how soon supply will materialize.[^whbase]

The policy's starting point is also not confined to a single administration. In 2024, the US Department of Energy set a target to add approximately 200GW of nuclear capacity by 2050, roughly tripling the then-existing capacity. In 2025, the final aggregate target was raised to 400GW. The long-term direction shows continuity, but the higher target also means the burden on actual construction pace and financing has grown.[^doe2024][^wh400]

### There Is Also Evidence That the Declaration Is Turning Into Action

On June 23, 2026, the US Department of Energy announced $17.5 billion in conditional loan commitments for procuring long-lead components. Long-lead components are equipment such as reactor pressure vessels that take a long time from order to delivery. The structure supports up to five projects, each procuring components for two AP1000 units.[^doeloan]

Each project must first commit equity of $500 million each from Westinghouse and its utility partner — $1 billion in total — before the loan can be drawn. If all five projects proceed on these terms, Westinghouse's aggregate equity commitment would reach $2.5 billion. This is a maximum-case calculation derived by multiplying the conditions; it is not an amount already spent.[^doeloan]

This program is evidence that nuclear expansion is not merely rhetoric. At the same time, the loan is disbursed only once technical, legal, environmental, and financial conditions are met, and this loan does not finance the entire construction cost of a power plant. Progress in supply-chain financing and final investment decisions for power plants cannot be treated as the same milestone.

## 2. How Far Has Korea Risen Historically — From Importer to Where?

Competitiveness in the nuclear industry does not end with a single set of blueprints. It accumulates through repeatedly constructing the same design, matching component quality, and incorporating lessons from operations back into the design.

The United States led the early spread of commercial nuclear power. Korea started as an importer of foreign technology and equipment, as exemplified by Kori Unit 1, which began commercial operation in 1978. Since then, Korea has built up capabilities in design, components, construction, and operations, and with the UAE Barakah project, four Korean-model reactors entered commercial operation in sequence between 2021 and 2024.[^shippingport][^kori][^barakah]

There is an important paradox here. Even Westinghouse, with its formidable reactor technology, filed for bankruptcy protection in 2017 under the financial burden of new nuclear construction projects in the United States. This is a case study showing that technological competitiveness and the profitability of construction contracts are separate things.[^wec2017]

Conversely, Korea's overseas completion track record is evidence of project execution capability, but the contract structure and labor sourcing conditions of the UAE cannot be transplanted directly to the United States. US site labor, licensing, construction standards, and litigation risk must be managed separately. The fact that Korea can build well does not by itself lead to the conclusion that it will complete US nuclear plants cheaply and quickly.

The current negotiations are therefore not a simple reversal of the past technology-import relationship. It is more accurate to view them as a process of determining the price and responsibilities under which the design, market access, and policy support that the US holds will be bundled with the manufacturing experience, construction capabilities, and capital that Korea holds.

## 3. Why the AI Power Gap Makes Nuclear Important Again

In the IEA's 2026 outlook, global data center electricity consumption rises from 485TWh in 2025 to 950TWh in 2030 — roughly doubling. This figure covers total data center consumption including general cloud services, not AI consumption alone.[^ieaai2026]

The shortage is not only a question of total global electricity. The real issue is whether sufficient, reliably stable power can be delivered in the specific regions where data centers are located, at the times they need it. Even if a power plant exists elsewhere, it cannot be used if the transmission grid is not connected. If servers are ready but waiting for grid interconnection, the invested equipment cannot generate revenue. The IEA also highlights the time lag between rapidly expanding data centers and slowly scaling power infrastructure.[^ieaai2026]

Nuclear power's advantage is its ability to deliver large-scale, low-carbon electricity over long periods. Once a plant is commissioned, it can underpin long-term power purchase agreements. However, outages for maintenance and fuel replacement do occur, and AI loads fluctuate instantaneously as well, so a single nuclear unit alone does not resolve all hours of power quality and reserve requirements.[^ieasupply]

Moreover, when a data center signs a new contract to buy electricity from an already-operating nuclear plant, the nation's total generation does not increase by that amount. The effect of changing who buys existing power must be distinguished from the effect of actually increasing supply through plant restarts, uprates, or new construction.

Actual development concepts also combine multiple generation sources. The Blue Energy project in Texas announced by GE Vernova in August 2026 envisions securing approximately 1GW of gas-fired generation first by 2030, then adding up to five BWRX-300 small modular reactors from 2032. Since the target for a final investment decision is 2027, this cannot yet be counted as operating capacity or confirmed total construction revenue.[^geblue]
This case shows two things. Nuclear and gas can complement each other within the timeline of a project. And the benefits of US nuclear expansion do not flow to Westinghouse alone.

The growth of AI power demand does not follow a straight line either. The energy required for the same task may fall, while model usage and more complex tasks may rise. Power generation investment must verify actual electricity demand — the sum of both factors — and customers' ability to pay.[^ieaai2026]

## 4. Nuclear economics depend on financing as much as technology

Nuclear is a business that spends heavily on construction upfront and recovers its investment by selling electricity over many years. Even with identical facilities, higher financing rates and longer construction schedules increase the capital cost that each unit of electricity must carry.

Government financial support, long-term power purchase agreements from creditworthy buyers, and schedule reductions from repeat construction can all change the economics of nuclear. However, transferring risk to the government does not make the risk disappear. Rather than lowering the developer's cost of capital, the burden of losses may shift to taxpayers or electricity consumers.

For Korea's investment in the United States to carry real economic meaning, it must go beyond simply providing cheap money. Korea must secure returns and business rights commensurate with the credit, currency, and construction risks it bears.

## 5. What is missed by dividing the global nuclear market into China and everything else

Looking at the recent history of new nuclear construction, China and Russia have accumulated far more repeat-build experience than the United States. According to the IEA, of the 52 reactors on which construction began worldwide between 2017 and 2024, 25 used Chinese designs and 23 used Russian designs.[^ieanuclear]

The market excluding China includes Russia. Therefore, non-Chinese nuclear demand cannot all be added up as potential orders for Korean and US firms. Buyers seeking to avoid Chinese and Russian technology, and buyers who may choose Russian nuclear plants based on financing terms, are different markets.

Russia's competitiveness lies not only in reactor design but in bundling construction, fuel, and financing together. Conversely, countries seeking to reduce dependence on Russia want an alternative package that includes fuel and maintenance as well. For Korea and the United States to compete, they must present not only reactor price but the reliability and financing terms of this entire bundle.[^rosatom][^osw]

### Who designs, who builds, and who sells the electricity

The designer develops the reactor's architecture and safety logic. Equipment manufacturers produce components to that design. Constructors install and integrate the work on site. The plant owner finances and sells the electricity, while the operator runs the facility safely. A single company may fill multiple roles, but the roles themselves must be distinguished.

China's CAP1000 has a lineage connected to the US AP1000 technology. The nationality of a technology and the nationality of the supply chain that actually executes the project do not always coincide. In the Bailong project introduced by CNNC, a SPIC-affiliated owner is confirmed to have placed contracts with the same group's SNERDI.[^china][^bailong]

France is pursuing the EPR2 program, and GE Vernova Hitachi is developing and supplying the BWRX-300. These alone are enough to show that the claim that Westinghouse monopolizes non-Chinese nuclear technology does not hold.[^edf][^geblue]

Korea's position is stronger than that of a simple subcontractor, but more limited than the description of an originating-technology country that independently dominates all markets. The source of Korea's negotiating leverage is that it is a supplier with both the capability to export Korean-designed reactors and the capability to manufacture and construct foreign designs. However, cost competitiveness at US sites and legal market access must be demonstrated separately.

## 6. What does a stake in Westinghouse actually buy?

Westinghouse is a US-based company, but its disclosed ownership structure is 49% Cameco and 51% an institutional investor consortium led by Brookfield Renewable. This is not a transaction in which the US government allocates shares in a 100%-state-owned enterprise to Korea.[^ownership]

This distinction matters in negotiations. The US government can exercise influence on the financial, policy, and licensing dimensions, but actually selling existing shares requires an agreement with the owners. The Korean investor has also not been finalized. Equating a nation securing strategic benefits with a specific Korean listed company directly receiving the equity upside from a Westinghouse stake is a mistake.

The US loan programs described earlier also require substantial equity commitments from Westinghouse. Therefore, if Korea subscribes for new shares and commits those funds to projects, the capital could accelerate plant execution. On the other hand, if Korea acquires only existing shares, the sale proceeds go in principle to the existing shareholders. A secondary-share transaction also has value if it secures strategic rights, but it should not itself be described as funding for new nuclear construction.

### Why Korea's participation could also benefit the seller

If Korea provides equipment, construction, and financing together, the project's feasibility may improve. If more nuclear plants actually proceed, Westinghouse gains greater opportunities to earn from design and technology services and long-term fuel and service contracts. Existing shareholders may choose to sell a portion of their stake, recover cash, and increase the value of what remains. This is an analysis of transaction incentives, not a confirmed purpose stated by the current seller.

The seller is not without alternatives either. On 31 July 2026, Cameco announced that Westinghouse had confidentially submitted a draft registration statement to US securities regulators for an initial public offering. The number of shares and the price have not been set. Even if Korea can provide strategic value, there is no guarantee it can buy the stake cheaply.[^ipo]

### The US government has also demanded a share of future gains in exchange for its support

The commitment disclosed by Cameco in October 2025 is structured to support a minimum of $80 billion in nuclear investment. This $80 billion is the scale of plant investment, not the equity value of Westinghouse.[^uswec]

The commitment also provides that if the government satisfies conditions including final investment decisions and definitive contracts, it will participate in Westinghouse cash distributions above a defined threshold and hold conditional rights at an IPO. This does not mean the US government already holds 20% of common equity.[^uswec]

The same question applies to Korea. If Korea provides capital and industrial capability that enhances the enterprise value for other shareholders, the contribution must be reflected in the share price and commercial contracts. Diplomatic achievement in the form of simply having secured a stake does not by itself validate the economics of the transaction.

<p>Under the October 2025 disclosed commitment, the participation right vests only if the US government concludes final investment decisions and definitive contracts for new nuclear construction totaling at least $80 billion. The vested participation right entitles the government to 20% of Westinghouse cash distributions exceeding $17.5 billion after vesting. Additional conditions — including vesting before January 2029 and an anticipated IPO valuation — govern the IPO demand and warrant conversion structure.</p>
<p>This differs from a simple 20% common equity stake. The $17.5 billion threshold here and the $17.5 billion supply chain loan announced by the Department of Energy in June 2026 are the same number but separate items. When evaluating actual dividend, dilution, and exit values for Korean investors, this seniority right and subsequent definitive contracts must be examined together in due diligence.</p>
</details>

## 7. The special significance of nuclear within the broader investment-in-America negotiation

The framework of the November 2025 US–Korea joint fact sheet was $350 billion in total: $150 billion in shipbuilding investment and a $200 billion strategic investment MOU. The strategic investment MOU explicitly stated an annual funding cap of $20 billion and an adjustment mechanism that accounts for foreign exchange market impact. It should not be read as a structure in which the full amount is wired in cash all at once.[^trade]

Within this larger negotiation, nuclear is a field in which Korea can offer negotiating assets beyond money. The United States needs a supply chain that can actually complete plants, and Korea wants access to long-term orders and technology and service revenue in exchange for providing capital. Because each side lacks something the other has, room for exchange exists.

From Korea's perspective, the economic significance breaks into four directions. Dividends and exit gains from equity, value-added from domestic production, supply chain maintenance through repeat overseas orders, and reduced friction costs around nuclear technology. All of these effects depend on contracts, and they overlap with each other, so they should not be simply added together.

For example, labor costs and local subcontractor revenues generated in the United States do not all become Korean GDP even if they are included in a contract value won by a Korean firm. Income from overseas equity and value-added from domestic factories are also separate items. When calculating a nation's economic gain, total contract value should be decomposed into domestic value-added retained in Korea, net return on overseas investment, and the costs of guarantees and financing.

Nuclear is not unconditionally superior to other investment candidates. If the stake is bought too expensively, domestic volume is limited by local procurement requirements, and Korea ends up absorbing construction losses, even an industrially significant project can become a poor financial investment.

## 8. What provisions matter more than stake percentage in the negotiation

A 15% stake does not automatically create a board seat. Voting rights at shareholders' meetings, board participation, consent rights over major decisions, and information access rights are distinct entitlements. What type of shares are purchased, and what is written into the articles of incorporation and shareholders' agreement, is what matters.
Even if Korea becomes a Westinghouse shareholder, existing intellectual property agreements do not change automatically. Westinghouse and KEPCO/KHNP announced a dispute settlement in January 2025, but the official announcement kept the detailed terms of the agreement confidential. I did not treat the royalty or territorial restriction details mentioned in press reports as verified contract text.[^ipsettlement]

The U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission design certification for the APR1400 already exists. However, design certification, construction and operating licenses for individual sites, and U.S. regulations on technology exports are separate matters. An equity transaction alone does not eliminate these procedures.[^apr][^part810]

The specific clauses needed for an investment decision are concrete. One must verify whether Korean companies are guaranteed a certain volume or bidding rights, whether the minimum procurement share is denominated by value or by number of components, and how delivery prices are set. How AP1000 and APR1400 will be jointly offered in which markets is also necessary.

Who bears responsibility when construction costs rise is equally important. If Korea provides completion guarantees without a liability cap, a low equity price or a large contract value may not compensate for that risk. Conversely, if the client bears the costs of design changes and regulatory delays, and Korea is responsible only for the manufacturing and construction scope it can control, the quality of the contract is different.

For equity investors, obligations for additional capital calls, anti-dilution protection, dividend priority, U.S. government pre-participation rights, and post-IPO lock-up restrictions also matter. Transactions where one buys in to gain business rights but ends up only providing liquidity to other shareholders while capital is tied up for a long period should be avoided.

## 10. Can the U.S.-Korea Alliance Lead Non-Chinese Nuclear Expansion

Building a strong supply-side coalition in some key markets is possible, but the assumption of dominating the entire non-Chinese market is excessive.

The path to success is relatively clear. One must create repeatable standard designs and contract structures within the United States, supply equipment with delivery and quality verified through Korean factories, and execute local construction consistently. If costs and schedules are validated on the first project, a virtuous cycle becomes possible in which financing and ordering for subsequent projects becomes easier. This path also aligns with the need for repeat construction and supply chain expansion that the IEA has identified.[^ieanuclear]

However, the United States also wants its own jobs and supply chain. Even if Korean factories initially supplement insufficient production capacity, demand for U.S. domestic production may intensify over the medium to long term. A path where Korean companies' overseas profits grow while their domestic export share declines is also possible. This is another reason to view national policy and the investment value of individual companies separately.

The French camp is a competitor, and Japanese and Canadian operators and equipment companies may be both competitors and collaborators. In markets where Russian and Chinese financial offers are available, price and financing terms will continue to matter. Therefore, when evaluating markets, one should start from global nuclear demand and then narrow down to actually accessible volume by working through each country's technology choices, political relationships, financing, and bidding structure.

Korea also has strategic choices. Expanding participation in the AP1000 supply chain may grow Korean companies' manufacturing and construction volume, but this does not always align with the direction of independent design and service profits from Korean-type reactors. A collaboration that maintains both the ability to sell Korean-type reactors and the ability to build other designs well is, in terms of long-term negotiating leverage, the better judgment.

### How Much Additional Profit in 2035 Would Justify a 10% Higher Value Today

Instead of arbitrarily presenting a price target, one can back-calculate the magnitude of additional profit that would justify further upside.

The assumptions are as follows. Existing business value remains unchanged, and the new nuclear business generates additional sustainable controlling shareholder net profit starting in 2035. A price-to-earnings ratio of 20x is applied to that profit, and it is discounted over 9 years at 10% per year. No dividends before 2035, and no additional share issuance or separate financing burden, are simplifications.

Working through the Doosan Enerbility calculation: 55.60 trillion won × 10% × 2.3579 ÷ 20 ≈ 0.656 trillion won. The 655.5 billion won is not a total net profit target for 2035, but rather the additional profit that must be layered on top of existing value in this simplified model.

This calculation does not automatically lead to the conclusion that Doosan is overvalued. The current share price also reflects other businesses and profits from earlier periods. Conversely, it shows that one cannot assume significant upside remains simply because a company is the most important player in the industry.

If the multiple applicable in 2035 is 30x, the required additional profit shrinks to two-thirds of the table figure. If profits materialize earlier or the cost of capital falls, the present value also grows. However, applying both a high multiple and a low discount rate optimistically at the same time amounts to underestimating risk twice.

In an actual valuation of Doosan, one must separate the energy business and subsidiary stakes, net debt, and non-controlling interests. For Hyundai E&C, profits and losses from non-nuclear construction businesses must be viewed together, and for KEPCO E&C, one must verify how many design personnel can be added to convert into revenue. A full fair-value assessment combining each company's same-date disclosed financials and consensus estimates was not included in this calculation.

Any remaining upside comes not from the 400 GW figure itself, but from contract terms better than the market expected, faster repeat orders, and higher cash profits. Based on current information alone, I do not judge that this is significantly unpriced across all nuclear stocks.

## 13. Who Are the Largest Listed Korean Beneficiary Companies

### Doosan Enerbility: The Manufacturing Company with the Broadest Potential Participation
Doosan Enerbility sits at the center of industrial beneficiaries. The company has manufacturing capability for major components of large nuclear reactors and a track record of overseas supply, with experience participating not only in Korean nuclear supply chains but also in the U.S. nuclear supply chain. The company has disclosed its record of supplying nuclear components for the U.S. Vogtle Units 3 and 4.[^doosanhistory][^doosanlarge]

This strength differs from a structure where a single reactor design must capture the entire market before any benefit is realized. In August 2026, the company also announced a contract to manufacture major structural components for TerraPower's first Natrium project. It can access manufacturing demand for both large conventional reactors and certain next-generation reactor designs.[^doosannatrium]

This does not mean Doosan is the only company in the world capable of manufacturing these components. Expanding production capacity requires capital investment, skilled labor, and quality control. Even as orders grow, working capital and capacity expansion costs may come first in the early stages. The metrics to watch are not only the growth in nuclear-related order backlog, but also the profit margins and operating cash flow that those orders generate.

Doosan also participates in business in China. In July 2026, the company announced a contract to supply large forgings for the Laiyang project in China. It is therefore inaccurate to characterize the company's growth solely as a beneficiary of China exclusion.[^doosanchina]

### Hyundai E&C: Large Construction Opportunity, Risk Allocation Equally Important

Hyundai E&C has a direct opportunity when the scale of nuclear construction business expands significantly. The engineering contract for Bulgaria's Kozloduy Units 7 and 8 with Westinghouse, and early design participation in U.S. projects, demonstrate a real path of cooperation. However, early design and engineering contracts should not be extrapolated into confirmed revenue from full EPC contracts.[^hyundai]

The question that will determine Hyundai E&C's upside potential goes beyond construction volume. It is necessary to verify whether the client compensates for cost overruns, who pays for design change costs, and whether there are caps on liquidated damages and warranty liability. If these terms are favorable, large orders can translate into profit; if unfavorable, the lessons of the 2017 Westinghouse case must be recalled.

### KEPCO E&C: Large Change from Small Earnings Base Is Possible, but Rights Are Needed

KEPCO E&C has high involvement in business tied to Korean nuclear design, so the change relative to existing earnings can be large when additional design volume converts into results. However, increased AP1000 construction does not mean Westinghouse's technology royalties accrue to KEPCO E&C.

Profitability differs depending on whether it is additional export of Korean-type reactors, partial design services for AP1000, or a scope of design that can be repeated over the long term. The company has a history of announcing participation in the U.S. nuclear design market, but past contracts do not guarantee participation rights across the entire current business.[^kecdesign]

It should also be noted that this company had the largest share price reaction among the three companies since early September. The back-calculated figure of KRW 66.8 billion in additional earnings by 2035 being smaller than Doosan's does not make it easier to achieve. It requires time and labor costs to increase skilled design personnel, as well as an actual scope of orders.

Evidence that would raise the assessment positively includes binding procurement volumes for Korean companies, repeat order schedules, construction contracts with limited risk, and the scope of cooperation between Korean-type reactors and AP1000. If actual production, profit margins, and cash recovery follow, the value of long-term growth is reinforced.

Conversely, the assessment should be lowered if only the equity acquisition amount is large while business rights remain vague, if there are many nuclear candidate sites but no power purchase agreements or final financing commitments, or if Korean companies guarantee against design changes and policy delays. The same applies if new equity contributions and capacity expansion burdens only grow while share prices rise ahead of orders.

The opportunity where U.S. nuclear reconstruction meets Korean manufacturing and construction capability is significant. The conditions for converting that opportunity into a good investment are clear. Korea must secure business rights and cash earnings in proportion to the capital and liability it contributes, and the present value of those additional earnings must exceed the expectations already priced into the shares. Judgment should be based on the rights and obligations in the contract documents and subsequent per-company order disclosures, not on announcement dates.
